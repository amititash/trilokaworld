# JODHPUR

## A DELIGHTFUL BLEND OF THE MODERN AND THE TRADITIONAL

Jodhpur, the second largest city in Rajasthan is popularly known as the Blue City. The name is clearly befitting as most of the architecture – forts, palaces, temples, havelis and even houses are built in vivid shades of blue. The strapping forts that tower this magnificent city sum up to a spectacle you would not want to miss. The mammoth, imposing fortress of Mehrangarh has a landscape dominating a rocky ridge with the eight gates leading out of the fortress. The new city is located outside the structure. Jodhpur is also known for the rare breed of horses known as Marwari or Malani, which are only found here.

Jodhpur marks its origin back to the year of 1459 AD. The history of this prosperous city revolves around the Rathore clan. Rao Jodha, the chief of Rathore Clan is credited with the origin of Jodhpur in India. The city is known to be built in place of the ancient capital, Mandore of the state of Manwar. Hence, the people of Jodhpur and surrounding areas are commonly known as Marwaris. Also, it is believed that the relics of Mandore can still be witnessed in the Mandore Gardens.

- ![MEHRANGARH FORT] 



#### MEHRANGARH FORT



Rising perpendicular and impregnable from a hill which is 125 metres above Jodhpur’s skyline is the Mehrangarh Fort. This historic fort is one of the most famous in India and is packed with history and legends. Mehrangarh Fort still bears the imprints of cannonball attacks courtesy the armies of Jaipur on its second gate. Chiselled and sturdy, the fort is known for its exquisite latticed windows, carved panels, intricately decorated windows and walls of Moti Mahal, Phool Mahal and Sheesh Mahal.

- ![UMAID BHAWAN PALACE] 



#### UMAID BHAWAN PALACE



Umaid Bhawan Palace was built by Maharaja Umaid Singh in 1929 to counter a famine which had hit the state at the time. It was also known as the Chittar Palace while being constructed thanks to the use of stones drawn from the Chittar hill. The palace was designed by HV Lanchester, a renowned British architect, and was completed in 16 years. Built with sandstone and marble, the architecture of the palace is described as a blend of lndo-Saracenic, Classical Revival and Western Art Deco styles. It is recognised as one of the largest private homes in the world and also one of the more spectacular buildings. It is the only palace built in the 20th century.

- ![MOTI MAHAL] 



#### MOTI MAHAL



Moti Mahal, as the name suggests, is the Pearl Hall where the royal families held their audience. The hall is known to have glass windows and five nooks that enabled the queens to listen to the proceedings taking place in the Sringar Chowki, The Royal Throne of Jodhpur.

- ![SHEESH MAHAL] 



#### SHEESH MAHAL



Situated within the compound of Mehrangarh Fort is the glass palace of Jodhpur, popularly known as Sheesh Mahal. This magnificent piece of architecture is adorned with walls of mirror work that stretch across ceilings and to the floors. It is superimposed by the mirror work of brightly painted religious figures cast in plaster.

- ![PHOOL MAHAL] 



#### PHOOL MAHAL



Going by the name, the Phool Mahal or Flower Hall is the most exorbitant of all the halls in the palace. This beautiful chamber is said to be the pleasure dome for the Maharajas. The gold used for constructing the Mahal came from Ahmedabad, Gujarat.

- ![CHAMUNDA MATAJI TEMPLE] 



#### CHAMUNDA MATAJI TEMPLE



Chamunda Mataji was Rao Jodha’s favourite goddess and so her idol was bought to the Mehrangarh Fort. Thus, the fort became a place of worship and was turned into a temple. Since then, locals have followed the culture of worshipping Chamunda Mata. In fact, till date, the goddess remains the Isht Devi (the adopted goddess) of Maharajas and the royal family.

- ![RANISAR PADAMSAR] 



#### RANISAR PADAMSAR



Located near the Fateh Pole in Mehrangarh, the Ranisar and Padmasar are adjacent lakes that were constructed in the year 1459. Ranisar Lake was built on orders of Queen Jasmade Hadi, Rao Jodha's wife while Padmasar Lake was ordered by Queen Padmini of Rao Ganga, daughter of Rana Sanga of Mewar.

- ![Sardar Government museum Jodhpur] 



#### Sardar Government museum Jodhpur



The government museum, located in Umaid Garden, houses
a rich collection of relics including armory, textiles, local art
and crafts, miniature paintings, portraits of the then rulers of
Marwar Princely State. manuscripts and statues of the Jain
Tirthankaras.

- ![JASWANT THADA] 



#### JASWANT THADA



This milky white memorial built towards the end of the 19th century as a tribute to the leader Jaswant Singh is a huge tourist attraction. Jaswant Singh, who ruled Jodhpur, invested well in his state. He made attempts to bring down the level of crime, subdue dacoits, built railways and broadly worked on raising the economy of Marwar.
Jaswant Thada is managed and looked after by the Mehrangarh Museum Trust (MMT) and is open to public. The Trust is operating a Museum in Jaswant Thada displaying portraits of Marwar rulers along with informative didactics – the information serves as orientation space to understand the history of Marwar through the Portraits. Its grounds serve as a serene venue for morning concerts during music festivals such as the Rajasthan International Folk Festival and the World Sacred Spirit Festival.

- ![GHANTA GHAR] 



#### GHANTA GHAR



Ghanta Ghar, also known as the clock tower, is situated in
one of the busiest areas of Jodhpur. The tower was built by
Maharaja Sardar singh from whom the adjacent Sardar
market takes its name. The Sadar Market is quite popular
among tourists for spices, traditional clothes and fancy
jewelry . Clock Tower is a well- known land mark in
Jodhpur . The Tower serves as the Start/ end point of
heritage walk through the blue city.

- ![MAHAMANDIR TEMPLE] 



#### MAHAMANDIR TEMPLE



Mahamandir, meaning great temple, is a sanctified spot where tranquillity reigns supreme. Situated on Mandore road, the temple is an architectural wonder. It is supported by 84 pillars and ornamented with detailed designs and figures depicting various postures of Yoga.

- ![MANDALESHWAR MAHADEV] 



#### MANDALESHWAR MAHADEV



The Mandaleshwar Mahadev was built by Mandal Nath in AD 923. It is believed to be one of the oldest shrines in the city. The walls of the temple have some beautiful paintings of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati.

- ![SARDAR SAMAND LAKE AND PALACE] 



#### SARDAR SAMAND LAKE AND PALACE



Built on the banks of the Sardar Samand Lake by Maharaja Umaid Singh in 1933, the Sardar Samand Lake Palace is a spectacular hunting lodge. It remains the royal family's favourite retreat and houses a vast collection of African trophies and original watercolour paintings. The lake attracts several migratory and local birds such as the yellow-legged green pigeon, Himalayan griffon and Dalmatian pelican, making it a bird watcher's paradise.

- ![MASURIA HILLS] 



#### MASURIA HILLS



Masuria garden is one of the three most beautiful and
famous gardens of Jodhpur. Located on top of the Masuria
hill in the middle of Jodhpur city, it is popular among local

deity, Baba Ramdev. The site offers a stunning panoramic

#### SHASTRI CIRCLE



Shastri Circle is a traffic roundabout in the middle of Jodhpur City. While it has a job to do during the day, it is most spectacular at night, when it comes to life with lights and fountains. This draws locals as well as tourists to the spot.

- ![MANDORE] 



#### MANDORE



Towards the north of Jodhpur city is the ancient capital of
Marwar, Mandore. This area is of major historical
importance and you will find the dewals or cenotaphs of
Jodhpur’s former rulers. Unlike the original umbrella-shaped
cenotaphs that are typical patterns of Rajasthan
architecture, these are built along the lines of Hindu
temples. This enclosure also houses a museum ,a temple ,an
art gallery. A light and sound show operated in this garden
during the evening which introduces the spectators to the
rich cultural history of bygone era of princely empire of
Marwar.

Situated on Jaisalmer road, this small artificial lake is an ideal
picnic spot. It is like a canvas with a splash of romantic

This park is situated on the way to Jaisalmer, about 1

- ![BALSAMAND LAKE] 



#### BALSAMAND LAKE



Balsamand Lake is about 5 kilometres from Jodhpur on the Jodhpur-Mandore Road. Built in 1159 AD, it was planned as a water reservoir to cater to Mandore. The Balsamand Lake Palace was built on its shore later as a summer palace. It is surrounded by lush green gardens that house groves of trees such as mango, papaya, pomegranate, guava and plum. Animals and birds like the jackal and peacock also call this place home. This lake is now a popular picnic spot with tourists and locals.

- ![GUDA VILLAGE] 



#### GUDA VILLAGE



Guda, a Bishnoi village, is home to a vivid range of exotic wildlife and nature. It is a habitat for thousands of migratory birds in the area. One can often catch the Demoiselle crane frolicking at the lake. Antelopes and black bucks can also be spotted by the pond. This place is a must-visit for nature lovers.

- ![Mehrangarh Fort and Museum] 



#### Mehrangarh Fort and Museum



Mehrangarh, the fort of Jodhpur, crowns a rocky hill that rises 400 feet
above the surrounding plain and appears both to command and to meld
with the landscape. One of the largest forts in Rajasthan, it contains fine
palaces and preserves in its museum many priceless relics of Indian
courtly life.
Jodhpur is named after its founder Rao Jodha, a fifteenth-century chief of
the Rathore clan. In 1459, Rao Jodha (r. 1438-89) began to build a new
fort six miles to the south of Mandore, his then capital. A strategic
location was chosen for the new fort: an isolated rock providing high
elevation and good natural defenses. The fort was named Mehrangarh,
meaning ‘fort of the sun’ – a reference to the clan’s mythical descent from
the Sun god ‘Surya’. Over five hundred yards long, the fort wall is seventy
feet wide and rises in places to a height of one hundred and twenty feet.
Today Mehrangarh Museum has a unique importance as a repository of
the artistic and cultural history of the large areas of Central Rajasthan
and Marwar-Jodhpur. The museum boasts exemplary examples of 17th,
18th and 19th-century collections for the fields of Miniature Paintings,
Arms and Armours, Textiles, Decorative Arts and Furniture.
The Museum has also participated in many international exhibitions all
over the world, displaying and sharing the rich heritage of Marwar, and
interacting with prestigious institutions in the field.

#### Chokhelao Bagh and Interpretation Center



Visit the Chokhelao Bagh which sits at the foot of the Mehrangarh Fort.
This over two hundred-year-old garden, complete with the scents,
sounds, and textures of a garden of the eighteenth century Marwar has
been turned into a Botanical Museum by the Mehrangarh Museum Trust
by planting and in-situ displaying, exquisite endemically historic flora of
Marwar region.
The garden today, as in the past, is truly a celebration of nature as it
captures the changing colors of the seasons in the upper terrace of flower
beds. It is equally magical for night viewing when the Mehtab Bagh or
moonlight garden laid out in the lower terrace comes alive with the white
flowers ofchandni(Tabernaemontana coronaria) and the sweet-
smellingkamini(Maurya exotia). Visit this gem of a garden and carry
back the sensuous experience of eighteenth-century Rajput garden.

#### MARWAR FESTIVAL



The Marwar festival is one the most famous festivals of Jodhpur and India. The two-day festival is held every year in the month of Ashwin (between September and October) and for one night in Osian Town in the Thar Desert, in memory of the heroes of Rajasthan. It was originally known as the Maand Festival. The Marwar festival is a centre of authentic folk music, culture and lifestyle of Rajasthan’s rulers. The royal collection of art forms in this festival revisit the legends and stories written and sung to honour the former rulers of Marwar. Additionally, other attractions comprise of camel tattoo show and polo. The festival is held at famous venues like the Umaid Bhavan Palace, Mandore and Mehrangarh Fort.

Rajasthan's captivating "Blue City," Jodhpur, is a historic stronghold whose origins trace back to 1459. Founded by Rao Jodha, a Rajput chief of the Rathore clan, it quickly rose to prominence as the capital of the powerful kingdom of Marwar. Situated strategically on vital trade routes, Jodhpur flourished as a center for commerce and culture. The Rathore rulers, renowned for their valour and patronage of the arts, shaped Jodhpur into the majestic city it is today. The imposing Mehrangarh Fort, a testament to their military prowess and architectural grandeur, dominates the cityscape. Palatial wonders like Umaid Bhawan Palace and Jaswant Thada offer glimpses into the royal legacy. Vibrant bazaars, filled with the scent of spices and the sight of intricately crafted handicrafts, showcase the city's rich artisanal traditions, while folk music and dance echo through the narrow lanes. Let us have a look into what are the best things to do and places to visit in Jodhpur. The best places to visit in Jodhpur Mehrangarh Fort Jeremy Woodhouse / Getty Images Mehrangarh Fort, a formidable citadel etched into Jodhpur's skyline, is a testament to Rajput valour. The city's founder, Rao Jodha, commenced its construction in 1459, and subsequent rulers added to its grandeur. This imposing structure, perched on a 400-foot-high hill, boasts thick, impregnable walls and seven gates, each a chronicle of battles won in the medieval ages. Within its ramparts, intricate palaces like Moti Mahal (Pearl Palace) and Phool Mahal (Flower Palace) showcase Rajput artistry. The Fort Museum houses a rich collection of royal artifacts, including palanquins, weaponry, and miniature paintings, offering a glimpse into Marwar's regal past. The fort's ramparts provide panoramic views of the "Blue City," making it a must-visit for history enthusiasts and architecture aficionados. Timings: 9am-5pm, open all days Tickets: Rs 100 for Indians and Rs 600 for foreigners Umaid Bhawan Palace Elena-studio / Getty Images Umaid Bhawan Palace, a magnificent sandstone edifice, stands as one of the world's largest private residences with 347 rooms showcasing Jodhpur's royal grandeur. Commissioned by Maharaja Umaid Singh in 1929, it took 16 years to complete this magnificent palace. Designed by British architect Henry Vaughan Lanchester, the palace blends Indo-Saracenic, Classical Revival, and Art Deco styles, creating a unique architectural marvel. Its distinctive use of "chittar" sandstone gives it a warm, golden hue. A portion of the palace remains the residence of the Jodhpur royal family, while another section is a luxurious Taj Palace Hotel. Visitors must explore the museum within the palace, showcasing the royal family's history, vintage cars, and exquisite artifacts. The stunning Durbar Hall, with its murals and Belgian crystal chandeliers, is a highlight. The lush gardens surrounding the palace offer a serene escape from the arid and rocky landscape. A stroll through the palace grounds reveals the intricate details of its architecture. Spend a night at the Palace Hotel to experience royal hospitality firsthand. Timings: 10am-4:30pm, open all days Tickets: Rs 30 for Indians and Rs 100 for foreigners Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park Mehrangarh Museum Trust Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park, nestled at the foot of Mehrangarh Fort, is a remarkable bio-diversity park showcasing the region's native desert flora. Created in 2006, it transformed a barren, rocky wasteland into a thriving ecological haven spread across 72 hectares. The park aimed to restore the natural ecology of a large, rocky area adjoining the fort. You can explore trails winding through carefully curated sections around Devkund Lake, witnessing diverse desert plant species, many indigenous to the region. The park's focus on native plants allows visitors to learn about the unique adaptations of desert flora. Birding enthusiasts might be able to spot Shrikes, Partridges, Black-shouldered kites, and Rock Pigeons around the lake. A key highlight is the Visitor Centre where information about the geological history of the area and the ecological restoration work is displayed. The park also provides stunning views of Mehrangarh Fort. Timings: 7am-6:30pm (Apr-Sep), 8am-5:30pm (Oct-Mar), open all days Tickets: Rs 100 for Adults, Rs 200 for guided walks Jaswant Thada Shweta Dravid Jaswant Thada, a pristine white marble memorial, stands as an oasis amidst Jodhpur's rugged landscape. Maharaja Sardar Singh built it in 1899 in memory of his father, Maharaja Jaswant Singh II. Today it serves as a royal crematorium. The intricately carved marble structure, often referred to as the " Taj Mahal of Marwar," features delicate latticework and portraits of Rathore rulers. Take a leisurely stroll across the main cenotaph and the surrounding smaller memorials dedicated to other royal family members to admire the exquisite craftsmanship. The tranquil lake and meticulously maintained gardens enhance the site's peaceful aura. The panoramic views of Mehrangarh Fort and the city from Jaswant Thada are particularly captivating, making it a must-visit for those seeking a blend of history and architectural beauty. Timings: 9am-5pm, open all days Tickets: Rs 30 for Indians and Rs 50 for foreigners Mandore Gardens Jui-Chi Chan / Getty Images Long before the blue hues of Jodhpur painted the landscape, Mandore (about 9 km from Jodhpur) reigned as the Rathore dynasty's capital. Even after the capital moved to Jodhpur, Mandore remained the royal cremation grounds until Jaswant Thada was built. Today, it is famous for the beautiful Mandore Gardens. Visitors come here to see the weathered ruins of Mandore Fort, where you can almost hear the whispers of a bygone era and imagine the royal city that once stood. The Hall of Heroes is a must-visit, where majestic, rock-cut figures of brave Rajput heroes stand tall. Stop by to see the Temple of 300 million Gods, a dazzling display of life-sized, vividly painted deities. The only Ravana Temple in Rajasthan is also located here. It is said that Ravana's wife, Mandodari, belonged to Mandore. History buffs can visit the Government Museum, which houses regional artifacts and old relics. For a truly unforgettable experience, let the sunset at Mandore be your grand finale; blending fiery skies, lush gardens, and historic stones creating an ambiance that transcends time. Timings: 8am-8pm, open all days Tickets: Rs 50 for the museum Jodhpur Stepwell (Toorji-Ka-Jhalara) searagen / Getty Images Built in the 1740s by Queen Tawarji, Maharaja Abhay Singh's consort, Toorji-Ka-Jhalara, served as a vital water source for the community. Besides the practical purpose, it was a place to showcase that period's cultural and architectural grandeur. Descend into its depths, and you'll see a labyrinth of intricately carved steps, each a masterpiece of geometric precision. The delicate friezes depict deities and mythological creatures, which have been seamlessly integrated into the structural design. The local rose-red sandstone provides a warm, textured canvas for the architectural details. Today, the stepwell has become a vibrant place with hip cafes, shops, and hotels, giving you a chance to walk through a piece of Jodhpur's history while also enjoying the contemporary buzz of the city. Timings: Open 24 hours on all days; free Masuriya Hill Garden Masuriya Hill Garden, perched atop Masuriya Hill, about 7 km from the airport, offers a panoramic escape from Jodhpur's bustling streets. Originally a communication outpost, its elevated position now provides breathtaking views of the "Blue City" and Mehrangarh Fort. The garden's most prominent feature is the massive statue of Veer Durgadas Rathore, a revered Marwar warrior, standing tall as a symbol of Rajput valour. You can explore the manicured gardens and enjoy leisurely walks. The viewpoint offers unparalleled photo opportunities, especially during sunset when the city is bathed in golden light. Beyond its sweeping city views, Masuriya Hill Garden is a hidden haven for bird enthusiasts, with over 150 bird species making it their home. You can take a guided birdwatching tour to identify these avian beauties. Timings: 7am-9pm, open all days Tickets: Rs 30 for Indians, Rs 100 for foreigners Iconic Temples Jodhpur's allure extends beyond its majestic monuments; its temples are silent witnesses to the city's profound spiritual heritage. Among them, the Chamunda Mataji Temple and the Achal Nath Mahadev Temple stand out as iconic landmarks. Nestled within the imposing walls of Mehrangarh Fort, the Chamunda Devi Temple stands as a powerful symbol of Jodhpur's spiritual heart. This sacred site, dedicated to Chamunda Mataji, a fierce form of Durga, holds the distinction of being the city's guardian deity. Rao Jodha, the founder of Jodhpur, installed the idol in 1460, moving it from the old capital of Mandore. Devotees flock to this temple to seek blessings and protection, drawn by its spiritual aura and historical significance. During Navratri, the temple transforms into a vibrant spectacle of devotion, filled with the sounds of chanting and the glow of countless lamps. The elevated position of the temple offers breathtaking panoramic views of Jodhpur's blue cityscape, providing a unique vantage point that blends spiritual experience with visual splendour. The Achal Nath Mahadev Temple, nestled within the heart of Jodhpur's old city, is another iconic temple. Dedicated to Lord Shiva, the temple was built in 1531 by Nanak Devi, queen of Rao Ganga, the famous King of Marwar between 1515 and 1531. It's a place where centuries of devotion and architectural artistry converge. The temple embodies traditional Hindu architecture with its shikhara, carved mandapa, and sanctum housing the Shiva lingam. Local sandstone construction and weathered details reflect centuries of devotion. Raj Ranchhodji Temple is another revered temple in the Blue City, dedicated to Lord Krishna. Built in 1905, it was named Ranchod after Lord Krishna (a loving name for Krishna) and is one of the most visited Krishna temples in India. Lakes around Jodhpur Jodhana Heritage Resorts If you want to escape the city's bustle, a trip to Balsamand Lake and Kaylana Lake will reinvigorate you. Located about 5 km from the city, Balsamand Lake is enveloped by lush gardens, creating a picturesque setting. This artificial lake was built in the 13th century by Balak Rao Parihar, a Gurjara-Pratihara ruler. Maharaja Jaswant Singh built a garden around it in the 19th century. Initially, the lake was made as a water reservoir for neighbouring villages but over time it became a popular place for people to relax and unwind. Visitors can stroll along the lake's banks, enjoying the cool breeze and the sight of the elegant Balsamand Lake Palace perched on its shore. The surrounding gardens, filled with fruit trees and flowering plants, attract various bird species, making it a pleasant spot for casual birdwatching. It also has a small zoo with peacocks, crocodiles, and rabbits, amongst other animals. Timings: 8am-5:30pm, open all days Kaylana Lake, a rugged jewel near Jodhpur, offers a wilder, more expansive experience. Constructed in 1872 by Pratap Singh, the then-ruler of Jodhpur, the lake is an important water source for locals. It had palaces and gardens in ancient times, but you can only see ruins today. Unlike other lakes, the vast waters and rocky shores of Kaylana Lake provide a sense of untamed beauty. You can enjoy boating, picnics and capture stunning photographs of the lake. If you are adventurous, hike up to the Shiv Mandir, and for those interested in birding, the lake will become the home of migratory birds in the winter. Timings: Daily; all day Bazaars of Jodhpur Instants / Getty Images Dive into the vibrant chaos of Jodhpur's bazaars, where the air hums with the scent of spices, colourful fabrics, and the glint of silver. More than a marketplace, these bazaars explore the Rajasthani artistic expression. Head to Sardar Market, nestled beneath the imposing Mehrangarh Fort, where the air is thick with the aroma of spices and the vibrant hues of tie-dye textiles. Its iconic clock tower guides you through a maze of stalls brimming with leather goods, silver jewellery, and traditional attire. Banjara Jewellers near the clock tower is best for silver trinkets. For a taste of royal craftsmanship, wander through Tripolia Market, renowned for its exquisite lacquer work. Discover beautifully painted wooden artifacts, brass items, and marble statues. For traditional Bandhani or Lehriya (tie-dye) fabric or readymade garments, Sonu Boutique and Bani Thani Collection will leave you spoilt for choice. And don't miss Mochi Bazaar, the epicenter of Jodhpur's leather footwear. It's a haven for juttis ( Embroidered Indian closed shoes) of every style imaginable, from simple everyday wear to elaborately embroidered ceremonial shoes. In all these markets, you must haggle for the best deals. Must-try eateries Beyond the vibrant markets and historical monuments, Jodhpur is a melting pot of flavours where age-old recipes meet modern palates. For an authentic Rajasthani feast, Shandar Sweet Home offers a traditional thali, a vibrant platter showcasing the region's diverse flavours. Quench your thirst at the legendary Mishrilal Lassi Wala, a 90-year-old institution where creamy lassis and savoury snacks have delighted generations. Street food aficionados will relish Shahi Samosa's oversized samosas, while Shri Arora Namkeen and Pokar Sweets satisfy cravings for snacks and traditional sweets. Mohanji Mithaiwala, another local favourite, offers a delectable array of traditional mithai (sweets). For a refined dining experience, Baradari at RAAS Hotels presents contemporary Rajasthani cuisine with a modern twist, while Balsamand Lake Palace offers a regal dining experience, where royal recipes are served in a luxurious setting. Clock Tower Paul Panayiotou / Getty Images Jodhpur's iconic Clock Tower, Ghanta Ghar, stands as a historic landmark and bustling hub in the heart of the old city. Constructed by Maharaja Sardar Singh during his reign between 1880 and 1911, it served as a central point for timekeeping and a symbol of civic pride. Its unique location within the vibrant Sardar Market makes it a tourist must-visit. The tower's architectural style, blending traditional Rajasthani elements with Victorian influences, offers a visual treat, drawing photographers like a magnet. You can couple it with your trip to Sardar Market, experiencing the lively atmosphere of the market, exploring stalls filled with textiles, spices, and handicrafts, all while the Clock Tower keeps time. Timings: 10am-6pm, daily Bishnoi Village David Clapp / Getty Images Just a short hop from Jodhpur's bustling heart, about 22 km, lies a world apart: Bishnoi Village. Time seems to slow here, and nature reigns supreme, thanks to the Bishnoi community's unwavering commitment to environmental preservation. You can delve into the art of traditional block printing, learning the intricate techniques used to create their vibrant textiles, or join a pottery session, moulding clay into rustic vessels, and discover the secrets of their age-old crafts. Stroll through the village, exploring the mud-walled homes, admiring the architectural ingenuity and artistic expression. Savour a traditional Bishnoi meal of Ker Sangri (berries and beans), Bajra roti (pearl millet bread), Dal (lentil gravy), and Buttermilk (yogurt-based drink). For a truly immersive experience, consider a guided tour that will help you understand the community's history and conservation efforts. Osian Melvyn Longhurst / Getty Images Located about 70 km from Jodhpur, Osian makes for a great overnight or full-day getaway to experience the heart of the Thar Desert. Take a camel ride across the undulating sands, with sunsets transforming the landscape into a spectacle of vibrant colours. Experience a jeep safari to the region's hidden villages and hamlets. On the drive, you may spot desert foxes, gazelles, and various bird species. Beyond the adventure, Osian, an ancient temple town, reveals its rich history through the 8th century Sachiya Mata (dedicated to mother Goddess) and the Jain Mahavira temple dated 783 AD, the oldest Jain temple in India. If you spend the night in Osian, you will get a chance to relish some finger-licking Rajasthani cuisine and watch the traditional Kalbelia folk dance done by the nomadic tribe of snake charmers of the Kalbelia community. Must-do experiences Jodhpur Polo &amp; Equestrian Institute Jodhpur isn't just a city of forts and palaces; it's a stage where ancient rhythms meet modern beats, and the thunder of hooves echoes alongside soulful melodies. The city comes alive with these events, spread across the entire year. If you are in Jodhpur during the polo season, you must experience the royal sport of polo, a thrilling display of horsemanship and strategy. The Jodhpur Polo &amp; Equestrian Club Institution, a bastion of equestrian tradition, hosts thrilling polo matches. With Maharaja Gaj Singh II of Jodhpur as its esteemed patron, the club upholds a legacy deeply rooted in the city's history. The highlight of the club's calendar is the annual Jodhpur Polo Season, typically held in December. Jodhpur RIFF/OIJO Jodhpur resonates with the sounds of world-class music festivals with the winters coming in. RIFF Jodhpur, held in October, celebrates Rajasthani folk and global music, creating a mesmerizing fusion of sounds. In December, the Magnetic Fields Music Festival transforms the historic palace of Alsisar (about 6 hours from Jodhpur) into a contemporary music haven, blending electronic and indie genres. February brings the World Sacred Spirit Festival, a soulful gathering of spiritual and devotional music from around the globe. More than just performances, these festivals are celebrations that weave together Jodhpur's cultural spirit with the shared passion of music lovers worldwide. When to visit

The best time to visit Jodhpur is between October and March, when the weather is at its most pleasant, though cooler winter days tend to attract bigger crowds. October is a good time to come to the city if you're a fan of music, as it coincides with the annual Rajasthan International Folk Festival, known locally as Jodhpur RIFF.

Getting around

The best way to get around Jodhpur is by taxi or auto-rickshaw, and auto-rickshaws are particularly convenient for navigating small lanes or to hail a ride quickly. Taxis can be called using ride-sharing apps, but if you plan to visit many different spots, it’s sometimes more efficient to hire a car with a driver for the day.

Traveler tips

For meals with a view, head over to the Stepwell Café, below Mehrangarh Fort. The café gets its name because it looks out over the Toorji Ka Jhalra Bavdi, a beautifully restored stepwell that was once used to harvest and collect water for year-round use. While it’s a great spot to take photos, the food in itself is enough to merit a visit, but you have choices—the menu features everything from filling calzones to fragrant biryani.

People Also Ask**Q:** What are the famous attractions in Jodhpur?

**A:**

Jodhpur's most famous attraction is the 15th-century Mehrangarh Fort, which towers over the city and can be seen for miles. Other popular attractions include the Umaid Bhawan Palace, which houses a luxury hotel and a museum, and the Jaswant Thada, a white marble memorial and royal cremation ground.

**Q:** Are two days enough for Jodhpur?

**A:**

Yes, two days are enough for Jodhpur. You'll have time to visit the main attractions, including the Mehrangarh Fort and Umaid Bhawan Palace and still be able to spend your second day shopping, wandering the blue lanes of the old city, or taking a day trip into the Thar Desert.

**Q:** Which is better, Udaipur or Jodhpur?

**A:**

Neither city is better than the other, but they are different. Jodhpur is better if you like forts, and it’s better for antique shopping. Udaipur is considered more romantic, with its whitewashed, lake-facing palaces, but you’ll need to take a trip to nearby Kumbhalgarh to see a proper fortress.

**Q:** What is unique about Jodhpur?

**A:**

Jodhpur's blue-painted old town makes it unique. It isn’t something you’ll see elsewhere in India. While it’s common to paint houses in colorful hues in Rajasthan—as you’ll learn if you visit the Pink City part of Jaipur—uniform blueness is a distinctly Jodhpuri trait.

**Q:** Why is everything blue in Jodhpur?

**A:**

Not everything is blue in Jodhpur, but the color dominates the old city buildings near Mehrangarh Fort, and it is eye-catching. There are many theories as to why they're painted blue, including that chemicals in the paint help to repel termites and that the color aids in cooling homes.

**Q:** What language is spoken in Jodhpur?

**A:**

The most prominent languages and dialects spoken in Jodhpur include Marwari, Rajasthani, Hindi, and English. Virtually everyone you interact with will be able to understand Hindi, and many people, especially those working in the tourism trade, can communicate in English with reasonable fluency.

# Top 5 Places to Visit in Jodhpur

![Mehrangarh Fort sitting above Jodhpur.] 

Mehrangarh Fort sitting above Jodhpur.

# 1. [Mehrangarh Fort] 

Sitting high above the city of Jodhpur is Mehrangarh Fort. It was built in 1460 by Rao Jodha, only one year after he founded the city of Jodhpur. The fort is one of the largest in all of India and is definitely worth a visit. Don’t miss the Chamunda Mataji Hindu Temple at the very edge of the fort. It has incredible views of the blue city below.

I recommend planning your visit to the fort early in the morning to avoid the heat and the crowds. We arrived at 9 AM and spent about two hours exploring. By the time we left, the fort was packed with other tourists.

The fort is free to explore, but entrance into the museum is 600 rupees.

# 2. [Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park] 

Only a short walk or rickshaw ride from the Mehrangarh Fort is Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park. This park was only opened in 2006, but holds volcanic rock that is estimated to be about 750 years old. When you enter the park, you feel like you’ve just escaped into a desert oasis miles from the hustle and bustle of the city. There are a number of trails to explore but I recommend the Green & Yellow Trail. It leads to the wall and has gorgeous views of the fort and blue city in the distance.

The cost to enter the Desert Park is 100 rupees.

![This color and those tiles makes for a gorgeous home.] 

This color and those tiles makes for a gorgeous home.

# 3. [Blue City Walk] 

Many blogs will recommend exploring the blue city with a tour guide, but I decided to research tour routes and explore the city on my own terms. I was not disappointed with this decision because I was able to take my time walking up and down the streets, turning whichever direction felt right and knowing I’d find my way to my end point eventually.

Start your blue city walk at the [Chandpole Gate] which leads into the blue city and head toward the direction of the [Clock Tower], which will be your ending point. Walk down the narrow streets until you either reach a dead end or find another street that leads deeper into the sea of blue. I first walked to the left, hit a dead end, backed up and went the other way. If you look on the map of Jodhpur below, you’ll notice that a lot of the streets around the Chandpole Gate are dead ends. What you can’t see from the map, however, is that some of these dead ends lead to some of the most magnificent blue houses, or if you’re lucky like I was, a steep ledge that provides a panoramic view of the entire city.

While you’re exploring, you will most likely run into locals, not only people, but cows and dogs. Here are some tips on what to expect.

The children you meet will ask you to take a photo with them...with your own camera. While they will never see these photos, they will still follow you around and ask to take a picture with them. I recommend taking them because they seemed to really enjoy it, but be clear that you’ll only take one photo. Otherwise, you could spend a long time in an unplanned photo shoot.

![They asked for one picture and their mom said it was okay.] 

They asked for one picture and their mom said it was okay.

The locals are very friendly. Some will stare at you blankly, some will wave and smile. Some may even offer to have you come into their homes or on their rooftops for a better view. Nine out of 10 times though, they will ask you for money if you do this. I fell for this during my walk because an elderly woman on a rooftop offered to have me come up. It was a great rooftop, so I said yes. Before I climbed down, she demanded money and I gave it to her. Honestly, it was worth the view, but be prepared for this.

![This was the view from the elderly woman's house.] 

This was the view from the elderly woman's house.

From my experience, cows and dogs wander the streets throughout all of India, and Jodhpur is no different. Both won’t bother you if you don’t bother them. I recommend avoiding feeding or petting the dogs, and creating a bit of distance between you and the many cows you will pass.

Getting up close and personal with these cows.

You’ll eventually make your way to the Clock Tower and this is where you’re walking tour ends. If you worked up an appetite like I did, I recommend dining at the Indique Restaurant. It’s on the rooftop of a beautiful hotel and the food was delicious.

# 4. [Sardar Market] 

Sardar Market is a vibrant shopping area spanning the base of the clock tower and the narrow streets surrounding it. Local vendors sell textiles, leather shoes, jewelry, spices and an array of other souvenirs. Sardar is said to have a total of over 6,000 tiny shops and is one of the oldest markets in Jodhpur.

Even if you don’t plan to make any purchases, this market provides the sights, smells and chaos of a truly local experience that you don’t want to miss. I found this [Sardar Market] guide very helpful and recommend reading it before you go.

![This colorful home was one of my favorites on the walk.]

# 5. [Janta Sweet Home] 

Janta Sweet Home has the most incredible Indian desserts including my personal favorite, laddu (sometimes spelled laddoo). These little balls of joy are made with flour, ghee/butter, sugar, nuts and dried raisins. They’re sweet yet savory at the same time and go perfectly with a cup of chai masala. In addition to sweets, Janta also has a restaurant where you can eat a full meal.

Janta Sweet Home is just a few blocks away from Sardar Market so make a pit stop here after you spend time shopping.

# Why is Jodhpur blue?

I can't confirm any of the claims below, but I've done a bit of research on why this city is blue and thought it would be fun to share some of the theories out there. Regardless of why the houses are painted blue, I sure appreciate the beauty of them.

> Theory 1: Jodhpur was the home to many Brahmins, which is theoretically the highest varna (class) in the Hindu caste system. Some say that the Brahmins painted their homes blue to differentiate their status in the city.

> Theory 2: Locals painted their homes blue because they believed it reflected the heat and kept their home cooler.

> Theory 3: Lord Shiva, one of the Hindu deities is often associated with the color blue and is shown with blue skin in many visual depictions. Some say that many citizens of Jodhpur were followers of Lord Shiva and therefore honored him by painting their homes blue.

> Theory 4: Termites. The least exciting theory, in my opinion, is that the color blue keeps the termites away.

Do you believe any of these theories? Share any other theories you've heard in the comments below!

## Top Places to Visit in Jodhpur

With marvellous architecture, royal hospitality, and natural splendour, Jodhpur has a lot to offer. Here are the top five places to visit in Jodhpur:

### Mehrangarh Fort

[Mehrangarh Fort] is one of the major Jodhpur attractions for tourists. This magnificent and formidable fort stands four hundred feet above the Jodhpur skyline. Of all the tourist sites in Jodhpur, this one has the most spectacular views.

### Mandore Garden

[Mandore Gardens] ' royal, natural splendour justifies its status as one of Jodhpur famous places. This garden houses the royal chhatris of the royal clan of Rathore. An amalgam of meticulously tended gardens and red sandstone structures makes it one of the most stunning tourist sites in Jodhpur.

### Jaswant Thada

Jodhpur sightseeing is incomplete without a visit to one of the iconic tourist spots in Jodhpur, [Jaswant Thada]. This historical architecture in marble is often termed the "Taj Mahal of Marwar". Inside the building, there are many portraits of Rajput rulers and other paintings of interest. The outer architecture resembles that of a Hindu temple.

### Clock Tower Market

The [clock tower market] is one of the sought-after tourist places in Jodhpur because of the sheer variety of traditional artefacts, Rajasthani textiles, and the colourful vibrancy of the place - all that at a reasonable price.

### Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park

Located next to Mehrangarh Fort, the [Rao Jodha desert rock park] contains volcanic rock and sandstone formations. The rock park along with Mehrangarh Fort could together be Jodhpur places to visit in one day.

## Best Time to Visit Jodhpur

### Summer

The [places to visit in summers] in Jodhpur are mostly outdoors and Jodhpur records very high temperatures, as high as 42°C, during the months between May to June.

### Monsoon

Jodhpur doesn't receive very heavy rainfall as most of India does. During the period of July to September the mercury dips down but the humidity in the air rises.

### Winter

Winter, which lasts from November to March, is the peak season for Jodhpur tourism. The temperature hovers anywhere between 10°C to 24°C.

## Places to Visit in Jodhpur with Family and Friends

### Panchkund Chattriya

The lesser-known Panchkund Chattriya which is about 40 minutes away from Jodhpur is slowly climbing the ladder of the list of things to see in Jodhpur. It has as many as 46 chhatris.

### Bishnoi Village

A safari is organised here to showcase the rich heritage and lifestyle of tribal Rajasthan. Fringed by khejri trees, beautified by lakes, and containing abundant wildlife, the Bishnoi village is one of the places to see in Jodhpur.

### Umaid Heritage Art School

This school is a place to explore the traditional arts of Rajasthan. The lessons on miniature Rajasthani painting are conducted here for free. It is one of the unique places in Jodhpur.
