# Kolkata

Kolkata, formerly Calcutta, is the second largest city in India after Mumbai. Sharing an intimate history with the British East India Company, Kolkata is said to be where India and England meet. Embark on a heritage walking tour and learn about the long and vibrant culture and history of how Kolkata came to be.

As the land of the Hindu goddess, Kali, take a journey through a region of temples and let it open your eyes to the sanctity of local worship and prayer rituals. Visit important landmarks like Jain Temples, the Dakshineswar Temple, and Kalighat–a famous temple dedicated to the goddess, Kali.

## An unforgettable experience along the Hooghly

"To understand the heart and soul of Kolkata, one must wander its vibrant streets and immerse oneself in its rich tapestry of culture and history." - Anonymous
As the sun dips below the horizon, casting a warm glow over the bustling streets of Kolkata, you will find yourself swept away by the city's magnetic charm. From its iconic landmarks to its bustling markets and mouthwatering street food, Kolkata is a treasure trove waiting to be discovered. Join us on a journey through the City of Joy as we unravel its many secrets and immerse ourselves in its vibrant spirit. Despite its bustling streets and chaotic traffic, Kolkata exudes a unique charm and warmth that captivates visitors.

##### Victoria Memorial, Kolkata

#### A visual prelude

Picture this – the mighty [Howrah Bridge] standing sentinel over the languid waters of the Hooghly River, its steel girders gleaming in the golden light of dawn. As the city awakens to the sound of vendors hawking their wares and rickshaw pullers weaving through the crowded streets, you will be captivated by the energy and vitality that permeates every corner of Kolkata. This is a city that pulsates with life, where every alleyway tells a story and every corner hides a hidden gem waiting to be discovered.

#### Through the narrow lanes filled with history

Kolkata, the cultural capital of India, is steeped in history and tradition. From its colonial past to its role in the Indian independence movement, the city bears witness to centuries of triumphs and tribulations. As you stroll through the narrow lanes of North Kolkata, you will be transported back in time, where grand colonial mansions stand side by side with ancient temples and bustling bazaars. Don't miss a visit to the iconic [Victoria Memorial], a majestic white marble structure that pays homage to the city's colonial heritage, or the sprawling grounds of the Maidan, where locals gather to relax and unwind amidst verdant greenery.

#### Mouthwatering delicacies await

No visit to Kolkata is complete without a stroll down Park Street – the city's culinary epicentre and nightlife hub. From fine dining restaurants to hole-in-the-wall eateries serving mouthwatering street food, Park Street offers a gastronomic adventure like no other. Indulge in a hearty meal of biryani, savour delectable sweets, or sip on a refreshing glass of chai at one of the many roadside tea stalls – the options are endless. And as night falls, the street comes alive with the sound of music and laughter as locals and tourists alike dance the night away at the city's legendary nightclubs and bars.

#### The soul of Kolkata

From the iconic Howrah Bridge to the vibrant bustle of New Market, Kolkata is home to numerous famous spots and hidden gems waiting to be discovered. Especially the soulful music of Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore, known as Rabindra Sangeet. From the beautiful "Ekla Cholo Re" to the captivating "Amar Shonar Bangla," Rabindra Sangeet embodies the essence of Bengal's cultural heritage unlike any other art form. Do not miss the chance to attend a live performance or explore the Rabindra Bharati Museum to learn more about Tagore's life and legacy.
Venture into the vast grounds of the [Indian Museum] to immerse yourself in the city's rich history and heritage, or take a leisurely boat ride along the tranquil waters of the Hooghly River to catch a glimpse of Kolkata's waterfront skyline. To experience authentic Bengali culture, be sure to visit Kumartuli, the city's renowned potters' quarter, where artisans create exquisite clay idols for festivals and celebrations.

[Kolkata] City Skyline[Kolkata], the City of Joy

**[Kolkata] ** (also known as Calcutta) is currently the third-most populous metropolitan city in [India] after [Mumbai] and [Delhi]. It has many placed to visit which are of interest to tourists.

## Museums and libraries

\[ [edit] \]

### Victoria Memorial

\[ [edit] \]

**[Victoria Memorial] ** was built in [Kolkata] to commemorate the Empress of India and Queen of the United Kingdom, [Queen Victoria] after her death in 1901, located at Queen's Way. The [Victoria Memorial] was modelled on the [Taj Mahal] and was commissioned in 1906 by [Lord Curzon]. Opened to the public in 1921, it was designed by the architects [William Emerson] and his protégé Vincent Esch at the extraordinary cost of 10.5 million rupees ($262,500), all of which was collected as voluntary donations, mostly from [British] and Indian nobility. The memorial holds paintings of the [British royal family], miniature paintings of the Mughal School, [oil paintings] of the Company School (notably the uncle - nephew pair of [Thomas Daniell] and [William Daniell]), historical artefacts like the throne of the [Nawab of Bengal], many lithographs and documents of historical interest, and various post-Raj artefacts significant in the history of Kolkata (added to the collection after independence). The memorial is set in extensive and beautiful lawns and is lit up at night. A laser audio-visual show is held on the lawns every evening. 'Nike', the Greek Goddess of victory, on the top of the museum is said to be haunted and has been prominently featured in many Kolkata stories and novels. It is regarded with pride in Kolkata and colloquially referred to as the "Victoria".[\[1\]] 

### Indian Museum

\[ [edit] \]

The courtyards and gardens inside the [Indian Museum] 

The **[Indian Museum] ** is the largest museum in [Asia] and the oldest in the [Asia - Pacific] region (est. 1814 at the location of the [Asiatic Society]). The Museum shifted to its present residence in 1875. Situated on [Jawahar Lal Nehru Road], it houses a collection of [Indian natural history] and an [Indian Art] collection to rival the [Smithsonian Institution] and the [British Museum]. Of specific note are the [meteorite] hall and [dinosaur] hall in the Natural History and Geology section, the [numismatics] section and the collections of [Gandhara Art], [Burmese] woodwork, [Mughal] miniatures and [Tibetan] banner sections in the Indian Art section. The [Anthropological Survey of India] headquarters and the Government College of Art and Craft are housed in the same building. The [Geological Survey of India] headquarters moved from the museum to [Bidhan Nagar] recently. The Indian Museum has a library of excellent historical value, with a special focus on the _Raj_ and Kolkata.

**SYAMAPRASAD MOOKHERJEE PORT HERITAGE INITIATIVE**

**The River Hooghly - Rich History**

Without appreciating the river Hooghly no introduction to the city is complete. It is as historic as the Thames is to the city of London. The Port of London and the Port of Calcutta were the two most important ports in the British Empire.

**Kolkata Port Heritage Tour**

Launched in 2018 by the Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port, the _Kolkata Port Heritage Tour_ ( styled A VOYAGE THROUGH TIDE AND TIME) offers a guided steamer journey along the Hooghly River, highlighting the city's maritime history. Conceptualized and designed by Maritime Heritage Campaigner and Port's former Security Adviser and heritage Coordinator Gautam Chakraborti, the unique tour connected both land and the river, covering the Port Museum , the historic Man-of-War Jetty and views of colonial-era dockyards and landmarks. It currently runs under another name "A Dialogue with the River" , in association with NGO KolikataMaritime and reputed private tour operators. In 2022 , the Port converted one of the 1940s built Paddle Steamers ( PS Bhopal) into a Heritage vessel . This was the brainchild of Vinit Kumar, the then Port Chairman. [\[2\]] [\[3\]] [\[4\]] 

### Marble Palace

\[ [edit] \]

The privately owned [Marble Palace] 

The **[Marble Palace] ** is a privately owned collection of eclectic sculptures, paintings and a small menagerie and aviary off [Chittaranjan Avenue] in [North Kolkata]. Built by [Raja Rajendra Mullick] in 1835, it houses two little-publicized [Reubens] and a [Joshua Reynolds], not to mention over 50 varieties of [marble] which grace the interiors of this mansion. Since COVID-19 in India, this palace is strictly prohibited for outsiders and tourists.

### Birla Industrial & Technological Museum

\[ [edit] \]

[Birla Industrial & Technological Museum] 

[Birla Industrial & Technological Museum] on [Gurusaday Dutta Road], was inaugurated in 1959 as the first popular science museum in Asia. Modelled on the [Deutsches Museum], it has interactive popular science exhibits and a significant collection of historical industrial holdings in India. Its collection of old [gramophones], [sound recorders], [telephones], [steam engines], [road rollers], and other industrial machinery of the period 1880–1950 is very significant. The museum sports a vintage model of the [Rolls-Royce Phantom I] make. It also actively organizes summer camps, awareness programs, and astronomy observations for school children.

### Science City

\[ [edit] \]

[Science City] 

[Science City] is a complex on the [John Burdon Sanderson Haldane Avenue] featuring a lot of interactive science and live bioscience exhibits, as well as having Kolkata's first [OMNIMAX] theatre.

### Rabindra Bharati Museum

\[ [edit] \]

The facade of [Jorasanko Thakur Bari] 

The [Rabindra Bharati Museum] is the ancestral home of the [Tagore family] and was converted into a museum in 1961, located at Dwarka Nath Tagore Lane near [Rabindra Sarani]. The brick mansions were the cultural hub of Kolkata for close to a century and were a major force in the [women's liberation movement]. It hosted the first [Brahmo] wedding and was an important center of the Independence movement. The museum has three large galleries - one of the life and works of [Rabindranath], a second gallery about his close relatives such as father [Debendranath Tagore], [Abanindranath Tagore], [Gaganendranath Tagore] etc. and a third gallery on the [Bengal Renaissance] in general.

### Gurusaday Museum

\[ [edit] \]

[Gurusaday Museum], on [Diamond Harbour Road], is the outcome of a lifetime collection of traditional [Bengal folk arts] in undivided [Bengal] by Sir [Gurusaday Dutt]. On his death in 1941, the collection was handed over to the Bratachari Society founded by Sir [Gurusaday Dutt] to preserve and protect [Bengal folk arts]. It was opened as a museum to the public with the help of the [Government of India] in 1963. It contains, among other fine handicrafts, [terracotta] panels, [kantha] or folk [quilt] work, and patas (or hand painted scrolls of the late 1900s), notably of the Kalighat School. The [Asutosh Museum of Indian Art], on [College Street], is the other museum specializing in [Bengal folk arts], but with significant archaeological holdings from sites in [West Bengal] and [Bihar] like [Chandraketugarh] and [Tamluk]. The first university owned museum in India, run by the [University of Calcutta] and is named after its famous vice chancellor Sir [Asutosh Mukherjee].

### Nehru Children's Museum

\[ [edit] \]

Nehru Children's Museum is named after [Jawaharlal Nehru], whose love for children was well known. It is located at [Jawahar Lal Nehru Road]. In order to pay homage to Pandit Jawahar Lal Nehru, National Cultural Association established the Museum in 1972 in Panditji's name on his birthday. The museum has a collection of dolls and toys from across the globe and has a doll - based retelling of the Indian [epics] [Ramayana] and [Mahabharata]. Established in 1972 close to the [Victoria Memorial], and commonly referred to as "Nehru Children's Museum"; this museum is aging awkwardly fast. There are dolls which are not only bounded to India, but also of different states, countries, religion like the dolls of Bangladesh, [Japan], China, Rhodesia, Korea, Indonesia. Other than these, there are dolls which are dressed according to the places like Delhi, Assam, West Bengal, Chennai, Maharashtra, Andaman, Mexico, Portuguese and many more. There is even a separate cabin displaying various forms and styles of Ganesh.

### Sabarna Sangrahashala

\[ [edit] \]

[Sabarna Sangrahashala] located at Baro Bari, Barisha, is the only family museum of Kolkata open to public. It was established in 2005 by the Sabarna Roy Choudhury Paribar Parishad and is a tourist destination specially for those who want to know more about the history of Kolkata. It is also a research institute in the making. The museum possesses some of the rarest _Kabulatipatras, documents_ and _articles_ of historical importance besides several artifacts dating back to the 17th century. The museum is dedicated to students for creating an awareness of history, heritage, and culture of the land. Every year in the month of February, the museum organizes the International History and Heritage Exhibition which is the only one of its kind in Eastern India.

### National Library of India

\[ [edit] \]

[National Library] 

[National Library of India] located in Alipore is India's leading public library. It was inaugurated in 1836 by the Governor General [Lord Metcalfe] by transferring 4675 books from the [College of Fort William]. Public donations were the main source of books for the library, and by donations of Rupees 300 from proprietors. [Dwarakanath Tagore] was the first proprietor of the library. The library was initially only partially public, as poor students could use the library for a limited period of time. The [Imperial Library] was founded in 1891 by merging several libraries like those of the [East India College] and East India Board. Governor General [Lord Curzon] initiated the merger of these two libraries into a single Imperial Library in 1903 at the [Metcalfe Hall]. The goal of the library was to collect _every_ book written about India at any time. The Assistant Librarian of the British Museum John Macfarlane was the first librarian and was succeeded by the first Indian librarian [Harinath De]. The library was moved to its present quarters in [Belvedere Estate], [Alipore] and renamed the [National Library]. It is a fully public library which co-ordinates the activities of all other Indian public libraries. True to its goal, any book published in India today has to send one copy to the [National library] in the spirit of the [Library of Congress], [United States].

### Birla Planetarium

\[ [edit] \]

[Birla Planetarium] in Kolkata runs many shows on educational and entertainment purposes about astronomy, astrophysics, space science, history of astronomy and mythology regarding stars and planets. It is situated on [Jawahar Lal Nehru Road] near several notable places like [Indian Museum], [Victoria Memorial], [Maidan], and [St. Paul's Cathedral]. It was inaugurated by the Prime Minister of India, Jawaharlal Nehru, on July 2, 1963. Projection on spherical ceiling accompanied by live audio (Bengali, English, and Hindi) is the major attraction of the Birla Planetarium. It also has an electronics laboratory, astronomy gallery, and an astronomical observatory equipped with a Celestron C-14 Telescope.

Kolkata also has some other small museums like the [Rail Museum], Tram Museum, [Naval Aircraft Museum], [Fanatic Sports Museum], [Ashutosh Museum of Indian Art], [Netaji Bhawan], [Swami Vivekananda Museum], [State Archaeological Museum], [Academy of Fine Arts], [The Asiatic Society], [Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Museum], [Mother's Wax Museum] and the Police Museum.

### Calcutta High Court

\[ [edit] \]

Facade of the [Calcutta High Court] 

[Calcutta High Court] – It is the oldest [High Court] in [India]. It was established as the _High Court of Judicature at Fort William_ on 1 July 1862 under the _High Courts Act, 1861_. It has jurisdiction over the [state] of [West Bengal] and the [Union Territory] of the [Andaman and Nicobar Islands]. The High Court building is an exact replica of the Stand Haus in [Ypres], Belgium. It is recorded that when the original Stand Haus burnt down, a blueprint of Granville's Calcutta High Court had to be consulted before rebuilding it. The court has a sanctioned judge strength of 63. Despite, the name of the city was officially changed from _Calcutta_ to _Kolkata_ in 2001, the old name is retained by the court as it is an institution.

### Bankshall Court

\[ [edit] \]

[Bankshall Court] – It is the present City Sessions Court of Kolkata. Two of its buildings are old heritage.

### Raj Bhavan

\[ [edit] \]

caption2 = The facade of the [Raj Bhavan] 

[Raj Bhavan] (Government House) – It was built in the early 19th century and is modeled on [Kedleston Hall]. The house was once the seat of the [viceroys] of India; later, when the government moved to New Delhi, it became the residence of the Governor of [Bengal], a function that it fulfills to this day. While the basic features of Kedleston have been faithfully copied (the Palladian Front, the Dome etc.), Government House is a much larger, three-storeyed structure. Also, the Government of India evidently did not have the funding constraints that forced the Curzons to leave their house incomplete: Government House has all four wings originally conceived for Kedleston. So today, a 'complete', brick built Kedleston, on a much grander scale, is located in its acres of gardens at the heart of the Kolkata business district.

### Town Hall

\[ [edit] \]

[Town Hall] 

[Town Hall] – In Roman-Doric style, this building was built by the architect Col. John Garstin in 1813 with a fund of Rupees seven lakhs raised from lottery to provide the Europeans with a place for social gatherings. At first, the hall was placed under a committee, which allowed the public to use the hall under such terms and conditions as were fixed by the Government. The public could visit the ground floor hall to see statues and large size portrait paintings, but they were not allowed indiscriminate access to the upper storey. Applications for the use of the upper storey were to be made to the committee. In 1867 Town Hall came under the custody of the Calcutta Municipality (later on Kolkata Municipal Corporation). In the year of 1897 the Town Hall had been partly renovated. After political independence in 1947, indiscriminate interference with the structure inevitably took its toll. Fortunately, that was prevented in 1998 by timely intervention. The town hall was featured on the 6th leg of [The Amazing Race 18], when the teams had to compete in a tea-drinking roadblock.

### Writer's Building

\[ [edit] \]

A panoramic view of [Writers' Building] in Central Kolkata

[Writers' Building] – It is the secretariat building of the [State] [Government of West Bengal] in [India].[\[6\]] The Writers' Building originally served as the office for writers of the [British East India Company], hence the name. Designed by Thomas Lyon in 1777 the Writers' building has gone through several extensions over the years. In 1821 a 128 ft-long verandah with ionic style columns, each 32 ft high, were added on the first and second floors. From 1889 to 1906 two new blocks were added. It acquired its Greco-Roman look, complete with the portico in the central bay and the red surface of the exposed brick. The parapet was put in place and the statues sculpted by William Fredric Woodington in 1883, that line the terrace, were installed.[\[7\]] The giant pediment at the centre is crowned with the statue of [Minerva]. The terrace also contains several other statues and notable among them are four clusters of statues, christened 'Justice', 'Commerce', 'Science' and 'Agriculture', with the Greek Gods and Goddesses of these four streams ( [Zeus], [Hermes], [Athena] and [Demeter] respectively) flanked by a European and an Indian practitioner of these vocations, adorn the building.[\[8\]] 

The 150 meter long Writers' Building covers the entire northern stretch of the a water body locally called [Lal Dighi] in [Binay Badal Dinesh Baag] area. Various departments of the [West Bengal] government are housed in this building. It is an edifice of great political significance and memories of the [Indian Independence Movement]. Writer's building was used as [Chief Minister's Office] and secretariat. However, from October 2013, certain departments and the office of Chief Minister moved to [Nabanna] in Howrah to facilitate restoration of Writers Building.[\[9\]] [\[10\]] [\[11\]]

The [General Post Office] (GPO) in Kolkata

## Historic hotels

\[ [edit] \]

The [Great Eastern Hotel] (officially Lalit Great Eastern Hotel) in February 2014, a large part of the hotel is still under renovationThe [Grand Hotel], now

The [Great Eastern Hotel] (officially Lalit Great Eastern Hotel) was founded as the Auckland Hotel in 1841, at the crossroads of the Hemanta Basu Sarani and British India Street, founded by confectioner [David Wilson] and named after the then Governor General [Lord Auckland]. It grew from strength to strength over the 19th and first half of the 20th century. Locally known as "Wilson's Hotel", it was also known as "Auckland Hotel and the "Hall of Nations" in the 19th century, and was referred to as the "Japani Hotel" (Japanese Hotel) colloquially in the 20th century, due to a large number of Japanese tourists there. The hotel was extremely elite, referred to as the "Jewel of the East" and "the best hotel East of the [Suez] " by [Mark Twain] on his voyage along the Equator, and described by [Rudyard Kipling] in " [The City of Dreadful Night] ". It had notable board members like the author [Peary Chand Mitra] and stockholders like [W. C. Bonnerjee] \- president of the [Indian National Congress]. The hotel was famous for its new year parties thrown by [Maharajahs] (like the [Maharajah of Cooch Behar]) until the 1950s. It has been host to such notables as [Elizabeth II] on her India visit, [Nikita Khrushchev] 's delegation in the 1950s, and visiting international [cricket] teams. The hotel kitchens, staffed by the legendary [Baruahs] of [Chittagong] (now in [Bangladesh]), was the talk of Kolkata. It steadily progressed downhill since the 1970s, and was taken over by the [Government of West Bengal] in 1975 on grounds of insolvency. [Labour union] problems caused the hotel to worsen until a sensationalist news campaign by [The Telegraph] exposed the sorry state of the hotel in the 1990s. The hotel was privatized in November 2005 with the help of PricewaterhouseCoopers and has been re-christened The Lalit Great Eastern Kolkata. After an extensive restoration, a part of the property was reopened in November 2013. The Lalit Great Eastern offers 244 rooms and suites along with four restaurants and bars, the largest conference and banqueting facility in Kolkata, besides all other five-star deluxe facilities and services.

The [Grand Hotel] had humble beginnings. In the 1870s Mrs. Annie Monk opened her boarding house at numbers 13, 14 and 15 [Chowringhee]. In the meantime, [Arathoon Stephen], an [Armenian] from [Isfahan] had arrived in [Calcutta] and proceeded to make his fortune in the jewellery business. By the early 20th century he had put together a sizeable fortune and had purchased numbers 16 and 17 Chowringhee. Soon he purchased Mrs. Monk's boarding house and number 18 Chowringhee as well, and proceeded to develop all the properties together into the hotel that came to be known as the Grand Hotel.

Acquired by the real-estate baron, it turned into a 3-story 500-room hotel. Acquired by hotelier [Mohan Singh Oberoi] in 1938, it became the Oberoi Grand. The hotel got a major lift during World War II when about 4000 soldiers were billeted there and would party regularly.

## City parks

\[ [edit] \]

Main article: [List of urban parks in Kolkata] 

[Maidan], overlooking the Chowringhee SkylineOne of the few lakes inside the [Alipore Zoological Gardens] 

[Maidan] means "field" in [Hindi], [Marathi], [Urdu] and [Bengali]. The Kolkata Maidan was once a vast uninterrupted field right down to the edge of the [Hoogli River], but is now being encroached upon by the city and is fragmented by roads. The Maidan has nurtured sports like [Polo] and has been the home of [equestrianism], [horse racing], [football], [cricket] and rugby in Kolkata. It houses numerous clubs including the "big three" of Indian football - [Mohun Bagan Athletic Club], [East Bengal Football Club] and [Mohammedan Sporting Club] along with their respective home stadiums. The arterial [Jawaharlal Nehru Road], the [Eden Gardens], the [Millennium Park] and the associated 3 km long Riverfront beautification project border the Maidan. The Maidan abounds with monuments and statues, the most famous of them being [Shaheed Minar] and the statue of ace footballer [Gostho Pal]. Elliot Park, [Mohor Kunja] and [Victoria Memorial] complex are three parks which are situated in the Maidan area.

[Rabindra Sarobar] or "The Lake" or 'Dhakuria Lake' is an artificial lake and urban park in the spirit of [Central Park], [New York City]. The park has a lake and an island with a footbridge, an open-air amphitheatre ( [Nazrul Mancha]), a sports stadium ( [Rabindra Sarobar Stadium]), a children's park and the rowing clubs of [Calcutta Rowing Club], Bengal Rowing Club and [Lake Club].

A view of [Rabindra Sarobar] in South KolkataLush green grass along the lake at [New Town Eco Park] 

The [Central Park] is a large urban park in the centre of the [Bidhan Nagar] township in [Kolkata Metropolitan Area], with a lake in the middle and information technology and government offices along its fringes.

[Prakriti Tirtha] or 'Eco Park', located in [New Town] in [Kolkata Metropolitan Area], is situated on a 480 acres (190 ha) plot and is surrounded by a 104 acres (42 ha) waterbody with an island called Ekanto in the middle.[\[12\]] [\[13\]] The park has been divided into three broad parts; (1) ecological zones like wetlands, grasslands, and [urban forest], (2) theme gardens and open spaces, and (3) urban recreational spaces.[\[13\]] The Eco Park is further divided into different sub-parts according to the different types of fauna planted.[\[14\]] Recently in the month of November 2017, it also opened up another section which displays the replica of "The seven wonders of the World". Toy trains and chair cars are available for the tourists on rent for making round around the park. There are four Gates for entry and exit purpose.

The [Acharya Jagadish Chandra Bose Indian Botanic Garden] (previously known as Indian Botanical Garden) spread over 270 acres (1.1 km2), was founded in 1786 and is the oldest botanical gardens in India. It is located at the bank of Hoogly River, [Shibpur], [Howrah] near [Kolkata]. Housing 50,000 species, the [Botanical Survey of India] and one of the world's most historically relevant [herbariums], it is famous for its 250-year-old, 98-foot-tall [The Great Banyan] tree - which has the largest girth of any banyan tree ever recorded (1300 ft).

[Alipore Zoological Gardens] was founded in 1875, inaugurated by The Prince of Wales (later [Edward VII]). Initially started from the personal menagerie of the then Governor General of Bengal [Arthur Wellesley] and [Carl Louis Schwendler] \- a [German] electrician, it grew based on gifts from British and Indian nobility - like Raja Suryakanta Acharya of [Mymensingh] in whose honour the open air tiger enclosure is named the "Mymensingh Enclosure". The zoo was ill-reputed because of cross breeding experiments between [lions] and [tigers] to produce strains like [tigons], [ligers], and [litigons]. [Adwaita] was a male [Aldabra giant tortoise] in the zoo who died in March, 2006. He was reported to have been more than 250 years old - a candidate for the longest-lived animal. The other success story of the zoo was a live birth of the rare [Sumatran rhinoceros] in 1889. The zoo is downsizing to meet animal comfort requirements laid down by the [Central Zoo Authority of India]. The zoo is also on the [flyway] for several [migratory birds] like the [sarus crane].

A host of new amusement parks have sprung up in recent times - the most notable being are [Nicco Park] in [Bidhan Nagar] \- the first modern amusement park in [Kolkata Metropolitan Area] and [Aquatica] in [New Town] \- the theme water park. Other important parks include Nalban in [Bidhannagar] and Captain Bhery Eco & Aquatic Hub in Chingrighata.

[Benubana Chhaya] is a categorized water park in [Kolkata] located at [Bishwa Bangla Sarani], [Baishnabghata Patuli Township]. The [Nature Park of India] is a reserved area administered by the government of India.

\[ [edit] \]

The [Shaheed Minar] or Ochterlony Monument, KolkataThe [Victoria Memorial] A view of [Prinsep Ghat] in the eveningGwalior Monument

The [Shaheed Minar] or "Tower of the Martyrs", (formerly Ochterlony Monument) was constructed on the northern fringe of the [Maidan] in honour of [Sir David Ochterlony] who commanded the [British East India Company] forces in the [Gurkha War] (1814–1816). It was renamed [Shaheed Minar] in honor of the fallen [freedom fighters] after Indian independence.

Situated at [Prinsep Ghat], is a [Palladian] porch in the memory of the eminent Anglo-Indian scholar and [antiquary] [James Prinsep] was designed by W. Fitzgerald and constructed in 1843.Located between the Water Gate and the St George's Gate of the [Fort William], the monument to Prinsep is rich in Greek and Gothic inlays. It was restored by the state's public works department in November 2001 and has since been well-maintained.[\[15\]] In its initial years, all royal British entourages used the Prinsep Ghat jetty for embarkation and disembarkation.[\[16\]] [\[17\]] [\[18\]] 

Other memorials in Kolkata include the Panioty fountain, Police Memorial and the [William Jones] [obelisk] at the [South Park Street Cemetery]. [Lascar War Memorial] located on the Napier Road in the [Hastings] area, is a memorial dedicated to the memory of 896 [Lascars] (sailor or militiaman from Indian subcontinent), who died fighting for the [British Navy] during the World War I. [Gwalior Monument], also known as Ellenborough’s Folly is an octagonal cenotaph about 60 feet high, crowned with a bronze dome cast from guns captured from the Marathas. It was erected in 1847 by Lord Ellenborough, the Governor-General of India, as a memorial to the officers and men who fell during the Gwalior War in 1843.

Kolkata has many statues celebrating [British] heritage and the Indian Renaissance and freedom movement. The [Maidan] is a particularly good place for statue-hunting. A few of the more notable landmarks are as follows:

- The statue of [Subhas Chandra Bose], by Marathi sculptor Nagesh Yoglekar is located at the [Shyambazar] five-point crossing.
- The statue of [Sir James Outram], by Irish sculptor [John Henry Foley], built in 1874, is located in front of the [Victoria Memorial] originally at the [Mother Teresa Sarani] and [Chowringhee Avenue] crossing.
- The statue of [Iswarchandra Vidyasagar], by Bengali sculptor Pramod Gopal Chattopadhyay, built in 1899, is located on the premises of [Sanskrit College].
- The statue of [Rabindranath Tagore] by [Soviet] [Belarusian] sculptor of Jewish origin [Zair Azgur], built in 1963, is located at the premises of [Jorasanko Thakur Bari], the [Tagore family] residence.
- The statue of [David Hare] was built in 1847. It is one of Kolkata's few marble statues, on the precincts of [Presidency University].
- Besides, the statue of the celebrated Bengali film star [Uttam Kumar] stands at the Deshapran Sashmal Road.
- The statue of [Karl Marx] near Maidan.
- Bengali War Memorial, located at Vidyasagar Udyan, is a war memorial dedicated to the fallen soldiers of [49th Bengali Regiment], the only British Indian Army regiment to consist entirely of ethnic Bengalis, who served in World War I.

## Sports venues

\[ [edit] \]

| |
| --- |
| This article **is in [list] format but may read better as [prose] **. You can help by [converting this article], if appropriate. [Editing help] is available._(January 2025)_ |

[Eden Gardens] Cricket Stadium[Salt Lake Stadium], Kolkata

- [Eden Gardens] is one of only two 100,000 seater amphitheatres for the game of cricket (the other being [Melbourne Cricket Ground]). It was initially an extension of the [Maidan] under the supervision of [Governor General] [Lord Auckland], and looked after by the [Eden sisters] of the [Auckland] family. The gardens house a transported [Burmese] [pagoda] of exquisite design. The pavilion was built in 1871 and the 1st [first class match] played in the season of 1911-12. It has since hosted many international [Test matches], [one day matches] and tournaments, including the final of the [Cricket World Cup] in 1987. Its exalted status in cricketing history comes from the lush outfield, stellar performances (like [V.V.S. Laxman] 's 281), and not least the intense crowd support. The stadium has a history of crowd violence - involving riots in the stands in 1967 (when the stadium burnt), 1996 and 1999.
- [Yuva Bharati Krirangan], also called the [Salt Lake Stadium], is a 122,000 - strong amphitheatre used for soccer matches and concerts. It is purposely not a [home stadium] for any soccer team. It hosted the bulk of the 3rd [South Asian Federation Games] in Kolkata in 1987.
- The [Netaji Indoor Stadium], adjacent to the [Eden Gardens], is a 12,000 seater air-conditioned indoor stadium, having hosted internationally significant events like the [state funeral] of [Mother Teresa] in 1997. Constructed in 1975 to host the [World Table Tennis Championships], it also has the Kshudiram Anusilani Kendra - a smaller auditorium without gallery facilities for training purposes.
- The [Calcutta Cricket and Football Club] (originally the [Calcutta Cricket Club]) is the second oldest cricket club in the world, after the [Marylebone Cricket Club]. Founded in 1792 as the Calcutta Cricket Club, it merged later with the [Calcutta Football Club] (founded 1872) to become the Calcutta Cricket and Football Club, and is located on [Gurusaday Dutta Road]. It has arguably the most picturesque cricket ground in Kolkata. Recent evidence in the form of an article in [Hickey's Bengal Gazette], suggests the club existed in 1780 - which would make it the oldest cricket club in the world.
- The [Royal Calcutta Turf Club] is the oldest turf club in India, and one of the most beautiful and largest in the world. Encompassing a significant area of the [Maidan], it was founded in 1847, and is distinguished for its "Monsoon Track" - one of the fastest draining tracks in the world. It was conferred the epithet "Royal" by [George V] in 1912.
- Kolkata is one of the few cities in the world to boast of three beautiful 18 holes [golf courses] within city limits - at the [Royal Calcutta Golf Club], [Tollygunj Club] and [Fort William]. The [Royal Calcutta Golf Club], founded in 1829, is the oldest [golf club] outside the [British Isles]. It was variously located in [Dum Dum] and [Maidan], but finally settled down in [Tollygunj] and was conferred the epithet "Royal' by [George V] at the [Delhi Durbar] in 1911.
- Kolkata is home to the world's oldest active [polo] club, the [Calcutta Polo Club]. Situated on the [Maidan], the club was founded in 1862 and is the second oldest polo club in the world. Kolkata has yet another polo club in the Fort William Polo Club. The CPC polo grounds are located in the maidan and maintained jointly with the RCTC.
- [Mohun Bagan Athletic Club], located in the Maidan area of Kolkata, is one of the oldest sporting institutions in the country. It was founded on 15 August 1889 and is one of India's top football clubs. The club is also known for its exploits in cricket, hockey, tennis, and athletics. Mohun Bagan club was also associated with India's freedom struggle.
- Kolkata is also home to one of the oldest squash and rackets clubs in the world - the [Calcutta Racket Club], founded in 1793.
- [South Club], established in 1920, has beautiful tennis courts and has been the venue for [Davis Cup] matches.
- [Rabindra Sarobar] is the home and pool for the rowing clubs of [Lake Club], Bengal Rowing Club and [Calcutta Rowing Club].

## Markets and malls

\[ [edit] \]

Main article: [List of shopping malls in Kolkata] 

[South City Mall] Interior[South City Mall], biggest mall of KolkataSir Stuart Hogg Market ( [New Market]), Kolkata

[New Market] is Kolkata's historic shopping district. Opened in 1874, it was named Hogg Market after the commissioner Sir [Stuart Hogg]. The beautiful fountain and benches at the market no longer exist, but the popularity of the market has not waned, and the beautiful gothic clock tower recalls the British heritage of the market. It was renamed New Market after Independence. New Market led the way for [Christmas] and [New Year] festivities with confectionery shops like Nahoum's putting up a special spread. The market is organized on the basis of merchandise. It burnt down partially in 1985 but has been restored and expanded with a new building which houses very famous Kashmir Handicraft store-Pumposh.

The [Mother Teresa Sarani], [Abanindra Nath Tagore Sarani] and [Shakespeare Sarani] are considered to be a [high street] of Kolkata with many commercial establishments and shopping complexes, boutiques, restaurants and stand-alone retail outlets.[\[19\]] [Gariahat] and [Shyambazar] are two shopping districts catering mostly to the middle class and lower middle class of Kolkata. [College Street] is an area famous for its bookstores.

Kolkata has seen a spurt of shopping malls with the rise of the buying power of the Kolkata populace. Shopping arcades like [Forum Mall] and Emami Shoppers' City in Central Calcutta have brought international brands from [Swarovski] to [Godiva] to the city, breaking the city's dependence on the older market complexes like A.C. Market, [Dakshinapan shopping center] and Vardaan Market, which were mainly [Chinese] import dependent.

Most of the new malls in Kolkata are located in the newer developed areas like [Bidhan Nagar], [New Town] and along the Bishwa Bangla Sarani. Some of the popular malls in Kolkata are City Centre (Bidhan Nagar), City Centre 2, Axis Mall and Home Town (New Town), [South City Mall] (the biggest mall in Eastern India) (Jadavpur), Acropolis Mall (Rajdanga in [Kasba] / East Kolkata Township), Metropolis Mall (Hiland Park), [Mani Square Mall] (Kankurgachi). Swabhumi has been modeled as an [ethnic] shopping arcade near the [Bishwa Bangla Sarani] in [Phoolbagan]. A new luxury shopping mall, [Quest Mall] has been opened in [Park Circus] by the RP-Sanjiv Goenka Group, which has brought many foreign brands such as Breitling, Burberry and Gucci for the first time to Kolkata.[\[20\]] [\[21\]]

## It’s a long story

Once the capital of the British Raj, Kolkata (formerly Calcutta) is a kaleidoscope of sights, sounds, and human life. Hemming the Hooghly River and home to 15 million people, West Bengal’s capital is a place where stately neo-classical and Indo-Saracenic architecture sits alongside jam-packed streets and teeming markets. Travelers find plenty of things to do and feed their souls here, from discovering the city’s Raj-era monuments to exploring crowded bazaars, temples, pottery-making communities, and bookshop-lined streets.

## All about Kolkata

Local Currency

Indian Rupee (₹)

When to visit

The rule for visiting Kolkata is to avoid its two periods of challenging weather: the humid heat of April–May when temperatures regularly hit 104°F (40°C) and the drenching monsoon rains of June–October. On either side are the cooler, usually dry days of November through March, which are comfortable for sightseeing. This winter season also coincides with some of Kolkata’s annual celebrations, from the color-splattering religious festival of Holi every March to December’s sparky Jazzfest and Christmas festivities.

Getting around

Guided tours, taxis, tuk-tuks, and ride-hailing apps are the most convenient option for zipping around Kolkata’s sights. The city’s famous mustard-yellow cabs and buzzy tuk-tuks are inexpensive and easily flagged down at the roadside. Alternatively, follow locals by using the city’s Metro trains or public buses, although their crowds, routes, and fares can be difficult to navigate. If time allows, Kolkata’s street trams are great for fun, cheap, but slow sightseeing.

Traveler tips

One of the world’s first non-church cemeteries, Kolkata’s South Park Street Cemetery is a tree-shaded compound that houses 1,600 British souls who died between 1767 and 1830. A wander around its birdsong-filled paths reveals such graves as that of Charles Dickens’ second son, Walter Landor Dickens, and Rose Aylmer, a British aristocrat believed to have died from cholera induced by “eating too many pineapples” at the tender age of 22.

The city of Joy, as  Dominique Lapierre had infamously called Kolkata. This is the place I believe to be the heart and soul of India. While going through the list enjoy the ride by memorizing yourself into the place and feel it. There’s nothing as soulful as Bengali culture, traditions, literature, and the people themselves. It’s the perfect balance between contemporary changes and the old school traditions. On one side I get to see the restless chaotic traffic and on the other, at a snail’s pace, the tram moves along carrying people in it. Here are 10 places to visit in Kolkata that showed me the unadulterated charm of this city.

## 1\. Kalighat Kali Temple

The first image that stays fresh in my mind about Kolkata is the Durga Pujo, and like our very own Satyajit Ray had said himself “Dominus Omnium Magister. It means God is the master of all things.” A very common sight in Kolkata is the Kali worship, and there isn’t a day that goes by here when thousands of pilgrims flock to the Kalighat temple. There wasn’t even a single dull moment at this place with the sounds of aarti and bhajans going on.

Idol worship here is done in all its grandeur, wrapping Kali Mata in gorgeous silk sarees and donning flower garlands over her. Undoubtedly this is one of the happening places in Kolkata.

## 2\. New Market (Sir Stuart Hogg Market) – Terreti Morning Bazaar

Two markets, not farther than 4 km from each other, entirely different experiences. Terreti morning bazaar, even when the entire city is sleeping, this street wakes up to light up their fires and prepare breakfast for the early birds. One among my favorites out of the numerous eat streets in Kolkata, this place offers amazing Chinese and Indian cuisines! A plate of momos straight out of the steam, and some mouthwatering manchow soup, and I was good to go for the rest of the day. An entirely contrasting situation was at New Market. A reflection of the chaotic busy life around the city. Pushcart transporters, street-side bhaji, and ghugni chaat stalls, colorful clothing materials, fruit sellers and so much more! There’s so much happening in so little time, I had to remind myself to keep moving to see the entire market. With sections that have so many things, it felt like I would be sinning if I missed out on seeing any of it.

Long story short, one entire day was spent around the Newmarket and Terreti morning market.

## 3\. Victoria Memorial Hall

A walk back in history is what this iconic Memorial, built as a dedication to Queen Victoria, offers. It hasn’t lost its charm one bit since the day it was built up.

The contrasts that Kolkata holds within it is also shown through the architectural idea of the Memorial. The 103 meters long structure has extensive European architecture with intricate elements of Mughal designs. I walked inside to the welcoming sight of aesthetically pleasing Western and Indian paintings. They often hold exhibitions here as well, and every time it’s a new experience. Don’t miss out on the gallery exhibitions here! They’re just as majestic as the memorial. I had walked around the memorial for hours and another couple of hours at the memorial garden. A few people were enjoying a peaceful morning and had a walk here. The fact that the gardens are made so accessible to the people around itself was fascinating to me. The garden has statues made of bronze and marble which looked nothing less than history being narrated through them to me. It is one of the most popular places to visit in Kolkata.

## 4\. Mother’s Wax Museum

This was my first time at a wax museum and I didn’t know what exactly to expect from here. Located in New Town, is this most visited tourist attraction of Kolkata. Live looking wax figurines of so many great figures, from Mahatma Gandhi, Mother Teresa to contemporary times celebrities such as Virat Kohli and Angelina Jolie. There’s just so much you could see here. Skillfully carved to look like the infamous people, it was almost impossible to walk around the place without having appreciated the effort that goes behind it. The number of people who flock here to click pictures with the wax figurines is yet another sight. Without exploring the museum you will feel left out also, this is one of the best places to visit in Kolkata.

## 5\. Indian Museum

For an art lover, this place is a must go to! Historical collections, archaeology, art from countries such as Egypt and so much more .the place is mostly packed with people, especially during the weekends. So planning a comfortable time based on my preference was what I had opted for. The museum is one of the most significant ones in the country and has so many fascinating exhibits!

## 6\. Sundarbans

Holding the badge of being the largest river delta and estuarine mangrove in the world, is this unbeatable wildlife experience of Sunderbans. A UNESCO World Heritage site, this natural wonder lies in the coastal region of the Bay of Bengal. Going around the early hours of 7-8 am I could spot several beautiful wildlife come out of their homes to wander around. This was a priceless sight. The distinguished varieties of plants here as well kept me informed about the expanse of natural resources we have. We often choose to stay unaware of all of these, and a visit to this place made sure that every ounce of ignorance in me was replaced by rich information. One of the exciting experiences here was spotting the royal Bengal tiger in its habitat. Entering the estuarine dekta area follows certain procedures though, such as taking beforehand permission from the office of the Field Director of Sunderbans Tiger Reserve. Without visiting Sundarbans could not make you the good city explorer. Sundarbans is great for nature lovers and so it is a must places to visit in Kolkata.

## 7\. Science City

Walking into the largest science center in India, was a memorable experience. # Top Attractions in Kolkata

Discover Kolkata's must-visit attractions. Unveil the city's rich heritage, vibrant culture, and unique experiences for your India travels.

![] 

13 December 2024

6 minutes

- [Top things to do] 

Kolkata is a fascinating destination, one that you won’t want to miss out on if you want to travel the world. One of the many reasons is the city's very rich, cultural heritage and history.

It’s also known for being one of the top birthplaces for literacy. Kolkata has produced several well-known writers and poets, including Rabindranath Tagore, with several festivals held to celebrate these icons.

If you want to start planning your trip to Kolkata, this is your guide to the city's top attractions to help you gain insight into what makes the city so special.

## Best Kolkata Attractions

If you are visiting Kolkata then you’ll want to immerse yourself in its rich, cultural history. Some of the must-visit sites, if you want the best experience, include the Howrah Bridge and the Victoria Memorial.

If you want to sample street food, consider Punchka, Kathi Rolls and Jhal Muri. Kolkata is known for skewered meat, puffed rice snacks and hollow puris with tangy tamarind and chilli, with street food being the best way for you to get the full experience.

With so much to see and do, it’s safe to say that Kolkata is one of the finest places to visit if you want to truly immerse yourself in the culture of India, and the rich historical ties it has to offer.

## Kolkata Historical Landmarks

There are several historical landmarks in Kolkata, some of them include:

### Victoria Memorial

The Victoria Memorial is a marble structure that is dedicated to the famous Queen Victoria. It was constructed in the early 1900s by Sir William Emerson and blends both Indian and British architectural styles. It’s made out of Makrana marble, with a number of other statues scattered throughout the grounds.

### Howrah Bridge

Another interesting attraction would be the [Howrah Bridge]. The bridge spans 705 metres and it’s currently listed as being one of the longest cantilever bridges in the world. It’s vital to the local transportation network and even though it is made out of steel, it has a very distinctive silhouette. It’s known by the locals as a symbol of the city, showcasing its resilience and spirit at all times.

### Indian Museum

The Indian Museum was founded in 1814, and it's home to a number of exciting exhibits. The museum is known for hosting a number of Indian paintings, with a range of decorative art. You also have a lot of artefacts and contemporary work. The Natural History selection has a huge range of fossils, taxidermy animals and minerals too. If you have a keen interest in the historical side of Kolkata, then you won’t want to miss out on this attraction.

## Kolkata Religious Sites

Kolkata is also known for being home to a number of religious sites. One of them would be the Dakshineswar Kali Temple.

### Dakshineswar Kali Temple

[The Dakshineswar Kali Temple] is a Hindu temple which can be found on the Hooghly River. It was built in the 19th century by Rani Rashmoni, who was a very wealthy landowner at the time.

One thing to take note of with the temple is that it helps to showcase a mixture of North Indian architectural styles. It has several intricate carvings and several spires tower over, helping to showcase its unique construction.

The temple is also known for having a spiritual significance. It’s associated with several saints, including Ramakrishna Paramahamsa. He helped to shape the spiritual landscape of India, making it what it is today.

### St. Paul's Cathedral

St. Paul’s Cathedral is full of stunning glass windows, with a stunning facade. It’s heavily inspired by Gothic architecture but at the same time, it has a strong Indian presence. It has been home to a number of historical events over the years, with the inside of the cathedral showcasing sculptures, intricate woodwork and stunning murals.

### Nakhoda Mosque

The Nakhoda Mosque is known for having grand domes as well as very intricate tile work. The main dome is very impressive but if you look around it, you will notice that it is also surrounded by a number of smaller domes, which help to make the structure. The mosque can house thousands of worshippers, so it is an integral part of the culture.

### Birla Mandir

Birla Mandir’s architecture tends to revolve around impressive stonework and intricate carvings. With white marble and stunning designs, this is not just a place of worship. It’s known for hosting a number of cultural events, including community gatherings and festivals.

### Belur Math

[Belur Math] is a complex temple that showcases a number of different architectural styles. There are notable Islamic, Christian and Hindu influences, which all help to showcase the nature of the religion. This is a place of pilgrimage too, for those who follow Swami Vivekananda and Sri Ramakrishna.

## Parks in Kolkata

There are several scenic parks you can visit during your time in Kolkata. These parks in Kolkata provide an opportunity to relax and unwind.

### Maidan

Maidan is one of the biggest parks in the area. It has lots of open fields and several recreational areas. It’s a hub for sports too, so if you’re a fan of cricket, football or even tennis, or rugby, you’ll be able to play here without restriction.

### Millennium Park

Millennium Park offers a lot of scenic views, including the stunning city skyline. It’s a pleasant spot for walks and there are also several photography opportunities as well. When you take into account the walking paths and children’s play areas, you’ll soon see that it is a top destination for those who have kids.

### Botanical Gardens

[The Botanical Gardens] cover 14,000 square metres. The garden has a huge array of plant species, with several tropical plants and herbs. If you’ve got a keen interest in botany then this is one place you won’t want to miss out on.

### Eco-Tourism Park

[The Eco Tourism Park] spans over 480 acres. The park has a huge number of gardens and wetlands, with a huge lake. This helps to create a diverse environment for fauna and flora. On top of this, there are a number of activities that you can participate in. As a visitor, you can explore the gardens and you can also visit the butterfly area. This is home to a number of native species, so you can take in everything.

## Cultural Experiences in Kolkata

There are several cultural experiences that you can take part in if you are visiting Kolkata.

### Kolkata’s Street Food

Kolkata is known for having a huge range of street food. Some of the top options include crispy wafers, chickpeas and other savoury snacks. You can expect the food to be topped with a range of chutneys and spices, so you can make the most out of your culinary experience.

### Rabindra Sadan

[Rabindra Sadan] often hosts a number of performances, including music concerts, plays and cultural festivals. The auditorium in the area has great acoustics too, so if you want to make the most out of your time, you can do so with ease by simply going to see a show.

### Kolkata Book Fair

[The Kolkata Book Fair] is known for having a lot of different books, in many languages. Some of them include Hindi, English and Bengali. Publishers also have several stalls which offer discounts on special editions.

## Othe Popular Attractions in Kolkata

There are several different attractions you can see in Kolkata, some of them include:

### Mother House

[Mother House] is where you’ll find the tomb of Mother Teresa. Visitors can go there to pay all of their respects and it is also a place of reflection and remembrance.  It’s a small museum that helps to showcase her mission and her life too, not to mention that it covers all of the work that she did for charity.

### East Kolkata Wetlands

The East Kolkata Wetlands are home to a wide range of flora and fauna. There are also a lot of different bird species, along with many types of fish. If you are an avid bird watcher then you will love this attraction.

### Science City

One of the best things about [Science City] is that it has a lot of hands-on exhibits. They cover a number of disciplines including space science and biology. There’s also the dome theatre, which has 2D and 3D shows that revolve around science, space and nature.

### Acropolis Mall

[The Acropolis Mall] has just about everything you could ever want. There are several dining options, including cafes and casual restaurants. If you want to embrace entertainment then you will find a multiplex cinema that shows all of the latest Bollywood films.

# 18 places to visit in Kolkata that are not tourist traps
Spanning food, culture, literature and more, these are the best things to do in Kolkata
By[Konkana Ray] 
12 September 2025
![places to visit in kolkata] 
Tom Parker
In Kolkata, every façade, tramline, and[coffee] house carries the hum of debates, revolutions, and poetry. First timers may ask for the “best places to visit in Kolkata,” but the city strongly resists being reduced to a checklist. Instead, Kolkata is best met in its moods through unhurried walks, the smoky comfort of adda over cha, the sudden burst of Rabindra Sangeet spilling out of a window or a ride in its iconic, rugged yellow Ambassador taxi. As someone born here, the city is not just geography but inheritance—an eternal conversation with history, art, food, and politics. Step in with curiosity, and[Kolkata] will reveal itself in layers, like an old book too rich to skim. From timeless icons to modern gems, these are the essential places to visit in Kolkata.
#### **Sacred sites: A city of many faiths**
![18 places to visit in Kolkata that are not tourist traps] 
IndiaPictures / Getty Images
Kolkata unfolds as a palimpsest of faiths layered over centuries, each leaving behind sacred spaces that continue to define its cultural and spiritual fabric.
On the banks of the Hooghly,**Belur Math**, the headquarters of the Ramakrishna Mission, with its manicured gardens, quiet courtyards, and meditative air, is an oasis of calm. Its unique architecture, blending gopurams, arches, domes, and spires, was conceived by Swami Vivekananda to embody Sri Ramakrishna’s vision of unity and harmony. Across the river rises the**Dakshineswar Kali Temple**, built in 1855 by philanthropist Rani Rashmoni. Its Navratna(nine-spired) structure and twelve identical Shiva[temples] along the ghats epitomise Bengal’s temple style. Then there is the**Kalighat Temple**, one of the 51 Shakti Peeths, where the fierce form of Kali is worshipped. Said to mark the spot where Sati’s right toe fell, Kalighat is one of the city’s oldest shrines.**St. Paul’s Cathedral**, with its soaring spire, crenellated tower and beautiful Indo-Gothic design, is striking on Kolkata’s skyline. Built in 1847 as the first Anglican cathedral in[Asia], this colonial-era landmark remains a notable superstructure that comes alive each Christmas Eve when hundreds gather for midnight Mass. The**Magen David Synagogue**, built in 1884, recalls Kolkata’s once-flourishing Baghdadi Jewish community with its Italian Renaissance design, ornate Parisian floral pillars, stained glass windows, and gleaming chandeliers—one of the finest synagogues in[India]. Equally grand is the**Nakhoda Masjid**, built in 1926, with its red sandstone, Mughal-style domes, minarets, intricate marble work and a magnificent gateway inspired by Fatehpur Sikri’s Buland Darwaza.
#### **Victoria Memorial**
![Victoria Memorial] 
Victoria MemorialRohit Sharma
Built in 1906 by Lord Curzon to honour the deceased Queen Victoria, this luminous Makrana marble monument rises amidst manicured gardens, its elegance both timeless and deeply woven into the city’s identity. The[architecture] blends Mughal domes with British symmetry, and tinges of Egyptian, Venetian and Deccani influences, coming together in an eclectic masterpiece the city proudly claims as its own. Inside, the museum and art galleries trace Bengal’s colonial past, while outside, the sprawling gardens are the site of picnics on winter afternoons,[school] trips, couples strolling beneath bougainvillaea, and children pointing skyward at the Angel of Victory spinning with the breeze.
*Hours: Tuesday- Sunday, 10am-6pm; Closed on Mondays and national holidays.*
*Fee: Rs50 for Indians, Rs100 for SAARC nationals, Rs500 for others*
*Wheelchair accessible*
*Gardens open daily from 5:30am- 6pm. Entry Rs30 per person*
#### **Museums****and art galleries**
![Indian Museum] 
Indian MuseumRohit Sharma
Kolkata is an endlessly rewarding treasure trove for culture vultures and museum enthusiasts. At the**Indian Museum**, sprawling galleries house mummies, fossils, and much more. The 19th-century**Marble Palace**is a neoclassical gem with chandeliers, Corinthian columns, and rare Western paintings.**Netaji Bhawan**, Subhas Chandra Bose’s[family home], now chronicles his legacy with immersive exhibits. At**Jorasanko Thakurbari**, you’ll glimpse Rabindra Nath Tagore’s world through[family] manuscripts and portraits. At**Metcalfe Hall**, a Greek Revival landmark, exhibits showcase Bengal’s alpona traditions, vintage Bengali movie posters, and photographs of old and modern Kolkata. In the Alipore**Jail Museum**, century-old cells tell stories of nationalists and revolutionaries who fought for India’s freedom and were incarcerated here by the British. At the**Asiatic Society**, centuries of manuscripts, rare maps, and oriental collections await curious minds.
![TRI Art Culture] 
TRI Art &amp; CultureTRI Art &amp; Culture
For those seeking a modern frame,**TRI Art &amp; Culture**, housed in a beautifully restored 1940s triangular mansion, mixes heritage and contemporary art with experimental exhibitions and sensory programs that invite discovery. The**National[Gallery] of Modern Art**in the stately Old Currency Building showcases Indian contemporary masterpieces in an evocative setting. The**Kolkata Centre for Creativity**(KCC), a multi-disciplinary and interactive art and creativity hub, showcases collaborative expression across five floors of galleries, libraries, and art tech labs. KCC houses**Emami Art**, which blends curated exhibitions with experiential film festivals, workshops, and artist residencies—all pulsating with[South Asia] ’s sociocultural sensibilities. And**Science City**has interactive exhibits and immersive installations that bring scientific discovery into playful focus.
#### **Historic neighbourhoods, through guided walks**
![Writer&#x27;s Building] 
Writer&apos;s BuildingShivam Pandey
Wandering through Kolkata&#x27;s heritage neighbourhoods is like leafing through a living storybook.**Bow Barracks**is home to red brick barracks built as a garrison for World War I soldiers and later repurposed as Anglo-Indian residences, where the[architecture] and Christmas revelry are reminders of a community’s enduring spirit.**B.B.D. Bagh**, once called Dalhousie Square or White Town, was the former nerve centre of the British Empire in India, where colonial-era landmarks like the iconic General Post Office, Writer&#x27;s Building, and Calcutta Town Hall still stand. Similarly,**Esplanade**, with its bustling crossroads and tram tracks, anchors the city’s everyday bustle. Sombre lanes in**Bhowanipore**whisper tales of Kolkata’s intellectual rise. Meandering through**Shobhabazar**, you’ll glimpse North Kolkata’s old-world zamindar mansions and crumbling bungalows. And for sports lovers, few rituals feel more local than standing at**East Bengal**or**Mohun Bagan club grounds**, where football is pure passion. Sign up for guided walks with organisations like[Calcutta Walks],[Immersive Trails], or[Calcutta Capsule], that go beyond landmarks and draw you into the city’s architectural history, politics, and poetry.
#### **Kumartuli, the potters&#x27; quarter**
![Kumartuli] 
KumartuliMonojit Dutta
In the narrow lanes of North Kolkata’s 300-year-old Kumartuli (Kumar=potter, Tuli=locality), the air is thick with clay and colour as artisans mould river silt into life. Around 150 families live in this potters’ quarter, where gods, goddesses, demons, and even historical figures emerge from straw and earth before taking centre stage in[Bengal’s] spectacular festivals. From August to October, the neighbourhood comes alive, as skilled sculptors painstakingly layer clay, carve divine features, and paint expressive eyes that will soon adorn pandals across the city. But Kumartuli isn’t only about Durga or Kali—it’s a living studio, where myth, history, and imagination share the same narrow alleys. What makes it unmissable is the intimacy of the experience: visitors can step inside workshops, camera in hand, tip a craftsman, interact with them, or simply pause to witness the saga of creation unfold,[season] after season, generation after generation.
#### **Maidan**
![Maidan] 
MaidanShivam Pandey
A sprawling green expanse in the heart of Kolkata, Maidan, flanked by Victoria Memorial and St. Paul’s Cathedral to the south, Eden Gardens to the east, and the Hooghly’s riverbanks to the west, was first laid out in 1758 by the British as a parade ground for Fort William. Though born of colonial design, it has long since been embraced by locals: joggers, spirited football and cricket matches, picnicking[families], young couples on quiet dates, tonga rides, political rallies, and photoshoots all find place here. A tram line glides through its greens, as if to remind you of Kolkata’s timelessness. The Maidan offers not just panoramic views of the Victoria Memorial but also a chance to feel the pulse of the city—playground, stage, and memoryscape.
#### **Japanese Forest, Eco Park**
![18 places to visit in Kolkata that are not tourist traps] 
Konkana Ray
Tucked inside New Town’s vibrant Eco Park is the Japanese Forest, a meticulously designed slice of Kyoto in Kolkata. Spread over 3.5 acres next to the lake, it mirrors the architecture and philosophy of traditional[Japanese] gardens, complete with vermillion torii gates, bonsai groves, wisteria-laced walkways, koi ponds, stone lanterns, and a monastery with a pagoda. Each element is symbolic: water for purity, stone for permanence, and bamboo for resilience. Visitors can wander through serene bamboo gardens, pause at Buddha sculptures, or savour Japanese delicacies at the on-site café. Beyond this, Eco Park itself brims with numerous activities for all ages, from zorbing, duo-cycling, paddle[boating], and so on.
*Entry: Free with Eco Park’s ticket, which costs Rs30*
*Hours: 12:30pm –8:30pm, Tuesday to Sunday*
#### **Park Street**
![18 places to visit in Kolkata that are not tourist traps] 
Konkana Ray
Running from Chowringhee Road to Park Circus, Park Street has, for years, been Kolkata’s major entertainment hub. An epicentre of post-Independence nightlife, where jazz, cabaret, and continental feasts defined the city’s cosmopolitan pulse, today, there are[restaurants], clubs, and bars that stay alive well past midnight. Step into old-world dining rooms like**Mocambo**or**Peter Cat**for continental classics and vintage charm, slip into**Peter Hu**for Asian plates, or head upwards to rooftop lounges such as**LMNOQ**and**OTT**– Over The Top, where the city skyline glitters alongside your cocktail.
#### **Dacres Lane**(**James Hickey Sarani**)
Dating back to the British era, Dacres Lane (fondly called Office Para) in Esplanade has been the city’s canteen for nearly two centuries, feeding everyone from colonial sailors and clerks to modern-day hurried office-goers. The narrow stretch is a riot of smells, sounds and flavours, where**Chitto Babur Dokan**continues to reign supreme with its comforting[chicken] stew, quartered toast and crisp diamond fish fry. Just a few steps away, Arun da’s**Joy Maa Tara stall**serves the cult-favourite begun sundary (brinjal fry) and steaming khichuri. From basanti pulao with chicken kosha to ghughni, dimmer devil, Indian Chinese and more, the lane is a showcase of the city’s food heritage. The perfect finale? A tall glass of creamy[lassi] or thick rabdi from**Sharma Milk**.
#### **Iconic bookstores**
![The Story] 
The StoryThe Story
Literature is ingrained in the city’s identity.**College Street**is a 900-metre stretch in Central Kolkata lined with countless second-hand and new book stalls, including the legendary**Dasgupta &amp; Co.**, which once helped source revolutionary literature and served as a refuge for Bengal&#x27;s nationalist thinkers and freedom fighters. Located right opposite the Salt Lake stadium,**The Story**rises as a 4,000 sq ft ultra-Instagrammable[bookstore] with nearly 20,000 titles, winding staircases, and the charm of a literary salon. On Park Street,**Oxford Bookstore**, established in 1919, remains a cultural institution with its own tea boutique (Cha Bar) and regular literary events.**Earthcare Books**, tucked away on Middleton Street, specialises in environmental, ecological and alternative literature, with earthy interiors and a loyal clientele. Opened in 1982,**Seagull Books**in Bhowanipore is both a publisher and a bookstore that champions English translations, critical theory, and diverse world literature.
#### **Shopping: boutiques and markets**
![The Kantha Store] 
The Kantha StoreThe Kantha Store
Kolkata’s boutiques showcase a tapestry of tradition and creativity.**The Kantha Store**(by Farah Khan) on Loudon Street stocks exquisite hand-embroidered Kantha sarees and apparel.**Byloom**occupies a lovingly restored heritage home in Hindustan[Park] and pairs Indian handlooms like matka, tussar, khadi and linen with a cosy café for thoughtful browsing. Meanwhile, Ballygunge’s**Parama Calcutta**brings nostalgia to life through quirky stories on kantha, batik, jamdani and other textiles—think shiuli, yellow taxis, Howrah Bridge, Satyajit Ray’s dialogues, old songs, and so on. Jodhpur Park’s**The Bengal Store**curates crafts and textiles from all 23 districts of Bengal, turning each aisle into a tribute to the region’s creative roots, and**145 East**turns the humble gamcha into chic[streetwear]. At the same time, classics like**Adi Mohini Mohan Kanjilal, Adi Akshoy**and**Indian Silk House**remain go-tos for traditional Banarasi and Baluchari silk sarees that families here have trusted for generations.
For high-end luxury browsing, the**Sabyasachi flagship**located in Ballygunge is more than a store; it’s a destination in itself, its grand interiors unfolding like a[museum] of opulence. In Tollygunge, designer Abhisek Roy’s label,**Roy Calcutta**, reimagines jamdani weaves and Bengali-style dhotis into modern couture and heirlooms, carrying forward the city’s sartorial legacy. And for Bengal’s handlooms—Dhaniakhali, Dhakai, Tangail, taant, or muslin—head to**Dakshinapan**and**Biswa Bangla Haat,**both treasure troves of art. To taste the city’s chaos and colour, nothing beats the buzz of street shopping for native gems at**New Market, Gariahat,**and**Hatibagan**, where bargaining is an art form.
#### **Hooghly riverfront: a city by the water**
![Howrah Bridge] 
Howrah BridgeGautam Ganguly / Unsplash
The Hooghly has always defined Kolkata’s rhythms. Head to**Prinsep Ghat**, a neoclassical Palladian porch built in 1843 to honour British scholar James Prinsep, for the best view of the Vidyasagar Setu, and a tranquil boat ride at sunrise or sunset, made better with phuchka, chaat or pav bhaji from the local stalls. The[riverfront] promenade, with its Corinthian columns and Gothic-Greek inlays, remains one of the city’s most romantic riverside landmarks.**Outram Ghat**is a quieter spot, a 19th-century colonial port and present-day major-immersion site, a spot for both history and ritual. Venture north to ghats like**Kashi Mitra**,**Bagbazar**and**Sobhabazar**, where life still lingers in slow motion over chaa, jhal muri and[riverside] adda. For the spiritually inclined, attending the mesmerising**Ganga Aarti**at**Babughat**is a must.
Cafés like Bageecha and Dockyard Co. offer coffee against riverfront sunsets. Stroll across the iconic**Howrah Bridge**, then drift along an evening cruise or the newly launched[water] metro, experiencing Kolkata from its most cinematic vantage points, gliding along the Hooghly.
#### **Cabin culture and street eats**
![Anadi Cabin] 
Anadi CabinBiswarup Ganguly
Born in the early 20th century as modest eateries with wooden benches and frosted glass, Kolkata’s cabins became the cradle of comfort food. Do not miss**Anadi Cabin**and its legendary Mughlai paratha, or**Allen Kitchen**for golden prawn cutlets. Queue at**Golbari**for a plate of rich, spice-laden[mutton] kosha or**Neelachal**for soulful fish kachoris.**Mitra Café**is an old-school spot for mutton cutlets and kabiraji, and**Lakshmi Narayan Shaw and Sons**for telebhaja. If biryani is your calling,**Arsalan**, Park Circus, delivers Calcutta-style perfection with aloo and eggs. Street food lovers swear by**Dilip-da’s phuchka**,**Kusum’s**kathi rolls, and**Maharaj**’s unbeatable kachori–jilipi combo.
#### **Cafes and bakeries**
![Roastery Coffee House] 
Roastery Coffee HouseKonkana Ray
Amid the lively café culture of Hindustan Park is the visually striking**Roastery Coffee House,**where yellow-and-white exteriors, chequered floors, and leafy courtyards meet the warmth of homely Bengali interiors, evoking the leisurely charm of old Calcutta. Try Continental classics, ice cream blended cold coffees, and its signature cranberry[coffee]. With its heritage appeal, Kalighat’s**Red Bari**delivers a slice of vintage Bengal in a restored 90-year-old Bengali mansion with a striking red exterior, teak lattice windows and Subko speciality brews.**Colab Coffee**in Jodhpur Park, set in a restored home with minimal rustic interiors and a cosy courtyard where old trees glow with cane lamps, pairs artisanal brews with comforting Italian plates. Its soulful, eco-conscious vibe echoes Kolkata’s timeless tradition of turning everyday spaces into hubs of adda and creativity.
At**8th Day**, hearty American-style fare meets work-friendly ease. Known for excellent brews and healthy meal options, it’s a go-to for both productivity and comfort. Salt Lake’s**Calcutta 64**offers a boutique feel with comforting burgers, wraps, and fish and chips at prices that don’t pinch. Then there’s**Flurys**, the grand dame of Park Street. Since 1927, this iconic[tearoom] has defined Kolkata’s café culture with elegant pastries, pastel-hued interiors, and praiseworthy breakfast options. And finally, no café map of Kolkata would be complete without the**Indian Coffee House**on[College] Street, where poets, politicians, and students have kept the adda spirit alive for decades over cold coffee, cutlets, and endless chatter.
For those who love classic old-school bakeries,**Nahoum &amp; Sons**, founded in 1902 by a Baghdadi Jew, is the city’s most beloved heritage spot. Its teakwood counters and glass cases still brim with plum cakes, rum balls, strawberry cake, and[cheese] puffs.**Saldanha Bakery**, often voted the city’s top pastry stop, is famed for its fruit cake, walnut cake and Christmas specials. And since 1985,**Kookie Jar**has been synonymous with bespoke cakes and breads, making every occasion memorable.
#### **Iconic****Bengali restaurants**
![Ballygunge Place] 
Ballygunge Place6 Ballygunge Place
For purists,**6 Ballygunge Place**remains a favourite for gourmet Bengali dining, known for festive classics like Daab Chingri, Bhapa Ilish, Bhetki Paturi, and Chitol Macher Muitha.**Bhooter Raja Dilo Bor**, themed after Satyajit Ray’s*Goopi Gyne Bagha Byne*, offers indulgent Bengali cuisine on bronze dinnerware—try the Gupir or Baghar[Thali], Sundi Rajar Thali or Dhonepata Kachalonka Chicken.**Ilish Truly Bong**is the go-to for hilsa lovers, famed for Ilish Paturi, Ilish Bhapa, Daab Ilish, and the luxurious Ilish-E-Mahabhoj Thali.**Saptapadi**is a favourite for its slow-cooked mutton dishes like Mangsho Sipahi and Abhinaba Mangsho, while**Kasturi**offers homestyle fare with the warmth of a Bengali kitchen; their fish thalis and bhetki dishes are hot favorites.
![Sienna] 
SiennaSienna Calcutta
**Calcutta Pi**’s killer Ilish Fingers, Mutton Kacha Kebab and Chingri Bora are both comforting and creative in equal measure. The chic**Sienna Store &amp; Café**follows a &quot;Bengal-to-Table&quot; philosophy, delivering creative Bengali fusion dishes like Mocha’r Salad and Lotus Root[Chicken] Meatball in a chic setting.**Bonne Femme**experiments with fusion plates like Mutton Kosha Bao, Nolen Gur Gondhoraj Som Tam, Crab Kulcha, Gondhoraj Chicken Stroganoff and the unmatchable Chicken Baked Rosogolla (sweet, soft rosogollas baked with a savoury[chicken] stuffing) that reimagine tradition without losing the Bengali soul.
#### **Cocktail bars**
![Nutcase] 
NutcaseAnik Dutta
![Nutcase] 
NutcaseAnik Dutta
In recent years, Kolkata’s nightlife, once concentrated to Park Street’s storied venues, has ventured into inventive cocktail bars.**AMPM**reimagines the café-bar hybrid with Gatsby-era flair. Try the Before PM Martini with spicy Bengali chilli, tequila, and espresso, or the Park Street Negroni, where coffee-infused[whisky] meets citrus sass.**Little Bit Sober**occupies a restored 1890s mansion, its deep ruby velvet interiors and speakeasy vibe drenched in theatrical appeal. Its “Poet Edition” cocktails borrow Bengali pantry favourites—think Gondhoraj Cocktail or Nolen Coffee—each described with miniature verse and crafted with techniques like fat washing and barrel ageing. The newly launched**ATM Members Bar &amp; Kitchen**serves a Victorian-style supper-club atmosphere— flickering chandeliers, butler service, and[cocktails] named after local traditions, including The Masked Dance, inspired by Chhau dance, and Potter’s Tradition, served in a terracotta glass that nods to Panchmura pottery. Beyond these, the city’s[cocktail] circuit has welcomed other imaginative spaces like**Cal-On**,**Manthan Songhai**,**Olterra**, and**Sorano**, each bringing mixology, music, or mood-forward design into its own narrative.**Nutcase Etc.**is a bold playground for offbeat concoctions like Tangra Town (tequila with chicken broth) and Sushi? (vodka blended with sake, mirin, and nori), where experimentation is the name of the game.
#### **Live music locations**
![Skinny Mo&#x27;s Jazz Club] 
Skinny Mo&apos;s Jazz ClubMargub Ali
From its iconic retro cabaret venues to newer, intimate performance spaces, Kolkata’s love of music is woven into the very fabric of the city.**Trincas**on Park Street remains the undisputed classic—since the 1960s, it has hosted jazz, blues, and retro nights that continue to draw in loyalists.**Skinny Mo’s Jazz[Club] **, housed in a restored 1920s Art Deco building in Kalighat, has quickly become the go-to for purists, with its dedicated jazz evenings and jam sessions.**Chapter 2**in Southern Avenue is a nostalgic nod to the Swinging Sixties, where old-school rock ‘n’ roll set the tone. For a more contemporary fix,**Someplace Else**at The Park Hotel is a cult favourite, famous for its packed gigs that champion indie and alternative rock.**TopCat CCU**, meanwhile, has emerged as a hub for experimental performances, from metal to electronic. And then there’s**Pancham er Adday**, a quirky RD Burman-themed[café] where live-music evenings turn into informal sing-alongs, keeping Kolkata’s musical heart as timeless as ever.
#### **Classic Bengali sweet shops**
![Image may contain Food Food Presentation Bread Plate and Egg] 
Sweets by Girish NakurPoorna Banerjee
![Image may contain Food Food Presentation Plate Cream Dessert Frozen Yogurt Bread Meal and Sweets] 
Roshogolla by KC DasPoorna Banerjee
In Kolkata, every[neighbourhood] has its favourite sweet shop, and locals will passionately defend their chosen mishti’r dokan. Start with**Chittaranjan Mistanna Bhandar**, where the Roshogolla is legendary—pillowy, syrup-soaked, and unchanged across generations. For the city’s finest Sandesh, the verdict tilts towards**Girish Chandra Dey &amp; Nakur Chandra Nandy**, a 19th-century establishment still handcrafting delicate, melt-in-the-mouth varieties. They’ve given Sandesh a playful twist with flavours ranging from jol bhora,[chocolate], parijat, jackfruit, to black currant.**Balaram Mullick &amp; Radharaman Mullick**take tradition forward with modern flair, with Baked Roshogolla, Baked Mihidana, Bhapa Sandesh, Mango Malai Chamcham, and Chhanar Murki as perennial favourites. Then there’s**K.C. Das**, credited with popularising the roshogolla and for creating the roshomalai or rasmalai, with its flagship at Esplanade drawing a perpetual crowd. Old-timers swear by**Putiram**, a College Street legend beloved for its old-world charm and classics like Rajbhog, Kamalabhog, Pantua and Lyangcha. For the most indulgent melt-in-the-mouth Gulab Jamuns, head to**Haji Allauddin[Sweets] **, a Mughlai touch to the Bengali palate. And to end on a creamy note, nothing quite matches the mishti doi at**Amrit**.
