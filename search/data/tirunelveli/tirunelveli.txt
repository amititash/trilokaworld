I have lived here for 1 year during 2006 .My father worked in Papanasam powerhouse and we live in a quarters in lower camp.People usually come here to visit agasthiyar falls which is a good one.Still upper there is vana pechi Amman temple , lower Dam, servallar powerhouse and dam, sorimuthu aiyanar koil and river and the uppermost place is karayar dam( thamirabharani dam). I was fortunate that I was not attacked by it.During day time soft animals like langur, peacock,blackbuck ,deer etc will come. The predators will come out mostly during dawn and dusk. 6PM is the time for leopard. Black bears have no specific time one can encounter it any time.During 2006 I have never heard any predator attacking human . They will attack cattle. Now the tiger population has increased manyfold and one day a tiger came and sat in vanapechi Amman temple during mid-afternoon in front of a big crowd performing temple festival. So I guess the tigers became fearless now.Servallar is a place with red coloured wild dogs which are bit dangerous and roam in packs of atleast 4. # Places to Visit in Tirunelveli

Tirunelveli, in the southern Indian state of Tamil Nadu, is a popular vacation spot. Because of its uniqueness, the region has a wide variety of landscapes, from mountains to beaches, fields to forests, rivers to lakes, lakes to low plains, and plains to woodlands. The attractions in Tirunelveli that have earned their status as "must-sees" provide the whole gamut of vacationer favourites. Nature lovers, history aficionados, pilgrims, and everyone else may all find something to enjoy in Tirunelveli.

[**District Science Centre**] 

##### An Insightful Tourist Site

District Science Centre is a museum in Palayamkottai, Tirunelveli town, Tamil Nadu state, located around 1.5 km from Tirunelveli Railway Station. One of India's 124 scientific centres, it is a member of the National Council of Science Museums. There are now four exhibition halls within the Centre, dedicated to the themes of "Fun Science," "Popular Science,Electronics," and a "New Wing of Electronics.

2 to 3 hours

[**Kalugumalai**] 

##### A Stunning Site with Several Sculptures

Kalugumalai, which translates to "Hill of the Vulture," is the inspiration for the name of the town. According to legend, this was a significant Jain community where Lord Mahavir preached and was revered by the locals. Ancient traders used the area now known as South Kazhugumalai as a passageway between the two halves of Kazhugumalai.

[**Thirukkurungudi**] 

##### A Sacred Site Close to Nambi River

Thirukkurungudi, in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu, is revered by Hindus as a sacred site due to its location on the Nambi River and close proximity to the Mahendragiri Hills. This Kshetram contains five Nambis. Each of these postures has its own name: Ninra Nambi (the standing position), Irundha Nambi (the sitting posture), Kidandha Nambi (the sleeping posture), Thiruparkadal Nambi, and Thirumalai Nambi. The Ninra Nambi Temple is the town's primary place of worship, and it is framed by two sets of twin towering Mada Veethis.

Banatheertham Falls, with its stunning white water wall, is a natural wonder that should be seen and celebrated for all that it is. The Banatheertham Falls are a stunning white waterfall that plunges through a verdant jungle in the Western Ghats.

[**Kattabomman Memorial Fort**] 

##### Historically Significant Site

The Kattabomman Memorial Fort was erected by the government of Tamil Nadu in honour of Veerapandiya Kattabomma Karuthayya Nayakkar, a freedom warrior (also known as Kattabomman). He was a powerful leader in the 18th century who refused to accept British East India Company rule and led his army into battle. After his arrest, he was given the death penalty.

[**Papanasam**]

# Tirunelveli Travel Guide

Seated on the west bank of Thamirabarani River, Tirunelveli is an important destination in Tamil Nadu. Like many other tourist places in the state, Tirunelveli also boasts some impeccable Hindu temples as attractions and pilgrimage destinations. Tirunelveli's history dates back to 2000 year ago, and thus, the city is inarguably one of the best places to trace the stories of various dynasties and its legends. The major attraction of this sixth largest city in the state, is the Kanthimathi - Nellaiappar Temple, which stands in the centre of this ideal pilgrimage spot and boasts the largest Shiva Temple in Tamil Nadu. Though the Hindu shrines make the top attractions in the city and around it, there also exist a Science Centre for those seeking to explore something different. Tirunelveli also has a gourmet treat for you in the form of Halwa, for which the city has been famous since 1800s. Yes, the melt-in-the-mouth delight is a must-try and has to be taken home as a souvenir for friends and family.

Apart from this, Tirunelveli's twin city, seated just opposite to it on the east bank of Thamirabarani gives you a glimpse of the elements of the modern era. Earlier known as the Oxford of India, Palayamkottai is home to numerous colleges and educational centres. Not only this, this city is famed as the venue for several national-level sports in India. Other than this, Tirunelveli's surroundings boasts of more Hindu temples, two dams - Manimuthar and Papanasam, Ariakulam and Koonthakulam Bird Sanctuaries, Manjolai and Upper Kodaiyar. Some odd 59 km from Tirunelveli, one also has the opportunity to sight breathtaking waterfalls in Courtallam/Kutralam which are said to have medicinal or healing properties. Our insightful Tirunelveli travel guide endeavours to offer you detailed information about this worth seeing city in Tamil Nadu. Find useful insights on top attractions, means of transportation, best time to visit and accommodation.

## Popular Tourist Attractions In & Around Tirunelveli

Tirunelveli tourism is not only confined to temples, even though the sacred places dot the city and its nearby areas significantly. There are also some scenic destinations near Tirunelveli where a nature lover would love to visit. Waterfalls, dams and wildlife sanctuaries all make the holiday in Tirunelveli unlike the other.

- ![Kanthimathi-Nellaiyappar Temple] 



#### Kanthimathi-Nellaiyappar Temple



The most important Hindu shrine in Tiruvelneli, Kanthimathi-Nellaiyappar is a twin temple which is dedicated to Goddess Parvati and Lord Siva. The temple houses the famous Musical Pillars which produces sounds in varied pitches.

- ![Krishnapuram Temple] 



#### Krishnapuram Temple



Located 10 km from Tirunelveli, Krishnapuram Temple is a Dravidian style shrine built by the Nayak and Vijayanagara kings in 16th century. The temple is dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Thenkalai tradition of worship is followed here.

- [![Kutralam]] 




#### Kutralam



Nestled in the Western Ghats, Kutralam is a popular tourist destination near Tirunelveli for its waterfalls which are believed to possess medicinal properties. The place is perfect for the nature lovers and is truly a feast to the eyes. [Read more] 

- [![Thoothukudi]] 


#### Thoothukudi



Some 46 km away from Tirunelveli, Thoothukudi is a port city which is also famous for pearl fishing. The major tourist attraction in Thoothukudi is the bakery made, macaroon. A Shiva temple along with a church also make important places to visit in Thoothukudi. [Read more] 

- [![Mundanthurai Wildlife Sanctuary]] 




#### Mundanthurai Wildlife Sanctuary



An important wildlife destination in Tirunelveli, Mundanthurai Wildlife Sanctuary is home to a plethora of animals like Tiger, Sloth Bear, Leopard, Rusty spotted cat, Brown palm civet and many more animals. The sanctuary is also an ideal place for birdwatching. [Read more] 

- ![Panchalankuruchi] 



#### Panchalankuruchi



A small historic village, some 54 km from Tirunelveli, Panchalankuruchi is home to the freedom fighter Veerapandia Kattabomman. The village is famed for a memorial fort dedicated to Veerapandia Kattabomman, and it also houses the temple of Devi Jakkamma, the hereditary Goddess of Kattabomman family.

- ![Kalakadu Wildlife Sanctuary] 



#### Kalakadu Wildlife Sanctuary



Home to the rare lion-tailed macaques, Kalakadu Wildlife Sanctuary is situated some 47 km from Tirunelveli. The wildlife sanctuary also houses a beautiful natural spring, Kudirai Mozhitheri and several species of wildlife animals including tigers.

- ![Sankaranarayanan Koil] 



#### Sankaranarayanan Koil



Situated in Sankaranarayanan Kovil town, the famous shrine is said to date back to AD 900 and houses a deity that is a meld of Lord Shiva and Vishnu. The temple also has an impressive dome that is 135 m high and is 9-tiered.

- ![Papanasam] 



#### Papanasam



Some odd 60 km from Tirunelveli, Papanasam is a picturesque destination that makes a great picnic spot on family vacations. This travel place in Tamil Nadu offers a visual treat of meandering Thamirabarani River, foaming Agasthiyar Falls, majestic Papanasam Dam and a sacred Shiva Temple.

## About Tirunelveli

**Tirunelveli**, the most important district in Southern Tamil Nadu has enormous footfall of tourists from across the world. The twin cities _Tirunelveli_ and _Palayamkottai_ are separated by a river “ _Thamarabharani_”. The city played major part in opposing British Raj in Tamil Nadu. The city was ruled by Cholas, Pandyas, Nayakar, Vijayanagar Empire and Vijayanagar Empire in ancient time. Tirunelveli is also a hub for many educational institutes.

Hotels like _Appletree, Sri Janakiram, Sakunthala International,_ etc. provides tourists with pleasant accomodation at fair rates. ### How can I commute within Tirunelveli?

Being a major city of Tamil Nadu, Tirunelveli does not have any problems when it comes to intra city commuting. Taxis and rickshaws are very easy to find. local market and street food.

According to a legend, Lord Shiva (as Vrihivriteswara) is believed to have descended in the form of a hedge and roof to save the paddy crop of a devotee. The more we dig deeper into this amazing city situated in South Tamil Nadu, the more we get closer to the history, culture and development of the region. So, if you are also curious to learn more, the places to visit in Tirunelveli offer a glimpse into the architecture, spirituality, culture and natural beauty of the region. Best Time to Visit Tirunelveli Located on the west bank of the Tamirabarani River, Tirunelveli has a hot semi-arid climate. The average temperature during summer (March to June) ranges between 25 degrees Celsius to 41 degrees Celsius. If you wish to travel the city during these months, then you can explore the tourist attractions early in the morning or late in the evening. Tirunelveli The monsoon season (July to September) is accompanied by occasional to heavy rains, adding a lush green touch to the landscape. Although sometimes during the peak monsoon, the rise in water level of the Thamirabarani River may cause flooding in the nearby areas, the early monsoon is perfect to enjoy light showers and soak in the beauty of the places to visit in Tirunelveli. Winter season (October to February) brings relief from the hot and humid weather and makes it the best time to visit Tirunelveli. The season is not as chilly as in northern India. But it is pleasant for sightseeing during the day. How to Reach Tirunelveli Tirunelveli is 624 kms away from the state capital Chennai, 58 kms away from Thoothukudi and 73 kms away from Kanyakumari. Being a major industrial city, it has excellent connectivity to the neighbouring cities and states via road, rail and air.  By Road The city is located on National Highway no.7 and is well-connected to major cities like Chennai, Kochi, Coimbatore, Bangalore, Salem and Pondicherry. You can reach the city by self-driving, hiring a taxi or taking a state and privately owned bus to the Veinthankulam New Bus Stand, Tirunelveli. If you are planning a stopover in Kochi, there are plenty of hotels in Kochi ready to host you. However, if you are looking for a halt in between the journey, you can easily find affordable hotels in Chennai, Kochi and other major cities on the way to Tirunelevi. By Train  The Tirunelveli Junction Railway Station is a significant railway station of the state and is extremely busy. The station connects the city to major cities in the country and the state. Tirvenelveli Junction Various express and super fast trains from cities like Chennai, Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi and Kolkata pass through Tirunelveli. By Air Tuticorin Airport, 41 kms away from the city, is the nearest airport to Tirunelveli. But, since there are only a few flights to the airport, travellers prefer to take a flight either to Trivandrum Airport, at 150 kms or Madurai International Airport, at 170 kms. From there, you can either take a train, a bus or a taxi to reach various places to visit in Tirunelveli. If you are flying by Trivandrum Airport, you can stopover at some of the best hotels in Thiruvananthapuram. However, if you are flying through the Madurai International Airport, then here too you will find plenty of great hotels in Madurai for a pleasant stay. Now that we have explored the best time and ways to reach Tirunelveli, let’s explore some of the best places to visit in Tirunelveli, which include ancient temples, churches, waterfalls, forts and dams. 1. Kanthimathi and Nellaiappar Temple One of the places to visit in Tirunelveli, which you cannot skip even on a business trip to the city, is the twin temples of Kanthimathi and Nellaiappar. Kanthimathi Temple is dedicated to Goddess Parvati, and Nellaippar Temple to Lord Shiva. Elephant in Kanthimathi Temple These ancient temples are the epitome of the vibrant and colourful architecture of the region and include various interesting sites. These sites include the Golden Lily Tank, a hall with 1000 pillars that create musical notes when you strike them at various places. Another significant site is the Golden Chariot, which is the third largest in the country. 2. Krishnapuram Temple Another significant religious site in Tirunelveli is Krishnapuram Venkatachalapathy Temple, also known as Krishnapuram Temple. Located 10 kms from the city centre, the temple is dedicated to Lord Vishnu. The temple is not only popular for its spirituality, but is also known for the Dravidian style of architecture, making it one of the best places to visit in Tirunelveli for history enthusiasts. Krishnapuram Temple Another interesting thing about the temple is the Thenkallai tradition of worship, which you can see during the four daily rituals and three annual festivals. Out of which, the ten-day Vaikunta Ekadashi during December-January attracts a large number of devotees. 3. Kappal Matha Church Located around 70 kms from the city, Kappal Matha Charch is one of the unique places to visit in Tirunelveli. This boat-shaped church is dedicated to St. Mary and is situated by the seashore in Uvari, a fishing town. The earlier constructed church was damaged by the sea erosion and the locals then decided to build the new one in the form of a seaplane. Kappal Matha Charch This magnificent piece of architecture overlooking the majestic sea attracts a large number of devotees and tourists, making it a must-visit place in the city.  4. Kattabomman Memorial Fort Built in the memory of the Freedom fighter Veerapandiya Kattabomma Karuthayya Nayakkar (also known as Kattabomman) by the Tamil Nadu Government, Kattabomman Memorial Fort is one of the popular tourist places to visit in Tirunelveli. The veteran was an 18th-century chieftain who refused the predominance of the British East India Company and waged war against them. During the war, he was captured and sentenced to the death penalty. The beautiful memorial was built on the site where he was hanged to death. 5. Papanasam Falls and Banatheertham Falls The twin falls located in the enchanting rainforests of the Western Ghats, Papanasam Falls and Banatheertham Falls, are the best places to visit in Tirunveli for adventure and nature lovers. According to the locals, taking a dip in the Papanasam Falls can absolve one of all their sins and is hence named as Papanasam Falls, which means removal of all sins. It is also believed that the waterfalls possess unique herbal and medicinal properties, and the water feels like a soothing oil massage.  Papanasam Falls On the other hand, the Banatheertham Falls became popular after the famous Hindi movie Roja was shot here and the falls received their name as Roja Falls. The waterfall is situated just over the Karaiyar Dam on the Tamirabarani River and is accessible through a boat ride and a ten-minute trek afterwards. The best time to visit these waterfalls is from December to January, when the humidity is low. 6. Courtallam Falls One of the best waterfalls in India, the Courtallam Falls is located within a small panchayat town and originates from the Chittar River in the Western Ghats. This 60-meter waterfall remains a popular tourist attraction throughout the year. The water of the falls is believed to have healing powers and is often visited by locals and travellers.  If you are an adventure seeker, there are various other waterfalls, including Shenbaga Falls and Honey Falls, just a short trek away. 7. Vettuvan Koil The sculptor’s paradise, Vettuvan Koil, is an unfinished temple made from carved rocks. Built in the 8th-9th century, it reflects the Pandya-architecture style through its carvings. Only the top portion of the temple is completed, which has mesmerising sculptures of Uma Maheswarar, Dakshinamoorthy, Vishnu, Brahma and other lords. Vettuvan Koil The temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva, is the only fine example of a Pandya-era monolithic temple carved out in three dimensions. So, if you are an archaeological enthusiast, this temple is a must-visit for you. 8. Krishnapuram Anjaneyar Temple Dedicated to Lord Hanuman, the Krishnapuram Anjaneyar Temple is associated with the legend of Ramayana and other legends of Lord Hanuman. It is believed that visiting the temple and worshipping the lord helps devotees to get rid of all the obstacles and keep them safe. According the legend, after being crowned as the king of Kishkinta with the help of Lord Ram, Sugriva sends his force in all directions on the mission of locating the whereabouts of Goddess Sita. The team that went down South was headed by Sri Angath, ably assisted by Sri Jambavan and Sri Anjaney (lord Hanuman). Lord Ram, who had confidence in Sri Anjaney, had entrusted him with a ring that was to be given to Sita. 9. Karaiyar Dam An ode to the human spirit of innovation, the Karaiyar Dam is not just a source of water and power but a testament to human engineering. Also called the Papanasam Dam, it is surrounded by lush greenery and cascading waterfalls, making it one of the best tourist places to visit in Tirunelveli. The dam stands 44 meters tall and is spread over a length of 227 meters, generating a total capacity of 32 MW. The location is perfect for enjoying a relaxing time while soaking in the breathtaking views of the surroundings. You can boat rides, birdwatching or embark on a trek towards the Banatheertham Falls for breath-taking views of the falls. So, now that you know about this incredible place in South India, don’t miss a chance to get a first-hand experience of these places and soak in the beauty of nature. Also, don’t forget to indulge yourself in the local delicacies, which include Aviyal, a famous food of Tamil Nadu.

## Tirunelveli District

### A bustling urban centre, that emanates the richness of its glorious past; that of the rise and fall of kingdoms, of its cultural lushness and heritage, leading up to its rise as one of the most important tourism destinations in Tamil Nadu.

Nestling peacefully on the banks of River Thamirabharani, Tirunelveli is the sixth largest city in Tamil Nadu. A city that reflects the grandeur of its amazing history, Tirunelveli has for a traveller many special treats and delights. Topping it are an array of temples that boast of the legacy left by the great Dravidian architectural genre. Works of art and masterpieces themselves, these temples are not just places of divine worship, but also architectural wonders.

Over 2000 years old, Tirunelveli has witnessed many decisive moments in history. Story of the etymological roots of the name Tirunelveli is fascinating as well. Legend has it that Vedasarma, an ardent devotee of Lord Siva had sown some paddy and went to the river. The region was affected by heavy rain soon, but in spite of that, the paddy did not submerge in water. Hence this place started to be addressed as “Tiru-nel-veli” (Divinely protected paddy). Through the course of history, the area has been under the dominion of various empires including the Pandyas, Cholas, Madurai Nayaks, Chanda Sahib and eventually the British. The legacy of these empires can be seen and experienced in every nook and corner of the district to this day.

A city of rich traditions, Tirunelveli is today a municipal corporation with an urban face. Not to mention the special gourmet treat known as ‘Halwa’, a melt-in-the-mouth sweet dish that is bought and taken back by people who visit the city. The city is also a venue for textile shopping and many other commercial activities.

Some general tips -

Apart from heat, it is election day on 19 April. So plan accordingly. I would suggest to go after 19 April to avoid issues.

Last year end floods destroyed many roads in the entire belt. So expect roads broken except GQ.

Kanyakumari - Explore Mathur thotti Palam if interested. I don't think there will be water in Thirparappu falls and if it is there it will be crowded. Chitral Jain caves are nearby. Apart from Padmanabapuram palace, this is the usual tourist circuit. For temples, Susindharam Thanumalaya swamy temple, Nagercoil Nagaraja Swamy temple and Thiruvattar temple is good. There is a small river front before Thiruvattar temple which is nice.

Anantya by lake is a nice resort as you booked for stay and is in rubber estate and it will be quite hot. Vaikundham legacy next is same. If you want to stay relatively remote and desolate, Indian hermitage Marungur is decent.

Tirunelveli-Tenkasi: Janakiram is the best place to stay in junction. Try Sodhi meals if on Fridays. After the bus stand is shifted it is relatively less congested.

As others told, Manimutharu and Manjolai estate is open but as per whims of forest office. There is no place to stay in estate. Only kuthirai vetti has a KMTR dorm which can be done only by Jeep or Thar not suitable for family stay. Anyway now nobody is there. Upper estate people vacated it few years ago. You can go beyond Kakkachi and upto Nalu mukku if road is repaired though permission is till Manjolai only. Ensure you order lunch by getting the mobile number of food joint in the higher reach at Manimuthar check post itself. Have pollution control certificate handy to show. Road is very narrow so check bus timing as in some places you cannot even give way. It will be a lot hot in summer. Upper kodaiyar is EB controlled and they have guest house. Unless you have contacts, one cannot go there.

Papanasam has only basic lodges. Vijay lodge is okay. I suggest to stay in Ambasamudram like Ambai grand hotel or home stay in Ambai or Kallidai or Surandai and do day trips to Papanasam, etc. Don't expect any tourism facilities like in Kerala. Karaiyar is controlled by TNEB and cannot be visited without contacts. Banatheertham is also closed. Agasthiyar falls is open.

Paddy crop is just cut, so expect fields to be brown in the district. I am just back from there last week. If interested in Sunflower farms, visit Sundarapandiapuram or Sambavar Vadakarai. Not sure of flower season. If you go near Sengottai border, visit Achan kovil, Kumba urutti falls and Kundaru dam on TN side.

Manjolai is doable with permission from Forest Office in Ambasamudram. They open at 10am and permits are issued First Come First Serve basis. You need to carry copies of aadhaars of all along with RC copy. You have to fill a permission form and produce with the copies and the slip will be issued immediately. But do note that roads aren't in great shape.

If you don't want to go to Manjolai, but only to Manimutharu falls, then this permission can betaken from the entry checkpost to Manimutharu.

Mundanthurai safaris are open now after closing for tiger census last month. There is no advance booking as far as I know.

I have converted our 100 year old house to a homestay in Alwarkurichi in the vicinity of Ambasamudram. [https://theagrahara.in].

I can provide more specific details if you need. Like others mentioned, this is typical summer season there. So not the best time to view the natural beauty of the place. But this is the case pretty much across South. There are a lot of unique temples you can visit around, if you are into that.

I did the trip successfully and it was enjoyable despite the heat ( which peaked at 43 degrees near Kovilpatti ).

Day 0: Started at 7pm from Whitefield. 11pm Salem.

Day 1: Salem - Ettaiyapuram (Bharatiyar house, Ettappa Palace, Kali temple, Muthuswamy Dikshitar memorial ) - Panchalankurichi ( Veerapandia Kattabomman fort ) - Ottapidaram ( VOC house ) - Arumuganeri ( in search of the famous Kattabomman bells ) - Tiruchendur. The stretch between Peikulam to Ambedkar Nagar and thereabouts is so lush green, thanks to the perennial Tamiraparani.

Day 2: Tiruchendur temple - Theri Kadu Dunes - Kanyakumari.

Day 3 : Kanyakumari - Suchindram - Aralvaimozhi windmill road - Nagercoil temple - Padmanabhapuram - Anantya

Day 4 : Anantya - Neyyar - Anantya - Chittar 1 - Chittar 2 dam - Lower Kodhaiyar Dam ( Denied permission ) - Pechiparai dam ( visited the dam and the backwater which is accessible via the CSI church road : you can actually take a dip in the water and there is quite a bit of water - in fact, nearly full ) - Perunchani dam ( had to request many times before they let us in ) - Anantya.

a) At Pechiparai checkpost, they didn't let us go further to Kodhaiyar. They said that entry was allowed only when the Kodhaiyar falls has water. Despite clear boards that vehicles will be allowed at a charge, I was not allowed. Returned without being able to visit.

b) Likewise, near Pechiparai Police station, the gate was closed. I was hoping to drive up to a village called Thottamalai the road for which would go along the backwater, but the gate was closed. They said that we could walk a bit to get some good views or ask someone to open the gate ( the person was not found and I didn't want to invent excuses to venture into a cordoned off area )

c) The good thing is that there are Govt buses to all these places : Pechiparai, Keeriparai, Kothaiyar and at each place I could see the buses either going to the destination or returning.

Day 5 : Anantya - Thirupparappu falls ( absolutely nothing right now ) - Thadikkarankonam - Kalikesam temple / river ( Kalikesam entry closes by 3pm, opens at 9 am ). There was some greenery and the river still gurgled gently along. Was a good experience; then took the Andithoppu road to Aralvaimozhi ( Boy, what a wonderful scenic road ) and then to Tirunelveli (Iruttukadai Halwa and Temple ). Took an auto from the hotel to temple and on the return leg, the kind auto driver showed us important landmarks in the city ( e.g, the double decker bridge from the 70s, Sulochanamudaliar bridge and the story behind it, Vanchai Vilas eatery etc ).
