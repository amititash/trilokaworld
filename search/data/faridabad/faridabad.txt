## Dhyankaksh, the school of equanimity and even sightedness

Recognizing the architectural exquisiteness and spiritual significance of Dhyankaksh, also known as School of Equanimity and Even- Sightedness,  the Tourism Department of the Govt. of Haryana has included it in the list of tourist spots of Faridabad. The picturesque “Dhyankaksh” is situated in the Vasundhara campus of the Satyug Darshan Trust, village Bhopani – Lalpur Road, Greater Faridabad.
The news has also been declared on the official website of the Govt. of Haryana i.e. [https://haryanatourism.gov.in] /. Besides this incredible “Dhyankaksh” other places that have been included in the list of tourist spots of Faridabad are Anandpur Dam, Aravali Golf Course, Gymkhana club, Mughal Bridge, Nahar Singh Cricket Stadium, Rajhans Convention Center, Rose Garden, and Surajkund. A haven of peace and serenity, “Dhyankaksh” sprawls over an area of 10 acres in the Vasundhara Campus of the Satyug Darshan Trust, Faridabad. Also known as “Sambhav-Samdrishti Ka School”, the architectural marvel adorned with white marble double domes was inaugurated on 26th January 2014. The internal roofs of the domes are all plated with gold, and the room is surrounded by alluring water bodies and lawns. Entry to the “Dhyankaksh” is free and facilities such as cafeteria, eating joints, parking are also available. Many renowned personalities viz. former president of India Late Dr. Abul Kalam Azad, ex-Governor of Hariyana, Shri Kaptan Singh Solanki, former diplomat Dr. Karn Singh and the likes have paid a visit to this treasure trove of tranquility. Anyone who is seeking peace and serenity can enter Dhyankaksh, regardless of religion, caste, creed, richness-poverty, gender or color, etc. Before entering into Dhyankaksh, one has to pass through the seven beautiful, larger than life gates, which are symbols of seven core Human Values, i.e. contentment endurance, truthfulness, righteousness, equality, selflessness, and benevolence. This “Dhyankaksh” has been adorned to mark the existence of Satyug and the pride of humanity. The disciples, here, are taught about the powers of spiritual knowledge and virtues to be able to stay aligned with the divinity in mind and let the body fulfill its due obligations in the mortal world. This helps them to preserve their spiritual stamina while struggling with the hardships of the physical arena and they can lead a life of a solemn person with faith in the divinity. People come from remote places to look at this beautiful reflection of unique architecture, and several cultural programs are organized throughout the year on the campus. Thousands of devotees visit the place on the occasion of Ram Navami. “Dhyankaksh” also hosts a “Humanity Olympiad” every year, which witnesses the participation of lakhs of students from more than 3000. The finale of the event is organized in the huge auditorium on the 7th of September, every year. The sprawling lawns of Dhyankaksh also play host to the Humanity Fest, which is celebrated every year at the abode of peace. Thousands of volunteers of ‘Humanity Development Group’ gather to celebrate humanity and young people from all walks of life attend this fest and pledge to become a good human being. The villagers from surrounding locations have also welcomed the decision of including “Meditation Room” in the list of tourist spots of Faridabad as the same would attract more tourists to the place and generate employment opportunities for the locals – thereby, uplifting their standard of living. The entry to the “Dhyankaksh” was of late limited for the visitors because of the regulations introduced in the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic. However, since the largest immunization drive in the history of the country is underway, “Dhyankaksh” will soon reopen for the visitors in full-swing as per he new directives of the State Govt.

## Anandpur Bandh

The Anang Dam, situated about 2 kms. south-west of Surajkund is ascribed to Anangpal of Tomar dynasty of eleventh century AD. It is battered on the east and has steps on the west with angular flaked steps on the southern and northern extremities. The dam is 19.8 metres in height and the steps on the west rise to the east to a height over 27.43 metres. Its length is 101.2 metres. At the varying depths, from the top of the dam, there are seven drainage channels that run through the thickness of the dam and were designed to maintain the appropriate levels of the water in the dam. The steps of stairs are made of ashlar block stone laid in lime but in appearance they resemble the rubble core.

### SurajKund

A water tank, resembling Roman amphitheatre, known as Surajkund, is believed to have been constructed by the Tomar king Surajpal, who is largely believed to be a bardic tradition king. It dates back to the pre-Islamic period, and presents a remarkable example of contemporary Hindu architecture. The shape of Surajkund resembles the rising sun. Its bed is about 130 meters in diameter. It is also believed, based on the discovery of some remnants on the site, a Sun temple once existed here.

Surajkund (literal meaning is ‘Lake of the Sun’) is an artificial Kund (‘Kund’ means lake or reservoir) built in the backdrop of the Aravalli hills with an amphitheatre-shaped embankment constructed in semicircular form. It is said to have been built by the Tomar king Suraj Pal of Tomar dynasty in the 10th century. Tomar was a sun worshipper and he had therefore built a Sun temple on its western bank. Surajkund is an ideal picnic spot, as it is situated only 8 kms. away from South Delhi. Substantiating this belief, there are ruins of a Sun Temple around the sunpool. The complex includes a beautifully done-up garden and a pool – Siddha Kund.

More importantly, Surajkund has earned fame for hosting the world acclaimed Surajkund International Crafts Mela organised here every year. Celebrated during 1st to 15th February, this is a fair that showcases the Indian handicrafts, handlooms and folk traditions. Set amidst a rich rural backdrop, the fair offers a lot of fun, frolic, entertainment and exclusive shopping opportunities. The fair comes alive with truly exotic, exquisite and exclusive ethnic items that range from delicately embroidered fabrics, hand woven furnishings, terracotta artefacts, jewellery, metal and cane-ware. Not to miss, a variety of mouth-watering Indian cuisines at the Food Court.

Moreover, the Chaupal and Natyashala pulsate with, folk dances and musical evenings that add riot of colours and euphoria of rhythm to the entire experience. Every year national and state awardee crafts persons from all the corners of India participate in the Fair. The year 2018 witnessed the 32th Surajkund International Crafts Mela.

The Mela is an annual event that highlights some of the finest handloom and handicraft traditions of the country. The first fortnight of February sees the rural India bask in the warmth of admiration at Surajkund Mela village that lies about 8 kms. from South Delhi. The Mela also celebrates the heritage, culture and art forms. A different theme State is chosen every year that puts its best foot forward.

### Raja Nahar Singh Palace

This beautifully maintained palace of the legendary Raja Nahar Singh dates back to the 18th century AD. The earliest parts of Raja Nahar Singh’s palace were constructed by his ancestor Rao Balram, who came to power in 1739. This construction continued in parts till about 1850. Today, urban centres have come up around the palace. But, the beauty of the palace continues to charm the visitors. The palace is a heritage property and visitors can relax in the well-decorated rooms along with other facilities available here.

Abhinav B — Google review A Step Back in Time: Exploring the Raja Nahar Singh Fort
The Raja Nahar Singh Fort, also known as the Ballabhgarh Fort, is a captivating historical landmark nestled in the heart of Faridabad, Haryana. Built in the 18th century by Raja Nahar Singh's ancestors, this majestic fort served as the seat of power for the Jat rulers of Ballabhgarh. Today, it stands as a testament to the region's rich cultural heritage and offers visitors a glimpse into a bygone era.
Architectural Grandeur
The fort boasts a blend of Mughal and Rajput architectural styles, with intricate carvings, arched gateways, and imposing towers adorning its facade. The central courtyard, once a bustling hub of activity, now provides a tranquil space for visitors to relax and soak in the fort's ambiance.
Exploring the Past
The fort houses a museum showcasing artifacts and relics from its glorious past, including weapons, paintings, and royal memorabilia. Visitors can also climb to the ramparts for panoramic views of the surrounding cityscape, offering a unique perspective on the fort's strategic location.
Modern Transformation
While preserving its historical charm, the fort has undergone renovations and now houses a heritage hotel and restaurant. This allows visitors to experience the fort's grandeur firsthand, enjoying comfortable accommodations and delectable meals within its walls.
Overall Impression
The Raja Nahar Singh Fort is a hidden gem in Faridabad, offering a unique blend of history, architecture, and modern amenities. Whether you're a history buff or simply seeking a peaceful escape, the fort is a worthwhile destination for a day trip or weekend getaway.
Things to Keep in Mind
* The fort can get crowded, especially during weekends and holidays.
* The museum has limited timings, so plan your visit accordingly.
* While the hotel and restaurant offer convenient options, there are limited dining choices outside the fort.
In conclusion, the Raja Nahar Singh Fort is a captivating historical landmark that offers a glimpse into the rich heritage of Faridabad. With its architectural beauty, interesting exhibits, and modern amenities, it's a worthwhile destination for travelers seeking a unique and enriching experience. ## About Faridabad

Situated in Haryana, **Faridabad** is part of NCR (National Capital Region) and shares border with Delhi and Gurgaon. Faridabad is the largest City in Haryana and a booming industrial hub of the state. Faridabad alone generates about 60% of the revenues of Haryana. It is said that Baba Farid, an eminent Sufi saint in the Mogul era, who founded Faridabad.

Faridabad has many tourist attractions such as Baba Farid's Tomb, Badhkal Lake and Raja Nahar Singh Palace. Famous Suraj Kund Mela - a cultural & crafts fair is held in the city in the month of February every year. Faridabad is about 25 kms. from Delhi.

## Attractions In faridabad

The [map of Faridabad] is dotted with industries, ranging from automobiles to electronics and agriculture to manufacturing goods. However, the city also has its fair share of religious and historical attractions, picnic spots, shopping malls and markets for visitors to have a good time. Raja Nahar Singh Palace and Baba Farid’s Tomb speak volumes of the quaint past of the city. Badhkal Lake, Aravalli Golf Course and Surajkund Lake are other sought-after places among locals as well as tourists. Other local highlights include Shiva Temple and Shirdi Sai Baba Temple that are a must-visit for the devout. Scroll down to know more about these popular **attractions in Faridabad** packages.

**A Fascinating Glimpse into History**

Presently a leading industrial centre of India, Faridabad was founded in 1607 AD with just a fort, mosque and tank built by Sheikh Farid – a Sufi saint who was the treasurer of Mughal emperor Jahangir. Although the monuments are in ruins now, Faridabad still has the reminiscence of its founder in the form of his tomb, located at the centre of the town. Constructed by the Mughals in 1265 AD after the death of Baba Farid, the tomb is made of white marble and has two gateways – Nuri Darwaza and Bahishti Darwaza. Within its walls are the graves of the saint and his elder son, covered in green chaadar. You can pay your regards to the saint and later on, eat langar which is served here every day. It must be noted that ladies are prohibited inside the tomb.

Another marvel from the fascinating [history of Faridabad] is Raja Nahar Singh Palace, which dates back to the 18th century. The construction of the palace is said to have started under the rule of his ancestor Rao Balram who rose to power in 1739, and continued in parts till 1850. Raja Nahar Singh lost his life in the War of Independence of 1857, but his palace fought the pangs of time and has been turned into a beautifully maintained heritage property with air-conditioned rooms and other contemporary facilities. The architectural works around the property, located in the main market of Ballabgarh, further make it a perfect place for you to visit, admire and capture in your cameras.

**Pay Reverence to the Divine**

If you are a believer of the Supreme Being, you must pay a visit to the Shiva Temple, situated at Sainik Colony in Sector 49. The shrine is famed for its 22 feet high Shivlinga and is frequented by a large number of devotees. Another such popular shrine in Faridabad is ISKCON Temple, or Sri Sri Radha Govind Temple, located at Ashoka Enclave in Sector 37. The temple is alive with the music from prayers and aartis sung in the praise of Lord Krishna.

Situated in Sector 16A, Shirdi Sai Baba Temple is perhaps the most revered **attraction in Faridabad**. Spread over an area of 3 acres, the temple houses a 5.25 feet tall statue of the deity, made of white marble. Devotees gather in its 560 sq. metre hall for prayers, besides other ceremonies like holy bath of Baba, Satyanarayan katha and Sai Naam Jaap. The temple is most crowded on Thursdays, but it is also the perfect day to enjoy its bhandara.

**A Rendezvous with Nature**

Badhkal Lake was the most popular **attraction in Faridabad** once; however, it has dried up over the last few years. Though you cannot enjoy boating there anymore, it is still a great place to unwind in the serenity of the Aravalli Hills surrounding the place. If you are in town during the spring, do not miss the Flower Show hosted at the Badhkal Lake Resort located adjacent to the spot.

For boating though, you can head to Surajkund Lake – an artificial reservoir built in the 10th century by the Tomar king, Surajpal. This site is popular for hosting the annual Surajkund International Crafts Mela. It is one of the most significant events in the books of domestic tourism as it exhibits the varied cultures of India and attracts millions of people from across the world.

Just southwest of Surajkund is the Anangpur Dam, which is also a great spot to enjoy the pleasant [weather of Faridabad]. Aravalli Golf Course and Town Park in Sector 12 are other great places in the city, if you wish to relax and spend some quality time amidst nature with your family and kids.
