# BIKANER

## WELCOME TO CAMEL COUNTRY

Bikaner is home to one of the only two models of the biplane used by the British during World War I. They were presented by the British to Maharaja Ganga Singh, then ruler of the city. Another unique aspect about Bikaner are the sand dunes that are scattered throughout the district, especially from the north-east down to the southern area. Bikaner is situated in the northern region of Rajasthan. One of the earlier established cities, Bikaner still displays its ancient opulence through palaces and forts, built of red sandstone, that have withstood the passage of time. The city boasts of some of the world’s best riding camels and is aptly nicknamed ‘camel country’. It is also home to one of the world’s largest camel research and breeding farms; as well as being known for having its own unique temple dedicated to Karni Mata at Deshnok, called the Rats Temple.

The origins of Bikaner can be traced back to 1488 when a Rathore prince, Rao Bikaji, founded the kingdom. Legend has it that Bikaji, one of Rao Jodhaji’s five sons, left his father’s Durbar in annoyance after an insensitive remark from his father, the illustrious founder of Jodhpur. Bikaji travelled far and when he came upon the wilderness called Jangladesh, he decided to set up his own kingdom and transformed it into an impressive city.

## ATTRACTIONS & PLACES TO VISIT AND EXPLORE IN BIKANER

- [Tab 2arrow] 
- [Tab 1arrow] 
- [Tab 3arrow] 

Come explore the wonders and sites that Bikaner has to offer. There’s always something to see in Rajasthan.

![Pointer Icon] 

- ![JUNAGARH FORT] 



#### JUNAGARH FORT



Junagarh is an impregnable bastion that holds the distinction of having never been captured. It was constructed in 1588 AD by Raja Rai Singh, one of Emperor Akbar’s most distinguished generals. The fort complex houses some magnificent palaces constructed in red sandstone and marble and visitors can feast their eyes on an attractive assortment of courtyards, balconies, kiosks and windows.

- ![NATIONAL RESEARCH CENTRE ON CAMEL] 



#### NATIONAL RESEARCH CENTRE ON CAMEL



The National Research Centre on Camel is 8 kilometres from the city. This camel research and breeding centre is the only one of its kind in Asia. The centre is spread out over 2000 acres of semi-arid land and is managed by the Government of India.The Centre offers camel ride facility along with selfi points to tourists. Tourists can also enjoy here various camel milk products i.e. flavored milk, camel milk coffee, kulfi, cheese etc.

- ![LALGARH PALACE AND MUSEUM] 



#### LALGARH PALACE AND MUSEUM



Maharaja Ganga Singh commissioned the construction of this majestic palace. This architectural marvel is made entirely of red sandstone and was built in 1902 to commemorate his father, Maharaja Lal Singh. The design was conceptualised by Sir Swinton Jacob, who created this oriental fantasy by blending Rajputana, Islamic and European architecture.

- ![RAMPURIA HAVELI] 



#### RAMPURIA HAVELI



Bikaner has several havelis (aristocratic homes), the most famous cluster being the Rampuria Group of Havelis. Built of dulmera (red) stone, every aspect of the havelis – jharokhas (casements), entrances, latticed windows, divankhanas, gumaharias or basements – is simply exotic. Leaves and flowers decorate every jharokha, lending it a pleasant touch. These massive havelis are decorated with golden work of the highest quality. Their dankhanas (drawing room) take one back to the Mughal and Rajput era. One can notice an abundance of Victorian influence in their design as well. The wood carvings in Rampuria Havelies are extremely exquisite. Spaced close to each other, the havelis are truly a sight to behold.

- ![GANGA GOVERNMENT MUSEUM] 



#### GANGA GOVERNMENT MUSEUM



Described as the best museum in the State, the museum contains a rich display of archaeological artefacts from Harappa and the early Gupta periods. There are separate sections for paintings, arts and craft, woven carpets, clay pottery, ancient coins and Rajput weaponry.

- ![LAXMI NIWAS PALACE] 



#### LAXMI NIWAS PALACE



Laxmi Niwas Palace was the residence of the king of Bikaner, Maharaja Ganga Singh. Built between 1898 and 1902 by British architect Sir Samuel Swinton Jacob, this structure displays an Indo-Saracenic architectural style. It is now a luxury hotel.

- ![PRACHINA MUSEUM] 



#### PRACHINA MUSEUM



Located in the great Junagarh Fort, this museum hosts royal costumes, textiles and accessories of Rajasthani royalty. The 'Poshaks' (garments worn by ladies) are a reminder of the now lost craft of traditional designs, styles and workmanship. The family portraits on display narrate a story about how changing cultural settings influenced the style of immortalising the former rulers.

- ![DESHNOK KARNI MATA TEMPLE] 



#### DESHNOK KARNI MATA TEMPLE



The Karni Mata Temple at Deshnok is a beautiful structure made of stone and marble, inside which resides an image of Karni Mata. The image is decorated with a ‘mukut’ (tiara) and garlands. The images of her sisters and the sisters of Avad Mata give her company on either side. The temple is known the world over for the presence of kabas (rats) that roam freely within the temple precincts. Know More 

- ![JAIN TEMPLE BHANDASAR] 



#### JAIN TEMPLE BHANDASAR



Jain Temple Bhandasar is a 15th century temple dedicated to the 5th Tirthankar (a person that has conquered the cycle of life, death and rebirth and paved the path for others to attain nirvana), Sumatinathji, and is one of the oldest monuments of Bikaner. The temple design includes intricate mirror work, murals and gold leaf paintings. Devotees throng to the temple from all corners of the country.

#### KODAMDESAR TEMPLE AND LAKE



24 kilometres from Bikaner is the Kodamdesar Temple. Kodamdesar Bhainru Ji was installed by Rao Bikaji sometime during the first three years of his arrival from Jodhpur. This place of worship was initially chosen as the site to lay the foundation of Bikaner, but was later shifted to its present location. A Beautiful lake is situated in the backyard of this temple. The serenity & tranquility of this lake mesmerize the devotees and tourists.

- ![SHRI LAXMINATH TEMPLE] 



#### SHRI LAXMINATH TEMPLE



The rulers of Bikaner regarded Lord Laxminathji (Lord Vishnu) as the real king of Bikaner and themselves as his Deewans or Ministers. This temple, made of marble and red stone, was constructed to enshrine Lord Laxminath whose throne rests here.

- ![SHIV BARI TEMPLE] 



#### SHIV BARI TEMPLE



Located just 6 kilometres from Bikaner, this Shiva temple is known as Laleshwar Mahadev Mandir and is fortified by a high wall. It was commissioned and built by Maharaja Doongar Singh in the 19th century to commemorate his father Maharaja Lal Singh. The highlights of this red stone temple are beautiful wall paintings, the four-faced black marble Shiva statue and a bronze Nandi statue facing the Shiva-Lingam. The temple also houses two large reservoirs of water known as bawaris.

#### GAJNER PALACE AND LAKE



Gajner is an incomparable jewel of the Thar. It was meant to serve as a hunting and relaxing lodge for the royal family as well as for visiting guests. It has now been converted into a hotel

#### DEVI KUND



8 kilometres from Bikaner is the royal crematorium. The chhatri of Maharaj Surat Singh is a fine example of the architecture of that era. Devi Kund also has cenotaphs of 22 female members of the royal family prior to Maharaja Gaj Sigh Ji, who committed sati. There is also one cenotaph of a Sata (male sati) of a ruler. Maharaja Surat Singh's Chhatri is built entirely in white marble with spectacular Rajput paintings on its ceiling.

Kolayat is an important pilgrimage place for Hindus. Locals as well as devotees from distant places visit this temple complex every year. The main attractions of the town are its several temples, ghats, the sacred lake and the town market. Kolayat is about 50 kilometres from Bikaner.

 Introduction: 
 A Glimpse into the Heritage of Bikaner 
 Nestled in the heart of Rajasthan, Bikaner is a city that exudes historical grandeur and cultural richness. Known for its majestic forts, palaces, and temples, Bikaner offers a journey back in time to the era of royal Rajput kings. This enchanting destination boasts a blend of architectural marvels and traditional Rajasthani hospitality, making it a must-visit for history enthusiasts and avid travellers alike. 
 
 Why Bikaner Should Be on Your Travel Bucket List? 
 Bikaner is not just about historical landmarks; it is a vibrant city where the past and the present coexist harmoniously. From the bustling markets filled with local handicrafts to the tranquil lakeside palaces, Bikaner promises an immersive experience that captivates every visitor. Whether you're an adventure seeker, a culture aficionado, or a foodie, Bikaner has something extraordinary to offer. 
 Junagarh Fort: 
 
 Historical Significance 
 Junagarh Fort stands as a testament to Bikaner's glorious past. Unlike other forts in Rajasthan, it was never conquered, highlighting the valiant spirit of its rulers. Built-in the late 16th century by Raja Rai Singh, this fort has witnessed centuries of history unfold within its robust walls. 
 Architectural Marvels 
 The fort is a stunning example of Rajput architecture with Mughal influences. Its intricate stone carvings, vast courtyards, and majestic halls showcase the craftsmanship of a bygone era. The stunning blend of red sandstone and marble creates a striking visual appeal that leaves visitors in awe. 
 Must-See Attractions Within the Fort 
 Inside Junagarh Fort, you'll find several notable structures such as the Anup Mahal, Ganga Niwas, and the Chandra Mahal. Each palace and hall boasts unique features, from exquisite mirror work to ornate frescoes. The fort's museum houses a collection of artefacts, including weapons, costumes, and paintings that provide deeper insights into the royal lifestyle. 
 Gajner Palace and Lake: 
 
 A Serene Lakeside Retreat 
 Gajner Palace, situated on the banks of Gajner Lake, is a tranquil retreat away from the city's hustle and bustle. This palace was once a hunting lodge for the Bikaner royals and now serves as a heritage hotel. 
 The Grandeur of Gajner Palace 
 The palace's stunning architecture, lush gardens, and serene lake create a picturesque setting. The blend of red sandstone with lush green surroundings and a tranquil lake makes it an idyllic spot for relaxation and photography. 
 Bird Watching and Wildlife Spotting 
 Gajner Lake is a haven for bird watchers and nature enthusiasts. The area is home to numerous species of migratory birds, especially during winter. Visitors can also spot wildlife such as deer and antelope in the nearby Gajner Wildlife Sanctuary. 
 Karni Mata Temple: 
 
 The Unique Temple of Rats 
 Karni Mata Temple, located in Deshnoke, near Bikaner, is one of the most unique temples in India. Known as the Temple of Rats, it is home to thousands of rats that are considered sacred. Devotees believe these rats, or "kabas," are reincarnations of Karni Mata's followers. 
 Mythological and Cultural Importance 
 According to legend, Karni Mata, a revered female sage, had miraculous powers. When her stepson died, she requested Yama, the god of death, to reincarnate him. Yama granted her wish, and since then, her followers are believed to be reincarnated as rats. This temple holds immense cultural significance and is a major pilgrimage site. 
 Visitor Tips for the Temple 
 When visiting Karni Mata Temple, be prepared for the unusual sight of rats scurrying around. It's considered auspicious if a rat runs over your feet. Make sure to cover your head and wear respectful attire. Photography is allowed, but be mindful of the temple's sanctity. 
 National Research Centre on Camel: 
 
 The Camel Capital of India 
 Bikaner is renowned as the Camel Capital of India, and the National Research Centre on Camel (ICAR) is a testament to this title. Established in 1984, ICAR is dedicated to the research and development of camels, an integral part of Rajasthan's culture and economy. 
 Research and Conservation Efforts 
 ICAR focuses on the breeding, health, and utility of camels. Visitors can learn about different camel breeds and their significance. The centre's efforts in the conservation and sustainable use of camels have garnered international recognition. 
 Camel Rides and Activities for Visitors 
 At ICAR, visitors can enjoy camel rides and interact with these gentle giants. The centre also has a camel milk parlour where you can taste unique camel milk products like ice cream and cheese. It's an educational and enjoyable experience for all age groups. 
 Sand Dunes Safari: 
 
 The Thrill of Desert Exploration 
 Exploring the Thar Desert is an adventure that should not be missed when visiting Bikaner. A desert safari offers the thrill of traversing the vast dunes and experiencing the stark beauty of the desert landscape. 
 Dune Bashing and Camel Safaris 
 Visitors can choose between exhilarating dune bashing in 4x4 vehicles or a more traditional camel safari. Both options provide a unique way to explore the desert and enjoy the mesmerizing sunset views over the dunes. 
 Overnight Camps and Cultural Shows 
 For a more immersive experience, overnight desert camps are available. These camps offer comfortable accommodations, traditional Rajasthani meals, and cultural shows featuring folk music and dance. It's a perfect way to experience the desert's serene beauty and vibrant culture. 
 Rampuria Havelis: 
 
 Architectural Elegance of the Havelis 
 The Rampuria Havelis are a group of exquisite mansions that epitomize the grandeur of Bikaner's merchant class. Built-in the 19th century, these havelis are renowned for their intricate stone carvings, jharokhas (overhanging enclosed balconies), and lavish interiors. 
 The Rich Merchant History 
 These havelis were constructed by the wealthy Rampuria family, known for their opulence and influence in Bikaner. Each haveli showcases the family's prosperity and the artistic finesse of that era. 
 Walking Tours and Photography 
 Exploring Rampuria Havelis on foot is a delightful experience. The narrow lanes and vibrant facades provide ample opportunities for photography. Guided tours offer deeper insights into the history and architectural nuances of these magnificent structures. 
 Bhandasar Jain Temple: 
 
 Spiritual and Architectural Wonder 
 The Jain Temple Bhandasar, also known as Banda Shah Jain Temple, is a marvel of spiritual and architectural beauty. Constructed in the 15th century, this temple is dedicated to Sumatinath, the fifth Tirthankara of Jainism. Its structure is adorned with vibrant frescoes depicting the lives of the Jain saints and intricate mirror work that adds to its splendour. 
 Intricate Frescoes and Artworks 
 One of the temple's most striking features is its intricate frescoes, which cover the walls and ceilings. These paintings are rich in colour and detail, depicting scenes from Jain mythology and daily life. The use of gold leaf in the artwork adds a touch of opulence and showcases the exquisite craftsmanship of the artists of that time. 
 Religious Significance 
 For the Jain community, Bhandasar Temple holds immense religious significance. It is not only a place of worship but also a site for pilgrimage. The temple's serene ambience offers a perfect setting for meditation and reflection, attracting devotees and tourists alike. 
 The Royal Cenotaphs: 
 
 Historical Remembrances 
 Devi Kund Sagar, nestled on the outskirts of Bikaner, houses the royal cenotaphs, or chhatris, of the Bikaner royal family. These elegant structures serve as memorials to the departed royals and are significant historical landmarks. 
 Architectural Grandeur 
 The cenotaphs at Devi Kund Sagar are architectural marvels adorned with intricate carvings and designs. Each cenotaph is unique in its architecture, reflecting the individuality of the royalty it commemorates. The craftsmanship displayed in these structures is a testament to the rich cultural heritage of Bikaner. 
 A Tranquil Retreat 
 Surrounded by lush greenery and serene waters, Devi Kund Sagar offers a tranquil retreat from the bustling city life. Visitors can stroll amidst the cenotaphs, admiring the architectural beauty and paying homage to the erstwhile rulers of Bikaner. 
 Lalgarh Palace and Museum: 
 
 Regal Splendor 
 Lalgarh Palace, a magnificent architectural marvel, stands as a tribute to the grandeur of Bikaner's royalty. Built in the early 20th century by Maharaja Ganga Singh, this imposing structure showcases a fusion of Rajput, Mughal, and European architectural styles. 
 Opulent Interiors 
 The interiors of Lalgarh Palace are adorned with exquisite artworks, intricate carvings, and luxurious furnishings. The palace museum houses a remarkable collection of artifacts, including royal portraits, vintage photographs, and antique weaponry, offering a glimpse into the opulent lifestyle of the Bikaner royals. 
 Cultural Extravaganza 
 Apart from its architectural significance, Lalgarh Palace is also a cultural hub, hosting various events and festivals throughout the year. Visitors can witness traditional Rajasthani performances, including folk dances, music recitals, and puppet shows, immersing themselves in the vibrant cultural heritage of Bikaner. 
 Shri Laxminath Temple: 
 
 Sacred Abode 
 Shri Laxminath Temple, dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Goddess Lakshmi, is one of the oldest and most revered temples in Bikaner. Built-in the early 19th century by Maharaja Rao Lunkaran, this temple holds immense religious significance for the locals. 
 Architectural Splendor 
 The temple's architecture is a blend of Rajput and Mughal styles, characterized by intricately carved pillars, ornate domes, and colourful frescoes. The sanctum sanctorum houses the idols of Lord Vishnu and Goddess Lakshmi, adorned with precious jewels and offered daily prayers by devotees. 
 Spiritual Serenity 
 Shri Laxminath Temple exudes a sense of spiritual serenity, attracting devotees from far and wide. The rhythmic chanting of hymns, the fragrance of incense, and the sight of devout worshippers create a tranquil atmosphere conducive to prayer and introspection. 
 Prachina Museum: 
 
 Preserving Cultural Heritage 
 Prachina Museum, located within the premises of Junagarh Fort, is a treasure trove of Bikaner's rich cultural heritage. Founded by Princess Siddhi Kumari, this museum aims to preserve and showcase the traditional arts, crafts, and lifestyle of Rajasthan. 
 Exquisite Artefacts 
 The museum houses a diverse collection of artefacts, including traditional costumes, jewellery, textiles, paintings, and household items. Each exhibit is meticulously curated, providing visitors with insights into the artistic and cultural traditions of Bikaner. 
 Interactive Exhibits 
 Prachina Museum offers a unique interactive experience, allowing visitors to participate in various cultural activities such as turban tying, mehndi application, and puppetry. These hands-on activities provide a glimpse into the daily lives and rituals of the people of Rajasthan. 
 Ganga Golden Jubilee Museum: 
 Cultural Repository 
 Ganga Golden Jubilee Museum is another prominent institution dedicated to preserving Bikaner's cultural heritage. Established in 1937 to commemorate the Golden Jubilee of Maharaja Ganga Singh, this museum houses a vast collection of artifacts spanning centuries of history. 
 Historical Treasures 
 The museum's exhibits include archaeological finds, sculptures, coins, manuscripts, and weapons from different periods of Bikaner's history. Highlights include relics from the Indus Valley Civilization, the Gupta Empire, and medieval Rajput kingdoms, offering valuable insights into the region's past. 
 Educational Insights 
 Ganga Golden Jubilee Museum serves as an educational resource for scholars, researchers, and history enthusiasts. The museum's informative displays, accompanied by detailed descriptions and multimedia presentations, provide a comprehensive understanding of Bikaner's cultural evolution. 
 The Old City of Bikaner: 
 
 Historic Heart 
 Bikaner's Old City, with its labyrinthine lanes and ancient architecture, is a living testament to the city's rich history and heritage. Encircled by imposing walls and grand gateways, the Old City exudes an old-world charm that transports visitors back in time. 
 Architectural Marvels 
 The Old City is replete with architectural marvels, including elaborately carved havelis, intricately designed temples, and majestic forts. The narrow alleys and bustling bazaars add to the allure, offering a glimpse into the vibrant lifestyle of its inhabitants. 
 Cultural Melting Pot 
 The Old City is a cultural melting pot, where traditions and customs from different eras converge harmoniously. From religious processions and folk festivals to street performances and culinary delights, every corner of the Old City pulsates with life and energy. 
 Urmul Desert Crafts Store: 
 Artisanal Delights 
 Urmul Desert Crafts Store is a haven for connoisseurs of traditional Rajasthani handicrafts and artefacts. Located in the heart of Bikaner, this boutique showcases a wide array of handmade products crafted by local artisans using age-old techniques. 
 Handcrafted Treasures 
 Visitors to Urmul Desert Crafts Store can browse through an extensive collection of textiles, pottery, jewellery, leather goods, and home decor items. Each product is a masterpiece of craftsmanship, reflecting the rich cultural heritage and artistic prowess of Rajasthan. 
 Supporting Artisan Communities 
 By patronizing the Urmul Desert Crafts Store, visitors contribute to the preservation of traditional crafts and the livelihoods of local artisans. The store follows fair trade practices, ensuring that artisans receive fair compensation for their skills and labour. 
 Conclusion: 
 Bikaner, with its fascinating blend of history, culture, and heritage, offers a captivating journey through time. From majestic forts and palaces to vibrant markets and spiritual sanctuaries, the city beckons travellers to explore its myriad treasures. Whether you're a history buff, an art enthusiast, or a seeker of spiritual solace, Bikaner promises an enriching and unforgettable experience.

 the greatest time to visit Bikaner because of the lovely weather from October to March. Then there is the annual Camel
 Festival in January, which draws more tourists at this time of year.
 
 Here are a few must-see places to visit in Bikaner that will offer you an appreciation of the splendour of the city's
 kingdoms and their superior architectural design. If you want to go deeply into the history of this stunning location,
 here is a list of the greatest Bikaner attractions.
 
 
 
 1. Junagarh Fort
 
 
 The city of Bikaner was constructed around the imposing Bikaner Junagarh Fort. The unbeaten and unconquered Junagarh
 fort, which still stands tall and narrates tales of a great past, is one of the most fascinating places to view in
 Bikaner. This fort is a rare example of its sort thanks to the inventive fusion of Gujarati and Mughal architecture.
 Photographers have several choices to capture it because of its towering majesty and lovely surroundings.
 
 The fort's interior palaces, gardens, balconies, kiosks, and other structures show a fusion of architectural styles
 influenced by the varied kings' cultural backgrounds and outside influences.
 
 
 
 2. Lalgarh Fort
 
 
 The former Maharaja of Bikaner created the architectural masterpiece known as Lalgarh Palace. Both historically and
 materially, it is a magnificent display of wealth. The palace has ornate wings with expansive hallways, and each one is
 decorated with paintings and other spectacular artefacts that bring the history of the area to life. The palace is a
 wonderful place to learn about and develop a passion for history, royalty, and architecture. A stay at the vintage hotel
 will actually allow you to feel this palace's splendour more closely. This opulent hotel has been built inside a section
 of the palace, where you will be treated like royalty while surrounded by opulent decor. Enjoy the historical splendour
 of this opulent palace and the historical beauty of India.
 
 
 
 3. Laxmi Niwas Palace
 
 
 The Lalgarh Palace complex in Bikaner, Rajasthan, contains the historic Laxmi Niwas Palace. It is one of the most
 attractive royal palaces in Rajasthan and one of the most popular heritage places in Bikaner. The former home of
 Maharaja Ganga Singh of Bikaner, Laxmi Niwas Palace was constructed between 1898 and 1902 in an Indo-Saracenic style by
 the British architect Sir Samuel Swinton Jacob. One of the most well-liked tourist attractions in Bikaner is the elegant
 red sandstone building.
 
 
 
 4. Jain Temple Bhandasar
 
 
 The Sumatinath Jain Temple in Bhandasar is devoted to this religion's fifth Tirthankara. You will be in awe of this
 temple's beauty because of its distinctive Rajputana architecture and complex design. It is one of the religious tourist
 places to visit in Bikaner because of the combination of red sandstone and white marble, as well as the alluring gold
 layer finishing. This three-story temple's magnificence is admirable, and every traveller should include it on their
 itinerary.
 
 
 
 5. Karni Mata Temple
 
 
 The Karni Mata Temple, one of Bikaner's unusual tourist attractions, is devoted to Karni Mata, a manifestation of the
 goddess Durga. The shrine is thought to be home to more than 20,000 rats. As they are thought to be the sons of Karni
 Mata, the visitors worship these rats and give them prasad. The temple's beautiful marble sculptures and silver gate
 contribute to its allure.
 
 
 
 6. Kote Gate
 
 
 The Kote Gate, which separates the modern city of Bikaner from the old city, stands tall like a guardian to the city and
 serves as its primary entrance. Local shopping is a Bikaner attraction. This market offers a wide range of products,
 including handicrafts, traditional attire, kitchenware, art made of camel hide, and traditional open-toed shoes. This
 location will thrill you with its abundance of dining options in addition to its shopping. Additionally, this location
 is surrounded by some of Bikaner's top shopping areas.
 
 
 
 7. Thar Desert
 
 
 At the Thar Desert, explore the wilderness in the centre of the desert and take in the breathtaking splendour of the
 sand ocean. Every visitor to Bikaner should go on a camel safari. You can experience nature in its most basic form while
 taking advantage of the excellent facilities offered by the organisers.
 
 8. Sursagar Lake 
 
 Also known as Chand Talao, Sursagar Lake was built in the 18th century and sits at the heart of Bikaner.
 It is a scenic and peaceful spot amidst the city's hustle, with a towering illuminated Shiva statue that shines
 brightly during Maha Shivratri.
 The lake includes floodgates at its base that release excess water into the Vishvamitra River when needed.
 
 9. Kolayat 
 
 Kolayat is a serene pilgrimage town known for the sacred Kolayat Lake and the historic Kapil Muni Temple.
 It hosts the famous Kapil Muni Fair, Bikaner’s largest, and is believed to be where sage Kapil Muni meditated to
 restore peace and balance.
 Its spiritual vibe and offbeat charm make it a unique stop for visitors.
 
 10. Station Road 
 
 Station Road is a bustling shopping area and a paradise for bargain hunters.
 You'll find everything from traditional Rajasthani jootis and leather goods to miniature paintings and handcrafted
 souvenirs.
 Its central location near the railway station makes it easily accessible for tourists.
 
 11. Bikaji Ki Tekri 
 
 Bikaji Ki Tekri is Bikaner’s oldest fort, built over 500 years ago by Rao Bika.
 Located just outside the city, this red sandstone fortress features intriguing architecture and a labyrinth of
 underground chambers.
 As a lesser-known heritage site, it offers a more intimate glimpse into Bikaner’s early history.
 
 12. Shri Laxmikant Temple 
 
 Shri Laxminath Temple is one of Bikaner's oldest and most sacred sites, dedicated to Goddess Laxmi and Lord Vishnu.
 Renowned for its intricate silver work and divine idols, the temple becomes especially vibrant during festivals like
 Diwali, Janmashtami, and Ramnavmi.
 It’s a spiritual cornerstone of Bikaner’s cultural landscape.

## You're Going to Love Bikaner

In the desert of Rajasthan, Bikaner rises like a gorgeous apparition, with its red brick palaces, exotic temples and atmospheric ancient center. Add in camel safaris and stunning desert scenery, and you've found one of north-west India's finest destinations.

## Top 5 Reasons to Visit Bikaner

### 1\. The Junagarh Fort

Without question Bikaner's premier attraction, the Junagarh Fort dates back to the 16th century and features a superb textile museum within its astonishing grounds.

### 2\. Plenty of Historical Attractions

Beyond Junagarh, Bikaner's highlights include the colonial Laxmi Niwas Palace and the Lalgarh Fort that was home for centuries to the Rajputana royal family.

### 3\. Magical Camel Safaris

Get out into the desert of Rajasthan on the back of a camel with expert guides who know every birdwatching spot and photo opportunity.

### 4\. Spectacular Rajasthani Food

From street food like bhujia (chickpea chips) to thalis that cover the entire table and a dizzying variety of sweets, Rajasthan is a fantastic Indian destination for gastronomes.

## What to do in Bikaner

### 1\. A Fortified City Within A City

The foundations of Junagarh Fort were laid in 1478 by Maharaja Rao Bika,and the city of Bikaner grew up around it. It is considered one of Bikaner's major tourist attractions. Within are temples, mosques, archways, mansions and a museum depicting the royal life the fort once witnessed. Upon arrival at Junagarh Fort, the first thing you'll notice are the artistic courtyards, balconies, and windows. Step into the Zenana quarters where elegant designs that demonstrate a high level of artistic skill are exhibited. Be enthralled by the carvings in red and gold sandstone.

### 2\. Where The Fun Begins

Formerly the residence of Bikaner's Maharaja, Sir Ganga Singh, the Gajner Palace is currently a Heritage hotel. It stands approximately 20 km from Bikaner beside the picturesque Gajner Lake. If you decide to stay for more than a day, the Gajner Palace offers the best in hospitality. Alternatively, if your budget doesn't go in line with the palace's prices, you could always opt for a day trip instead. The Gajner Palace is divided into five distinctive areas: Mandir Chowk, Dungar Niwas, Champa Niwas and Gulab Niwas. In each of these areas, you can enjoy desert safaris, boating, nature walks, and lots of other fun activities.

### 3\. Reliving Ancient History

One of the most well-known attractions in Bikaner, the Karni Mata Temple is also one of India's strangest holy places. This Hindu temple, which dates back to the early 15th century, is mainly dedicated to the Goddess Durga incarnate, Karni Mata. History records that when Mata's son passed on, she called upon Yama to bring him back to life, but Yama declined. Taking advantage of her power as the Goddess of incarnation, Karni Mata restored her son's life and declared that her future family will never die, but instead be reincarnated in rat form. So when you arrive here, be prepared for a warm welcome from approximately 20,000 rats.

### 4\. Ultimate Safari Experience

Situated a few kilometers from the magnificent Thar Desert is the world-famous Sand Dunes Safari, and this particular site brings a whole new meaning to the term 'safari.' [**Bikaner**] 

The city of Bikaner lies amid the vast expanse of the Thar Desert and is therefore assigned the title of "cultural paradise and holiday cheap destination." Bikaner is rich in festival and cultural extravaganza and houses beautiful buildings, art, and culture. The camels strolling through the beautiful dunes, old castles, and forts will enchant you. The beautiful city is rightly popular as one of Rajasthan's three great desert kingdoms. It has old-world charm, beautiful havelis, and a pleasant feel of a bygone age of the Rajputs. The Desert Safari and the Sand Dunes are the best things to do. People come from all over the world to see the international camel fair in Bikaner.

[**Bhandasar Jain Temple**] 

One of Bikaner's 27 beautiful Jain buildings is the Bhandasar Jain Temple. This temple honors the fifth Tirthankara, Sumatinath, and many consider it the most beautiful and tallest. A businessman named Bhanda Shah, a Jain built this temple. The builders used pure ghee and dry coconuts to construct the base of this temple. They crafted the three-story building using red sandstone. Vibrant wall paintings and usta art adorn the temple, making it a must-see place in Bikaner.

[**Desert Camel Safari Bikaner**] 

Bikaner's Desert Safari is rough, so everyone should try it at least once. In Bikaner, there are a lot of companies that can set up Desert Camel Safari and Jeep Safari. A learning experience of the way of life, culture, food, desert safaris, traditions, mysteries, stories, and many other things. Camel Safari takes tourists to a different world, filled with beautiful scenery but devoid of greenery. It is an entirely different kind of journey. Some words don't suffice to describe the beauty of the sunset. This adventure is a must-do when booking Bikaner tour packages.

[**Devi Kund Sagar Bikaner**] 

Devi Kund is about 8 km from Bikaner and has Chhatris, the royal tombs of brave Bika dynasty kings. Several cenotaphs can be found in this royal cemetery. All of the cenotaphs are amazing works of Rajput art. If you go to Bikaner, you have to see this beautiful building. You could also go to the close Dargah of Rustal Ali Shah Peer, one of the most interesting [places to visit in Bikaner].

[**Gajner Palace and Lake**] 

The city of Bikaner has Gajner Palace, which is on the shores of Gajner Lake. Maharaja Ganga Singh built this grand house as a hunting lodge for himself and his family. In 1976, it was turned into a hotel. Gajner Palace has many fun things to do, such as boat rides and desert safaris. The builders originally constructed it as a native Rajasthani palace, and it now serves as a stunning work of art for hosting guests. The Great Indian Desert, also famous as the Thar Desert, stretches through Bikaner. It offers opportunities for activities like desert safaris and camel rides, which can be included in your Bikaner tour packages.

[**Gajner Wildlife Sanctuary**] 

This Bikaner wildlife sanctuary is a beautiful place to be in nature. It is on the shores of Gajner Lake, close to Gajner Palace. It is one of the most well-known wildlife refuges in Rajasthan and a must-see on any Bikaner tour package. On top of a small hill, Gajner Wildlife Sanctuary is an oasis and wildlife haven with many plants, animals, and a thick layer of green forest. The refuge has a beautiful lake that the animals use to quench their thirst and make the area look nicer.

[**Ganga State Museum**] 

One of the most important museums in Bikaner is the Ganga Singh Museum. One of the must-see places for sightseeing in Bikaner is this museum, which is the best in Rajasthan. The Ganga Government Museum, also known as the Ganga Golden Jubilee Museum showcases art, cartoons, and unique secrets from Rajasthan. Constructed in 1937, this building now showcases terracotta items from the Gupta era, traditional Rajasthani musical instruments and rich gold paintings by local artists, beautiful rugs, and royal clothing.

[**Junagarh Fort Bikaner**] 

Beautiful buildings grew around the Junagarh Fort of Bikaner, which is in the area. The fort was first called Chintamani. In the 20th century, it was changed to Junagarh, which means "Old fort." The base of the Junagarh fort was built by Rao Bika in 1478. Back then, though, it was only a stone fort. The grand building we see today was opened on February 17, 1589. There are 16 generations of rulers in Bikaner, starting in the late 16th century, and their unique monuments are on show in the fort, making it an essential part of [Bikaner tour packages].

[**Kodamdesar Bheru Ji Temple**] 

The holy Kodamdesar Bhairu Ji Temple is in the village of Kodamdesar in the Bikaner district of Rajasthan. The founder and king of Bikaner (1472-1504 AD), Rao Bika Ji, built the Kodamdesar Bhairu Ji Temple. He was from the royal family of Jodhpur. He left Jodhpur in 1465 to start Bikaner State. Three years after he got there from Jodhpur, he built this temple. Initially, they chose this place of worship to build Bikaner, but later moved it to its current location. This temple is a hidden gem and one of the places to visit in Bikaner.

[**Lalgarh Palace & Museum**] 

The royal house Lalgarh House is in Bikaner. It is one of the few palaces that the British built for Indian kings and one of the best places to visit in Bikaner. The Lalgarh Palace is one of the most impressive palaces in Rajasthan's past. It stands in a royal place and tells stories about great kings and queens. The house for Maharaja Ganga Singh was built by the British builder Sir Samuel Swinton Jacob from 1902 to 1926 CE.

[**National Research Centre on Camel**] 

This study center looks at different kinds of camels because they are important to the desert's fragile ecosystem. This study center has a museum showing how camels are studied and how they grow. There are also facilities like camel rides and Safari. People can also use camel milk to make drinks and ice cream. The national government set up the farm in 1984 and named it the Camel Breeding Farm. Including this in your Bikaner tour packages will allow you to experience the fascinating role of camels in the region.

[**Rampuria Haveli**] 

There is a group of havelis called Rampuria Havelis in Bikaner, Rajasthan. You can visit the Rampuria Group of Havelis, a well-known heritage spot and one of the best places to visit in Bikaner. Many people know the Rampuria Group of Havelis as some of the most beautiful buildings in Bikaner. They're over 400 years old. Balujee Chalva built these havelis on the orders of a wealthy trading family from Rampuria.

[**Shiv Bari Temple Bikaner**] 

Shive Bari temple is a shrine to Lord Shiva. It was built in the 1800s out of red sandstone. The figure of Lord Shiva in black marble has four faces, and a bronze Nandi is standing in front of the Shiva Lingam. Inside the temple, there are two big water tanks called bawaris. The temple also tells you a lot about the unique features of Rajasthani design, making it a must-visit spot in Bikaner tour packages.

[**Shree Karni Mata Temple**] 

The Karni Mata temple, also known as the "temple of rats," is a well-known building in Rajasthan, India. It is in Deshnoke, 30 km from Bikaner. This temple is known across the length and breadth of India due to 20,000 black rats feeding for more than a thousand years. Though these rats are generally regarded as simply rid of filth by most people, many consider them holy, reverberating what people call 'kabbas.' Hundreds pour into the temple from far-off lands for a visit to worship these rats to fulfill their wishes. Just seeing these "rats" attracts so many Indians to the temple that this makes it one of the popular places to visit in Bikaner.

[**Shri Laxminath Temple Bikaner**] 

The Shri Laxminath temple in Bikaner is one of the oldest ones there. It decorates the holy place where Lord Vishnu and Goddess Laxmi live. In addition to the beautiful statue, this temple has beautiful silver art all over it. The temple hosts many holidays, including Janmashtami, Nirjala Ekadashi, Ramnavami, Diwali, and Geeta Jayanti. Be sure to visit the temple during these festivals and include it in your Bikaner tour packages for a truly cultural experience.

[**The Laxmi Niwas Palace**] 

Laxmi Niwas is one such historic palace in the dynamic city of Bikaner, Rajasthan. It is part of the royal residence at the Lalgarh Palace complex. In fact, many argue that it is one of the most beautiful castles in Rajasthan and one of the most important places to visit in Bikaner historically. The Laxmi Niwas Palace used to be where Maharaja Ganga Singh, the king, lived. Sir Samuel Swinton Jacob planned it, and Maharaja Sir Ganga Singh had it built between 1898 and 1902 CE. Building the castle was also crucial to the Maharaja of Bikaner because he wanted to create jobs there.
