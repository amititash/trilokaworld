# Places to Visit in Jamnagar

Jamnagar is a popular tourist destination and is home to many exciting attractions. With scenic spots, verdant hills, religious landmarks, and sanctuaries for birds and marine life, Jamnagar has a wealth of attractions that you may enjoy.
The islands in Jamnagar are home to a wide variety of interesting creatures, including octopuses, seabirds, corals, and seashells. Leopards, jackals, wolves, jungle cats, blue bulls, hyenas, and snakes can all be found in abundance here.
Jamnagar is also known for its festivities and often celebrates local deities and traditions. All in all, Jamnagar will delight your senses.

[**Bet Dwarka Near Jamnagar**] 

##### Blend of spirituality and water activities

Bet Dwarka, also referred to as Shankhodhar, is a populated island located in the Indian state of Gujarat about three kilometres off the coast of Okha. The state highway from Jamnagar to Dwarka provides quick and easy access to the location. Many ancient temples and religious texts have been discovered here that attest to this area being Lord Krishna's first place of residence.

[**Bala Hanuman Temple**] 

##### A temple that is always bustling with devotees

The Bala Hanuman Temple, also called Shri Bala Hanuman Sankirtan Mandir, stands on the southeast shore of Ranmal Lake in Jamnagar. The temple also features statues of other deities, such as Hanuman, Ram, Lakshmana, and Sita. Due to widespread devotion, hundreds of worshippers make daily pilgrimages to this shrine.

[**Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary**] 

##### Home to a wide range of birds

There is a wide variety of animals and plants in the 6 square kilometres that make up Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary. Around 220 native and migratory bird species call this sanctuary home, including several that are critically endangered, such as the Dalmatian pelican, Darter, Black-headed ibis, and Indian skimmer.

##### Tranquil spiritual site

The Bhujiyo Kotho Temple is a magnificent attraction in the city of Jamnagar. Former Jamnagar mayors and council members erected it near Lakhota Lake to protect the city against invasion. The height of Bhujiyo Kotho is its greatest advantage since it offers a panoramic view of Jamnagar.

[**Aradhana Dham**] 

##### Jain pilgrimage site

Aradhana Dham is a 40-acre Jain pilgrimage centre located on the edge of the Jamnagar-Dwarka Highway. For those travellers in need of some quiet time away from their hectic schedules, this is one of the best spots to go in Jamnagar. You can also stay in one of the rest homes at the Aradhana Dham for a longer period of time.

![Harsiddhi Temple] 

[**Harsiddhi Temple**] 

##### Revered temple amid nature

Harsiddhi Temple, also known as Harshad Mata Temple, is located around 30 kilometres from Porbandar. Lord Krishna, who is said to have worshipped her during his lifetime, may have been responsible for the construction of the temple. To vanquish and destroy the asuras, Krishna prayed for strength to Harsiddhi Mata. With her help, Krishna defeated the asuras and afterwards constructed this temple to honour his victory.

![Lakhota Museum] 

[**Lakhota Museum**] 

##### Insightful tourist attraction

Originally constructed as a fort, the surrounding lake protected the fort's defenders against invasion. Lakhota Museum is the current name for the site. There are artefacts at the museum that date from the 9th to the 18th century, as well as relics from the Middle Ages such as ceramics and whalebone.

![Maa Ashapura Temple] 

[**Maa Ashapura Temple**] 

##### Sacred Hindu temple

The Maa Ashapura Temple in Jamnagar is a renowned pilgrimage site since it is dedicated to one of the Kutch region's primary deities. Maa Ashapura is the worshipped deity who, as her name implies, grants the wishes of her devotees. The fact that the Maa Ashapura idol has seven sets of eyes is what sets it apart from others. Karad Vanias, a minister in the court of Kutch's monarch Lakho Fulani, erected the first temple around 1300 CE.

[**Pirotan Island**] 

##### Scenic island with a spiritual association

Those who wish to visit Pirotan Island must adhere to stringent regulations enforced by the Ports Department, Customs Department, and Forest Department. Those who are not Indian also need to obtain authorization from the local police. The original name for the city was Pirjothaan, and it was chosen to honour the holy site where the great Islamic scholar Khwaja Khizar Rahmatullahi Alaih is buried.

[**Lakhota Fort**] 

##### Ancient fort for history buffs

Around the grounds of Lakhota Fort lies a beautiful and peaceful lake. Lakhota Lake, or Ranmal Lake as it is often called, is located smack dab in the midst of Jamnagar. Jan Ranmal II, king of Navanagar, constructed both the lake and the palace sometime in the nineteenth century. The lake is a popular spot for both residents and visitors looking for a day of fun in the sun.

##### Home to thriving wildlife

The temple's most impressive feature is its cutting-edge and breathtaking design. You can find peace and quiet at the temple.

# Discover the Charm: 5 Places to Visit in Jamnagar with Family

Jamnagar, a historic city in Gujarat, is a treasure trove of cultural and natural beauty. Whether you are a history buff, a nature lover, or a spiritual seeker, Jamnagar offers a diverse range of attractions that promise an unforgettable experience. Here are five prominent Jamnagar tourist places that you should not miss.

## Lakhota Lake and Lakhota Palace

Lakhota Lake is a serene oasis in the heart of Jamnagar, offering a peaceful retreat from the hustle and bustle of city life. The lake is encircled by a picturesque walkway, making it a popular spot for morning and evening strolls. At the centre of the lake is the magnificent Lakhota Palace, a stunning structure built in the mid-19th century. The palace, which once served as a royal residence and military fort, now houses a museum showcasing artefacts, weaponry, and pottery from Jamnagar’s rich history. Visitors can also enjoy boating on the lake while admiring the architectural beauty of the palace.

## Marine National Park

The Marine National Park is a must-visit destination in Jamnagar. Located along the Gulf of Kutch, this park is India's first marine sanctuary renowned for its diverse marine life and vibrant coral reefs. The park consists of 42 islands, each with its unique ecosystem. During low tide, visitors can walk on the ocean floor and witness numerous marine species, including starfish, sea urchins, jellyfish, and octopuses. The Marine National Park is a haven for birdwatchers, with different migratory birds flocking to the area, especially during winter.

## Bala Hanuman Temple

The Bala Hanuman Temple is a spiritual landmark and a testament to Jamnagar’s deep-rooted devotion. Located on the southeastern side of Lakhota Lake, this temple is dedicated to Lord Hanuman. It holds a unique record in the Guinness Book of World Records for the continuous chanting of the Ram Dhun (the name of Lord Ram), which has been going on 24/7 since 1964. The temple is a hub of religious activity and offers a tranquil environment for devotees and visitors alike. The rhythmic chanting creates an aura of peace and divinity, making it a must-visit spot for spiritual seekers.

## Pratap Vilas Palace

Pratap Vilas Palace, an architectural marvel, reflects the grandeur of Jamnagar's royal heritage. This palace, constructed in the early 20th century, is an amalgamation of European, Indo-Saracenic, and Gujarati styles of architecture. The palace is adorned with intricate carvings, ornate pillars, and stunning frescoes. While the palace is not open to the public, the exterior itself is worth admiring for its regal design and historical significance. The surrounding gardens add to the charm, making it a picturesque spot for photography and relaxation.

## Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary

Birdwatchers and nature lovers will find paradise at Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary. Located about 12 km from Jamnagar, this sanctuary is home to over 300 species of resident and migratory birds. The unique blend of freshwater lakes, saltwater marshes, and mangroves creates the perfect habitat for birds like flamingos, pelicans, and painted storks. The best time to visit is during the winter months when the sanctuary is teeming with migratory birds. The bird sanctuary also has watchtowers and trails, allowing visitors to observe the birds up close.

Solarium, located in Jamnagar, was built in 1934 by late Lt. Col. Maharaja Jam Saheb Shri Sir Ranjitsinhji of Navanagar State and designed by French engineer Dr. Jean Saidam. It cost approximately Rs. 6 lakhs to build and is the only remaining solarium out of three that were originally built worldwide.

Solarium was sponsored by late Lt. Col. His highness Maharaja Jam Saheb Shri Sir Ranjitsinhji of Navanagar State in the year 1934. It was designed by Dr. Jean Saidam, a French Engineer. Approximately Rs. 6 lacs were spent to build this solarium. Actually there were total three solariums in the world. Two solariums located in France were destroyed during 2nd world war, due to bombarding and at present Jamnagar solarium is the only one in existence.
Total height of our solarium is 40 feet. The platform on which solarium cabins are built is mounted on a tower at a height of about 30 feet from the ground level. Platform is 114 feet long, having total 10 cabins for natural rays therapy. Its cabin size is 13 X 9 feet. The platform revolves in a horizontal plane and the patients bed in the cabin can simultaneously rotate vertically. Solarium is simultaneously adjusted every 10 to 15 minutes as per the movement of Sun in the sky and for this purpose a special arrangement is made in an observatory room to control this movements. Rotation of solarium is done by an electronic motor. However if the power supply fails, there is a pivoting system in the basement of the solarium by which a person can rotate it manually. To complete one full circle it takes 60 minutes.
Three types of rays therapy (i.e. Ultraviolet rays, Infrared rays and total radiation rays) are given in the treatment cabin by means of the special large concentrator glasses, which can concentrate two and a half time (2 ½ times) the sun rays. Special wavelength filter glasses are placed below in order to cut off the unwanted rays, so as the patient receives only desired rays for the treatment purpose. Due to unavailability of the concentrator glasses and filter glasses in India and abroad, and as the above original glasses were broken during last cyclone, the natural ray – therapy is not possible at present. Moreover all rays therapies are now available by prepared electrical gadgets. Therefore it has now only historical importance. It attracts large number of visitors from different parts of the country and abroad.

JADEJA R — Google review

Bhujio Kotho was built by Maharaja Jam Ranmalji II in 1852 on the bank of the Lakhota Lake as a draught relief structure. It is a 7 storeyed circular structure with 34 meter height. It is utilizing mainly for armoury storage and a watch tower in the past. First floor housed guns pointing in different directions through small windows. Upper floor has remnants of a huge water tank, used to supply water to the fort; and the Kotho. Top has a panoramic view of Jamnagar city.
Bhujio Kotho has a well from which water gushes out when you blow into a hole in the floor. A Large collection of armoury items including guns, swords and other weapons of war was also housed in the Kotho. The structure is made of lime masonry with "Otho Dongo" construction technic.
Bhujio Kotho suffered extensive damage due to earthquake of 2001. It is undergoing renovation by Jamnagar Municipal Corporation under guidance of Archaeology Department. Currently visitors are not allowed inside.

## A brief history of Jamnagar

Jamnagar was founded by Jam Rawal in 1540 and served as the capital of the princely state of Nawanagar. The city boasts a rich heritage influenced by Rajput rulers. Over the years, Jamnagar has flourished, becoming a hub for culture, trade, and spirituality. Its historic sites and architectural marvels reflect its glorious past.

## 10 best places to visit in Jamnagar

From pristine beaches to revered temples, Jamnagar offers diverse attractions for travellers. Here are the top ten places to explore in this vibrant city:

### 1\. Sivrajpur Beach

Sivrajpur Beach is a hidden gem known for its white sands and crystal-clear waters. It is an ideal spot for relaxation and water activities like swimming and snorkelling. The serene ambiance makes it a must-visit destination.

- **Location:** Sivrajpur Beach is located 12 km from Dwarka and about 70 km from Jamnagar city.
- **Entry fee:** There is no entry fee to visit Sivrajpur Beach, making it a budget-friendly destination.
- **Timing:** The beach is accessible from sunrise to sunset, offering picturesque views throughout the day.

### 2\. Bala Hanuman Temple

Famed for its continuous chanting of “Ram Dhun,” Bala Hanuman Temple is a spiritual haven. The temple holds a place in the Guinness World Records for the longest uninterrupted prayer.

- **Location:** Located near Lakhota Lake, the temple is easily accessible within Jamnagar.
- **Entry fee:** Entry to Bala Hanuman Temple is free for all visitors.
- **Timing:** The temple remains open daily from 6:00 am to 10:00 pm.

### 3\. Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary

A paradise for bird watchers, Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary hosts over 300 species of birds. Its unique ecosystem of freshwater and saltwater attracts migratory birds.

- **Location:** Situated 10 km from Jamnagar, the sanctuary is well-connected by road.
- **Entry fee:** The entry fee is Rs. 40 for Indian citizens and Rs. 100 for foreign nationals.
- **Timing:** The sanctuary is open from 6:00 am to 6:00 pm.

### 4\. Dwarkadhish Temple

One of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites, Dwarkadhish Temple is dedicated to Lord Krishna. Its stunning architecture and spiritual significance draw millions of devotees annually.

- **Location:** The temple is in Dwarka, about 130 km from Jamnagar.
- **Entry fee:** There is no entry fee to visit the temple.
- **Timing:** The temple is open from 6:30 am to 1:00 pm and 5:00 pm to 9:30 pm.

### 5\. Lakhota Talav

Lakhota Talav, a picturesque lake in Jamnagar, is surrounded by a fort and museum. It is a popular spot for evening strolls and bird watching.

- **Location:** Located in the heart of Jamnagar city, it is easily accessible.
- **Entry fee:** The entry fee to the museum is Rs. 10 for adults.
- **Timing:** The museum is open from 10:00 am to 5:30 pm, while the lake can be visited anytime.

### 6\. Baps Shri Swaminarayan Mandir

This intricately designed temple is a symbol of spirituality and devotion. The peaceful surroundings add to its charm.

- **Location:** The temple is located in Jamnagar city.
- **Entry fee:** Entry is free for all visitors.
- **Timing:** The temple is open daily from 7:00 am to 8:30 pm.

### 7\. Pirotan Island

Pirotan Island is part of the Marine National Park and offers a unique glimpse of marine life, including coral reefs and rare aquatic species.

- **Location:** Situated 20 km from Jamnagar, it is accessible by boat.
- **Entry fee:** There is no entry fee, but boat charges apply.
- **Timing:** Visits are allowed only during low tide, with timings varying daily.

### 8\. Adinath Mandir Jain Temple

A serene Jain temple, Adinath Mandir, is known for its architectural beauty and peaceful environment. It is a must-visit for spiritual seekers.

- **Location:** The temple is located near Jamnagar city.
- **Entry fee:** There is no entry fee for visitors.
- **Timing:** The temple is open from 6:00 am to 9:00 pm.

### 9\. Beyt Dwarka Beach

Beyt Dwarka Beach offers pristine views and tranquillity. It is an excellent place for a day trip and is popular among families.

- **Location:** Situated near Dwarka, it is about 130 km from Jamnagar.
- **Entry fee:** There is no entry fee for the beach.
- **Timing:** The beach is accessible from sunrise to sunset.

### 10\. Ranjitsagar Dam

Ranjitsagar Dam, located amidst scenic surroundings, is perfect for picnics and photography. The lush greenery and calm waters are its highlights.

- **Location:** The dam is located 20 km from Jamnagar city.
- **Entry fee:** Entry is free for visitors.
- **Timing:** The dam is open from 6:00 am to 6:00 pm.

### Overview

Known to the world as the Jewel of Kathiawar, Jamnagar is an offbeat tourist holiday destination in Gujarat brimming with the ornate building, pristine coastal region and picturesque lakes. Located on the coast of Gulf of Kutch, Jamnagar is the largest city on the western region of India. In the heart of the city, there is a fort amidst the lake while the coastal area is covered with an oil refinery, which is largest in the world, beaches and spectacular Marine National Park. With exquisite Rajput architecture and pristine coastline, Jamnagar is Gujarat's lesser-known gems.

Jamnagar (former Nawanagar) came into limelight 444 years ago when it was established on the bank of Nagmatti and Rangmattiriver by the Shri Jam Raval, who is deemed as one of the heirs of Lord Krishna. The city witnessed a major makeover during the rule of KS Ranjit Sinhi, who was also a famous cricketer. Ranjit Sinhi, along with the famous architecture Edward Lutyens, reconstructed the city in European style and made many gardens, lakes and important landmarks like Willingdon Crescent, Pratap Vilas Palace, and Solarium were built under his patronage. After such major uplift, the city started getting the attention of people and was captioned "Jewel of Khathiyawad." Legends even say that Lord Krishna used to stay in this city when he moved out of Mathura.

There are multiple attractions and places to see in Jamnagar but one place that dominates the old city is the Lakhota Fort, the original seat of Jamnagar ruler and Ranmal Lake, which surrounds the fort. Respiratory in the fort showcases finest sculptures from the nearby evacuation sites, dating from 9th to 18th centuries. Within the walking distance from Ranmal lake is the Kotha Bastion, which was once the storehouse of rulers' weapons'.

In the heart of the old city is the circular Darbar Gadh, an area where the rulers of Jamshedpur received members of the general public and heard their grievances. The lanes leading off from Darbar Gadh are worth exploring as one can see people putting together different colours and making the finest Bandhani fabric. In this area, there are two Jain temples, the Shantinath and Adinath temple, entirely covered with mirror, gold leaf, murals and mosaics, one cannot ignore these temples. Close to the temples is the 19th century Ratan Bhai mosque, its doors inlaid with mother-of-pearl.

A place of eternal beauty, Jamnagar is also an ornithologist's paradise. Jamnagar has a start of the art ecosystem called Khijadiya Bird Sanctuary. This birding paradise houses many avian species like the black-winged kite, brahminy kite, great thick-knee, common greenshank, grey francolin, etc. A major part of Jamshedpur is hugging the Gulf of Kutch. The coastal area of Jamnagar is famed for Marine National Park. Evidently the first marine park in India, this is the only place in India where one can see coral reefs without diving in the water. Sights like pods of dolphin swimming in the water and migratory birds crossing the azure Gulf of Kutch are quite prominent. One can also visit the nearby beaches like Narara beach, Balachadi beach, and Madhi beach to bask in the sun or for long walks.

Making brass utensils, and tie-dye fabrics were the main occupations of Jamnagar people until there was emergence in the refinery sector. Refinery complex owned by Reliance in Jamnagar is the world's largest oil refinery in the world, which on an average 660,000 barrels of crude oil per day. Earlier, Jamnagar was also called 'pearl city' as many as it was a pearl fishing center with one of the biggest pearl fisheries in the world.

Jamnagar is a mixture of ancient sites and natural beauty, with a healthy dash of breathtaking sites thrown in. From lakes to places to gardens to museums, Jamnagar has a lot to offer to tourists.

## Jamnagar

In 1540 A.D. Jam Raval founded the city of Jamnagar as the capital of the Princely State of Nawanagar. The city was established around the Ranmal Lake and at the confluence of the rivers Rangmati and Nagmati. The city was later refurbished by Maharaja Kumar Shri Ranjitsinhji in the 1920s and later came to be known as Jamnagar or “the city of Jams”. The word 'Jam' means 'king' and this area was ruled by the Jadeja Rajput rulers, who were thought to be the descendants of Krishna’s Yadava clan. Lord Krishna had relocated the Yadavas from Mathura to establish his kingdom in Dwarka which falls under the Jamnagar district.

### The Foundation stone of Jamnagar

Jam Lakaji, father of Jam Rawal was awarded twelve villages by Bahadur Shah. Later, Jam Rawal moved to Kathiawar and founded the city of Nawanagar (the new town). Under the rule of Jam Vibhaji in 1852, the city progressed due to the establishment of schools, hospitals, and the railway line to Rajkot.

![ranjit singh ji] 

### Maharaja Kumar Shri Ranjitsinhji

Maharaja Kumar Shri Ranjitsinhji is the world famous cricket personality from Jamnagar. He has a very important contribution in the development of Jamnagar. He ruled from 1907-33 and in 1914, with the help of an architect, Sir Edward Lutynes he reconstructed the city. He was very much influenced by the European Architecture, and he redesigned the city according to the European style. The previously walled city became open and the houses started having standardized architecture for which it is described to have a uniform look. Jamnagar got the title of the Paris of India. Many important landmarks like the Willingdon Crescent, Pratap Vilas Palace and Solarium were built under his patronage. The Bedi port was constructed during his time and the railway connections were also further developed. Long time ago, Jamnagar was famous as a small pearl fishing town. It was and still is a famous centre of the traditional techniques of tie-dye and bandhani, two very time consuming processes to colour fabrics. It is believed that for the last 500 years this city has mastered the art of these techniques.

### Tourist Attractions of Jamnagar

Jamnagar has a huge reserve of natural parks and sanctuaries. The one and only marine sanctuary in India, the Marine National Park, is near Jamnagar, on the coral reef island of Pirotan. Khijada Bird Sanctuary, Gaga Wildlife Sanctuary and Peter Scott Nature Park are also some other eco-systems to visit in Jamnagar. Jamnagar also has four famous marble Jain temples: Vardhman Shah's Temple, Raisi Shah's Temple, Sheth's Temple and Vasupujya Swami's Temple. The Bala Hanuman temple in Jamnagar is very renowned as it is in the list of The Guinness Book of World Records for the longest continuous chanting of "Ram Dhun", since 1 August 1964. The Lakhota Tower is another tourist spot in the Lakhota Lake and was built for drought relief during the reign of Jam Ranmalji. Ranjitsagar Dam, Pratap Vilas Palace, Ratan Bai Masjid, Darbar Gadh, Bhidbhanjan Temple, Khijadia Temple, Bohra Hajira, Bhujio Kotho, Manekbai Muktidham, Rozi port and Bedi port are some more places in Jamnagar to visit. Associated with names of the Jadeja Rajput Jams and the famous cricketer Ranjitsinhji, Jamanagar is definitely a nice place to visit.
