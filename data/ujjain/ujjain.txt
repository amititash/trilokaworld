## Ancient City Ujjain
Ujjain, a city located in the state of Madhya Pradesh, is a divine land that offers a unique blend of solitude and spiritual experiences. This temple town is renowned for hosting one of the largest religious gatherings in the world, Simhastha (Kumbh Mela), which occurs every 12 years on the banks of the Shipra river.
Also known as the temple town of Madhya Pradesh, the city is home to more than 100 temples and numerous pilgrimage sites of different eras. Here are some famous temples and must-see places in Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh for tourists to explore.
## Places To Visit In Ujjain
### Mahakaleshwar Ujjain
The biggest attraction of Ujjain is the[Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga Temple]. It is one of the 12 jyotirlingas, where Bhasma aarti is a daily ritual that you must not miss and this is what Ujjain is famous for. The process begins with the 'Abhishek' or the ritual bath of the shivling then smearing it with multiple offerings like curd, honey, sandal paste and finally cleaning it with milk and water. Finally, the Shiva linga is decorated with ash and shringaar material. To attend the Bhasma aarti, make sure to go through the prior registration process from the authorized website[https://shrimahakaleshwar.com/].
According to ancient legends, the foundation of the temple was laid by the Lord Brahma (God of Creation). It was considered by historians that there was no Shikhara on the top of the temple till Gupta period. However, later on, a shikhara was constructed and it enhanced the beauty of the temple.
### Mahakal Lok Corridor
To develop tourism, preserve ancient heritage, and provide world-class modern facilities to the pilgrims, the 'Mahakal Lok' project has been implemented in Ujjain.
The length of the[**Mahakal Lok Corridor**] is more than 900 meters. It is surrounded by the old Rudrasagar Lake, which is also a part of the development project. Once you enter the corridor, you will see 108 pillars, around 200 statues, and murals that display stories of Shiva.
### Kal Bhairav Temple
By crossing the bridge over the Shipra river, close to Bhagirathi caves, you'll reach the very famous Kal Bhairav temple. The devotion toward the ashta bhairava or eight attendants of Lord Shiva is an integral part of Shiva Bhakti (devotional worship) in Ujjain and since Kal Bhairav is chief among those eight, this temple is particularly important in the town.
The temple is believed to be built by King Bhadrasen of Mahismati, now Maheshwar. The significance of the temple is mentioned in the Hindu scriptures like Avanti Khanda of the Skanda Purana. The offering to the deity includes flowers, coconut, incense and especially liquor. One can purchase the offerings from the shops out of the temple premises and can hand it to the priest in the garbhagriha. The priest then offers half of the liquor to the idol of Kal Bhairava and returns the rest to the devotee as prashad.
### Bada Ganapati Temple
The temple is located next to the campus of Mahakal Temple on the path leading to Harsiddhi Temple. It houses a gigantic 4 meters tall statue of Lord Ganesha. There is also a four-faced brass idol of Lord Vishnu in the inner chamber.
### Harsiddhi Temple
Goddess Annapurna is seated between the idols of Mahalaxmi and Mahasaraswati in the temple. Being the significant deity worshiped in the temple, the goddess Annapurna is painted in vibrant vermilion. The pillars in the Ardhamandapa of the temple date back to the 11th century exhibiting their antiquity. The present temple was restored during the Maratha period and two soaring deepastambhas, each adorned with 1008 lamps, were made to stand outside the entrance. The glory of the lamps is best sighted during Navaratri festival which is celebrated with much zeal in Ujjain.
### Ved Shala
The sacred city of New Ujjain is home to Jantar Mantar or Vedh Shala. Maharaja Jai Singh II, also known as Sawai Jai Singh constructed an observatory in 1725 that included 13 architectural astronomical instruments. He constructed five observatories in the eighteenth century in the north Indian cities of Shahjahanabad (Delhi), Jaipur, Ujjain, Mathura, and Varanasi. One of these five observatories, the Vedshala, was constructed while he served as the governor of Ujjain. Vedh Shala was built with the intention of determining eclipses, local time, height (of the location), as well as the declination of the Sun, stars, and planets.

## Best time to visit Ujjain
Ujjain can be explored anytime in the year. However, the best time to visit Ujjain is during festivals like Mahashivratri, Mondays of Savan month, Nag Panchami and especially during Kartik Mela (yearly) and Kumbh Mela (once in 12 years).
## How to reach Ujjain?
**By Air:**The closest airport is Devi Ahilya Bai International Airport, Indore at a distance of 55 km, which is well connected through regular flights to major cities like Delhi, Mumbai, Pune, Bhopal, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Nagpur, Raipur, and Kolkata.
**By Rail:**The Ujjain railway station is located in the heart of the town. It is connected to all the major cities within the state as well as the rest of the country with both commuter and express trains. The main bus stand at Dewas Gate is located 600 meters from the railway station.
**By Road:**Both private and state-operated direct buses ply between Ujjain and Indore, Omkareshwar, Maheshwar, Burhanpur, Dhar and Bhopal. There are two main bus stands in the city. The Dewas Gate bus stand is at the heart of the town from where one can easily hire other modes of transport as well, including auto-rickshaws, tempos and cycle rickshaws or cabs. The other bus stand, Nanakheda Bus Terminus is at a distance of approximately 5 kilometers.

 History and Cultural Significance of Ujjain 
 
 The history of Ujjain dates back thousands of years, making it one of the oldest living cities in the world. Known as Avantika in ancient times, Ujjain was an important political and cultural hub during the Mauryan and Gupta empires. It was also one of the Sapta Puris — the seven sacred cities of Hinduism — believed to grant salvation to devotees. 
 
 According to Hindu mythology, Lord Shiva resides here as Mahakaleshwar, the presiding deity of the city. The temple of Mahakaleshwar is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas in India, attracting millions of pilgrims every year. Apart from its religious significance, Ujjain was also a center of astronomical studies, famously associated with King Vikramaditya and the legendary scholar Kalidasa. 
 
 Today, Ujjain continues to carry forward its glorious legacy through its vibrant temples, ghats, and festivals, especially the Kumbh Mela, which draws devotees from around the globe. 
 
 Top Ujjain Tourist Places You Must Visit 
 
 If you’re planning a trip, here are some of the best Ujjain Tourist Places that define the city’s spiritual and cultural essence. 
 
 1. Mahakaleshwar Temple 
 
 The heart and soul of Ujjain, the Mahakaleshwar Temple is one of India’s most powerful and revered Shiva shrines. The temple’s architecture beautifully blends Maratha, Bhumija, and Chalukya styles. The highlight here is the early morning Bhasma Aarti, where the idol of Lord Shiva is worshipped with sacred ash. Devotees believe that visiting Mahakaleshwar frees one from all sins and leads to spiritual liberation. 
 
 2. Ram Ghat 
 
 Situated along the River Kshipra, Ram Ghat is among the oldest and most famous ghats in Ujjain. It becomes the main site during the Kumbh Mela, where millions take a holy dip to purify their souls. The evenings here are magical — as diyas (lamps) float on the water, chants fill the air, and the ghat glows with divine light. It’s a place that captures the very essence of Ujjain’s spirituality. 
 
 3. Harsiddhi Temple 
 
 Dedicated to Goddess Harsiddhi, this temple is considered one of the Shakti Peethas. The temple stands close to Mahakaleshwar and holds deep mythological importance. The twin lamps in the temple courtyard remain lit throughout the night, creating a mesmerizing sight for visitors. The temple is also known for its Navratri celebrations, which attract large crowds every year. 
 
 4. Kal Bhairav Temple 
 
 Another significant shrine among Ujjain Tourist Places is the Kal Bhairav Temple, dedicated to Lord Bhairava — a fierce form of Lord Shiva. This temple is unique because devotees offer liquor to the deity as part of rituals. It’s believed that Lord Bhairava protects the city and its people from negative forces. 
 
 5. Sandipani Ashram 
 
 Located a short distance from the main city, Sandipani Ashram holds great importance in Hindu mythology. It is said to be the place where Lord Krishna, along with his brother Balarama and friend Sudama, received education from Guru Sandipani. The ashram still preserves ancient artifacts and markings believed to be from that era. 
 
 6. Kaal Sarp Dosh Nivaran Temple 
 
 Many people visit Ujjain to perform rituals for Kaal Sarp Dosh Nivaran, a special puja believed to remove obstacles caused by planetary positions. This temple is one of the main centers for performing such rituals, attracting devotees seeking peace and progress in life. 
 
 7. Bhartrihari Caves 
 
 Nestled near the banks of the Kshipra River, these ancient caves are named after the philosopher-poet Bhartrihari, the stepbrother of King Vikramaditya. He renounced worldly pleasures and meditated here. The calm surroundings of the caves make them ideal for meditation and introspection. 
 
 8. Chintaman Ganesh Temple 
 
 Among the oldest Ujjain Tourist Places, this temple is dedicated to Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. It is said that the idol here is self-manifested (Swayambhu) and extremely powerful. Locals and travelers alike come here to seek blessings for success and happiness. 
 
 Ghats of Ujjain: The Spiritual Heartbeat of the City 
 
 The ghats of Ujjain are not just bathing spots; they are sacred spaces where faith meets nature. The most prominent among them is Ram Ghat, followed by Mangalnath Ghat, Bhartari Ghat, and Kshipra Ghat. 
 
 During dawn and dusk, these ghats come alive with the sound of conch shells, temple bells, and Vedic chants. Pilgrims gather to take a dip in the holy river, believing it washes away sins and brings spiritual purity. The evening aarti on Ram Ghat is a sight to behold — an unforgettable experience for every visitor. 
 
 Festivals and Culture of Ujjain 
 
 The culture of Ujjain is deeply rooted in spirituality and devotion. The city hosts several religious events throughout the year, but none is as grand as the Simhastha Kumbh Mela, held once every 12 years. It’s one of the largest spiritual gatherings on Earth, symbolizing the unity of faith and tradition. 
 
 Other major festivals like Mahashivratri, Navratri, and Diwali are celebrated with great enthusiasm. Streets glow with lamps, temples are beautifully decorated, and chants of mantras fill the air. These festivals make Ujjain one of the most vibrant and spiritually uplifting cities in India. 
 
 How to Reach Ujjain 
 
 Ujjain is well connected by road, rail, and air. The nearest airport is in Indore, about 55 km away, from where regular taxis and buses are available. Ujjain Junction railway station connects the city to major cities like Delhi, Mumbai, and Bhopal. Once in the city, autos and cabs make local travel easy and convenient. 
 
 Best Time to Visit Ujjain Tourist Places 
 
 The best time to explore Ujjain Tourist Places is from October to March, when the weather is pleasant and ideal for sightseeing. During this period, you can comfortably visit temples, ghats, and historical sites. Avoid the summer months (April–June), as temperatures can get quite high. 
 
 If you wish to experience the city’s true spiritual energy, plan your visit during Mahashivratri or the Kumbh Mela, when Ujjain turns into a divine spectacle of faith and festivity. 
 
 Why Travelers Choose to Visit Ujjain 
 
 Ujjain is more than a well-known pilgrimage city. It offers a direct look into long-standing traditions, temple practices, and historical developments that have shaped the region. At Mahakaleshwar, along the river ghats and across its older neighborhoods, daily life continues in ways that reflect a deep connection to history and belief. 
 
 Visiting Ujjain allows you to observe how culture, worship, and community function together in one place. For many travelers, the experience becomes meaningful because of the city’s consistent way of preserving and practicing its heritage.

## Religious & Spiritual Significance of Ujjain

Ujjain is one of the most important cities in India due to its religious and spiritual significance. It is one of the four cities where the Maha Kumbh Mela, the world's largest religious gathering, takes place every 12 years. Additionally, it is considered one of the seven sacred cities in India, believed to be visited once in a lifetime to attain moksha, or liberation from the cycle of reincarnation.

Ujjain is home to the Shri Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, which are sacred lingams of Shiva. The city is also known as Mahakal Ki Nagari, or the City of Mahakal, referring to Shiva's fierce form. Additionally, Ujjain is an important center for Vedic studies, with many ancient sages studying the sacred Vedas in the city. Situated on the banks of the Shipra River, Ujjain is also home to the Kalbhairava Temple, another significant pilgrimage site dedicated to the fierce form of Shiva. Ujjain is a vibrant and historic city with a rich religious and cultural heritage. It is from popular points of interest for pilgrims and tourists alike.

## Popular Points of Tourist Interest in Ujjain

Ujjain, home to magnificent temples and one of the four cities that host the world's largest gathering, the Maha Kumbh, requires no introduction for its significance. This city, regarded as one of the most religious and spiritual places in India, is said to be the center of the land and served as the reference point for calculating time in the surrounding regions. Established thousands of years ago, Ujjain holds a prominent position among India's oldest cities. Here are the best places to visit in Ujjain that define its significance and are not to be missed:

## Mahakaleshwar Temple

The Mahakaleshwar temple is a resplendent shrine dedicated to Lord Shiva, the destroyer of evil. It is one of the top tourist places in Ujjain, a holy city in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh. The temple is renowned for its intricate architecture, which is a blend of Hindu, Jain, and Mughal styles. The temple is home to one of the 12 Jyotirlingas, which are sacred lingams (pillars) of Lord Shiva. The Mahakaleshwar lingam is believed to be self-manifested, making it one of the most revered Jyotirlingas in India.

- Location: Jaisinghpura, Ujjain
- Time required to visit: 1-2 Hours

## Kal Bhairav Temple

The Shree Kal Bhairav Temple is dedicated to a fierce form of Lord Shiva known as Kalbhairav. He is believed to be the god of annihilation and is responsible for providing strength and power to the devotees. The temple follows special and strict rituals, and many visitors attend the holy rituals daily. The temple is believed to have been built 6,000 years ago and holds great importance in the Hindu religion. You can talk to the locals and listen to detailed stories about Kal Bhairav.

## Kandariya Mahadeva Temple

The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple in Khajuraho is one of the popular points of interest and among the best places to visit. This Temple grand tribute to Lord Shiva. It is the largest temple in the area, known for its vibrant architecture. The temple walls are adorned with intricate carvings depicting men and women in intimate poses. These erotic sculptures showcase the celebration of love and passion.

- Location: Bhairavgarh, Ujjain
- Time required to visit: 30 Minutes

## Ram Ghat/Shipra River

Ram Ghat, located on the Shipra River, is one of the must-visit places in Ujjain. The holy river holds great significance in Ujjain's history and provides a sense of peace when you're in its vicinity. Many people take a holy dip in the river, which is believed to wash away unintentional sins. However, even if you are not religious, you can still enjoy the serenity of this massive water body. Evening prayers at Ram Ghat are also an event not to be missed, as they are performed daily at dusk.

- Location: Kartik Chowk, Ujjain
- Time required to Visit: 1 Hour

## Sandipani Ashram/Gomti Kund

The Maharshi Sandipani Ashram has a remarkable story and is steeped in great history. It is believed that Lord Krishna received his teachings at this ashram from Sage Sandipani. The ashram exudes serenity and calmness, creating a deep connection between visitors and the environment. Just 500 meters away from the Sandipani Ashram is Gomti Kund, which is also associated with Lord Krishna. The holy water of Gomti Kund is said to have been used by ancient sages for sacred rituals. While you cannot take a dip in the Kund, you are allowed to touch the water or collect some for yourself.

- Location: Indira Nagar, Ujjain
- Time required to visit: 1-2 Hours

## Jantar Mantar

Jantar Mantar, also known as Vedh Shala, is an observatory built for studying astronomical phenomena and geolocation of heavenly bodies. Ujjain held significant importance in the study of time, serving as the center for measuring time differences in India. The Vedh Sabha Observatory was constructed in 1725 and features several structural instrumental devices that can still be used to calculate their intended purposes.

- Location: Jabsinghpura, Ujjain
- Time required to Visit: 1-2 Hours

## Bhartrihari Caves

The Bhatrihari cave is another mysterious place that will leave you in awe of the historical existence of ancient India. Ujjain boasts a rich history and has been home to many remarkable individuals, one of whom is Sage Bhatrihari, the step-brother of King Vikramaditya. Known for his brilliance, he found solace in these caves. Spending an extended period within their confines, he achieved a peaceful state of mind and even penned literary masterpieces during his time there.

Upon venturing deeper into the cave, you will find yourself immersed in a completely different atmosphere. It is truly unbelievable how these caves were constructed in ancient times, without the aid of modern equipment, and have withstood the test of time for thousands of years.

_**Note:** Keep a torch with yourself as it is dark inside the cave._

- Location: Barnagar road, Ujjain
- Time Required to Visit: 1-2 hours

## Bade GaneshJi Ka Mandir

Bade Ganeshji ka Mandir is another famous place in Ujjain, located not far from the Ujjain Junction. The temple is renowned for its colossal idol of Lord Ganesh, standing at almost 20 feet tall. Within the temple premises, the authorities also offer lessons in Sanskrit and astrology. Additionally, the temple features a grand idol of Lord Hanuman with five faces, believed to have been installed even before the idol of Lord Ganesh.

- Location: Jaisinghpura, Ujjain
- Time Required to Visit: 30 minutes.

## Best Time to Visit Ujjain

The best time to visit Ujjain is during the winter and spring months (October to March) when the weather is pleasant or cold. This period is ideal for exploring bustling places throughout the day on foot. However, during winters, the nights can be chilly, so it is advisable to pack extra warm clothes for the cold nights.

Summer is not a favorable time to plan a visit to Ujjain as the temperatures can reach around 40°C, while the monsoon season brings humid weather. Therefore, it is recommended to avoid planning a trip to Ujjain between the months of May and September.

**By Train:** Ujjain has its own railway station, which offers train services to major cities across India.

**By Road:** NH3 connects Ujjain to Indore, with a road distance of 55 kilometers. The journey on this route typically takes around 1 hour and 30 minutes.

# Top 9 Places to visit in Ujjain | Madhya Pradesh Tourism
By[Joydeep Phukan]

Are you planning a trip to Ujjain in Madhya Pradesh, India?
In this article, you will learn about the top 9 places to visit in Ujjain &#8211; temples, ghats, kund and caves.
Save time! Jump into a topic →[Toggle] 
**First, let us know about UJJAIN**
Ujjain is a city in the Malwa region in the state of Madhya Pradesh in central India.
From the Mauryan Empire to the Mughals and then to Maratha Rule &#8211; Ujjain has seen it all. You will find cultural structures related to all three.
Ujjain is famous for its spirituality and temples. People believe that Ujjain has existed since the Pre-Mahararata Era. Avanti, Avanitaka and Avantikapur are the other names for Ujjain.
A[holiday tour to Ujjain] will make sure you have the most authentic cultural and spiritual experience in Madhya Pradesh.
Ujjain is at a distance of about 192 km from the state capital Bhopal and 55 km from Indore.
The city&#8217;s sacred river Shipra, the ancient temples, lakes and festivals like Kumbh Mela make Ujjain one of the most revered pilgrimage destinations for Hindus with a heritage that dates back to the Vedic era.
## Top 9 Places to Visit in Ujjain
![Top 9 Places to Visit in Ujjain - Photos of Temples, Ghats, Kunds and Caves] Places in Ujjain you must visit.
### 1. Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga Temple
One of the twelve jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva, Mahakaleshwar Temple is one of the most visited tantra temples of the Hindus in India.
Photo by Vikram Chouhan / Unsplash
The architechtural styles of Chalukya, Bhumija, and Marathas can be seen on it . It is believed that in the temple Lord Shiva in lingam is self-manifested.
Amplifying the spiritual experience, you can spend time at the Rudra Sagar Lake located beside it.
Rudrasagar Lake. Photo by Himanshu Tiwari / Unsplash
### 2. Ram Ghat
The holy Shipra River attracts a huge number of devoted Hindu pilgrims at Ram Ghhat in Ujjain.
It is one of the four sacred sites where the famous Kumbh Mela is held. Apart from the mela, attending the evening aarti at the ghat is a wholesome spiritual experience.
Among other temples at Ram Ghat, Chitragupta temple is the most revered one.
Cleanse your soul by taking a holy dip at the ghat’s Shipra River. The river is believed to have originated from the Varaha &#8211; the third incarnation of Lord Vishnu, according to the Hindu texts.
### 3. Kal Bhairav Temple
Kal Bhairav Temple is dedicated to Kal Bhairav. He is the city’s guardian deity. The interesting thing about this temple is that the deity is believed to drink alcohol.
You can also learn about the Tantra cult here. The temple does not have idols or images.
The deity of the temple is a natural rock formation beautifully adorned with a Maratha-style turban and vermilion.
### 4. Jantar Mantar
Jantar Mantar is an architectural marvel and an observatory. It was built by Maharaja Jai Singh in the seventeenth century.
Its outdoor complex is dotted with many huge astronomy tools that have been designed to be utilized by the naked eye.
It offers important insights into the movement of the sun and planets. The complex houses many interesting monuments, such as Nadivalaya Yantra, Shanku Yantra, Digyansha Yantra, and Samrat Yantra.
### 5. Bhairogarh
Bhairogarh is a quaint village located in the suburbs of Ujjain, between Mangal Nath and Kal Bhairav temples.
It has been known to be the hub of batik for hundreds of years. The village houses more than eight hundred artisans who have mastery in traditional batik printing.
This unique painting style is carried out on cushion covers, scarves, sheets, saris, napkins, handkerchiefs, and more!
The village is also famed for having remnants of structures that date back to the third and second centuries BC.
### 6. Mangalnath Temple
According to Matsya Purana, Mangalnath Temple is the birthplace of Mangal. The temple is situated on a hill. From the top, you can see the beautiful Shipra River too.
The temple is constructed of red-hued sandstone. It is believed to have been built on the spot where Shiva and the demon Andhakasura had a fierce battle.
A drop of sweat dropped on the ground during the battle from the forehead of Lord Shiva leading to the formation of the Shiva Linga.
### 7. Kaliadeh Palace
Kalideh Palace is also known asKDPalace.
The Sultan of Mandu built the palace during the time of Mahmud Khilji in 1458 AD.The magnificent Persian architecture can be seen in the palace’s central dome.
The palace was damaged during wars. However, it was restored in 1920 by Maharaja Sir Madho Rao Scindia. Earlier Sindhia dynasty used to stay here when they visited Ujjain.
It is located on the river banks of Shipra. One can enjoy a perfect view of the river flowing from both sides.
### 8. Gomti Kund
According to Hindu mythology, Gomti Kund holds holy waters found in different parts of the world. Devotees fill their bottles with the water of this kund and take it home.
It is believed that all the holy waters were summoned by Lord Krishna leading to the creation of the Gomti Kund.
In the Puranas, The Gomti Kund is mentioned to be the source of water supply to the Sandipani ashram in the olden days where Lord Krishna received education under Guru Sandipani.
### 9. Bhartrihari Caves
Bhartrihari Caves are at the banks of the River Shipra near Gadkalika Temple. These caves have been named after a famous sage and poet who lived here in the eleventh century.
It is also believed that King Vikramaditya, Ujjain’s legendary ruler has a close association with these caves. The entrances of the caves are very narrow making it a bit tricky to access.
Inside the caves, there are rooms, idols, stone pillars, and images of Hindu deities.The caves also house Bhagirathi’s statue in a sitting position.
### Conclusion
I hope this article on the Top 9 places to visit in Ujjain has helped you plan your spiritual and cultural trip.
Be it spirituality, history, and culture &#8211; Ujjain has it all. Its significance dates back to the Vedic era, making it a popular pilgrimage destination.
Among its must-visit sites, the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga Temple stands as a spiritual epicentre, while the Ram Ghat on the Shipra River offers a sacred and serene atmosphere.
The Kal Bhairav Temple provides a glimpse into unique tantric traditions, and the Jantar Mantar observatory showcases remarkable ancient scientific achievements.
For history enthusiasts, the Kaliadeh Palace and Bhartrihari Caves reveal layers of Ujjain&#8217;s history.
Bhairogarh village celebrates the enduring art of batik, and Mangalnath Temple enchants it with its mythological ties.
