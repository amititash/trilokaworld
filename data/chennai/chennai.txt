## Temples are an art form

The capital of the south [Indian] state of [Tamil Nadu], [Chennai] is a large, busy city with an exceptional dining scene and mix of architectural styles. There are plenty of things to do in Chennai, from taking sunrise beach strolls to sampling local street food, but history and [architecture] are its biggest draws. You’ll find a mix of colorful, ancient temples built in the Dravidian tradition and European-style churches and government buildings that were constructed in the Raj era, when Chennai was known by its former name: Madras. Other popular things to do in Chennai include browsing the 46 galleries of the [Government Museum], exploring the tropical forest of Guindy National Park, and soaking up the sun on the Coromandel Coast.

When to visit

Winter is the best time to visit Chennai. While December does get some rainfall, January and February are mostly dry. Temperatures begin to climb in March and April, peaking around 100°F (38°C) in May.

The monsoon season starts in June, bringing with it heavy rainfall that can continue well into November. The city is equipped to deal with downpours, but the rains can put a damper on travel plans.

Getting around

There are lots of ways to get around Chennai, from relying on taxis, which can be arranged through rideshare apps, to using auto-rickshaws, which are easy to hail on the street. Just make sure to agree to a price before you get in. Bus service is not always the most comfortable, but urban trains and the Chennai Metro are both reliable ways to get around.

Traveler tips

If you're craving some relief from city life, head to Amethyst, a complex featuring a boutique, flower shop, art gallery, and café—all housed in a historic granary. The Wild Garden café draws in the most visitors, not only for its extensive menu of espresso drinks and European food but also for its ambiance. Grab a table at the large leafy garden or sit inside the antiques-filled dining room.

## Attractions

\[ [edit] \]

### Beaches

\[ [edit] \]

- **[Marina Beach] ** is a 13-km-long urban beach along the [Bay of Bengal] that runs from [Fort St. George] in the north up to [Foreshore Estate] in the south. It is India's longest beach and one of the world's longest beaches and attracts around 50,000 visitors during weekends. Attractions at the Marina include the [Chennai Lighthouse], [MGR Memorial], [Anna Memorial] and Jayalalitha Memorial. The Marina's 6 km promenade includes statues of several historical figures including [Mahatma Gandhi], [Annie Besant], [Robert Caldwell], [Thiruvalluvar], [Bharathiyar] and [Kamrajar].MGR Samadhi in Marina Beach
- [**Elliot's Beach**], also known as Besant Nagar Beach, begins where the marina ends. The beach is famous for its calm atmosphere and is preferable among morning walkers. The iconic Karl Schmidt memorial, named after the Dutch sailor who lost his life in the process of saving others from drowning is located at the heart of Elliot's Beach.

### Museums

\[ [edit] \]

- [**Government Museum, Egmore**] Established in 1851, the museum consisting of six buildings and 46 galleries covers an area of around 16.25 acres (66,000 m2) of land. The objects displayed in the museum cover a variety of artifacts and objects covering diverse fields including [archeology], [numismatics], [zoology], [natural history], [sculptures], palm-leaf [manuscripts] and [Amravati] paintings. The Government Museum Complex in [Egmore] also houses the [Connemara Public Library] and the National Art Gallery. Connemara Public Library is one of the four National Depository [libraries] which receive a copy of all books, newspapers and periodicals published in India. The National Art Gallery building is one of the finest Indo-sarcenic type of architectures in the country.[Chennai Rail Museum] 
- [**Chennai Rail Museum**] a [railway museum] in Perambur which has a rich rail heritage of [India] with the host of both technical and heritage exhibits with a sizable collection of [steam engines] belonging to various decades of the [British Raj]. The museum was opened on 16 April 2002 and located on 6.25 acres on the premises [Integral Coach Factory] near [Villivakkam]. Most of the older models were manufactured by the [North British Locomotive Company] [\[1\]] and some of the collection dates back more than one hundred years as it covers the railway history of South India.[\[2\]] A toy train offers rides around the premises on regular days. Museum remains open from 10.00 am to 6.00 pm (Last entry 5.30 pm) Tuesday to Sunday and remains closed on every Monday and National Holidays. The Indoor Art Gallery is now fully renovated and opened to the public.
- [**Birla Planetarium**], a modern planetarium that provides a virtual tour of the night sky and holding cosmic shows on a specially perforated hemispherical [aluminium] inner dome. It is located inside the Periyar Science and Technology Centre campus at Kotturpuram which has 8 galleries showcasing over 500 exhibits. The planetarium conducts sky shows including [Solar System], [eclipses], [Earth], [Man on Moon], [comets], [shooting meteoroids], [stellar cycle] and the [deep sky] every day at different times in both English and Tamil. The planetarium's 360-degree sky theatre is the first of its kind in India. The planetarium organises a special show on every second Saturday of the month to view the night sky from 7:00 pm to 9:00 pm.

### Historical Monuments

\[ [edit] \]

- **[Vivekanandar Illam] ** or Vivekananda house is remembered as the place where [Swami Vivekananda] stayed for nine days when he visited Chennai (then Madras) in 1897. Vivekananda House now houses a permanent exhibition on Indian Culture. Located on the busy Kamrajar Salai along the Marina Beach, it has become an important spiritual tourist attraction in the city.Valluvar Kottam
- **[Valluvar Kottam] ** is a popular [monument] in [Chennai], dedicated to the classical [Tamil] poet, philosopher, and saint, [Thiruvalluvar] who wrote his famous [Thirukkural] some 2,000 years ago. All 133 chapters and 1330 verses of the [Thirukkural] are inscribed on bas-relief in the front hall's corridors. A life-size statue of [Thiruvalluvar] has been installed in the 39 m high chariot.St.Mary's Church inside Fort St.George

### Historic Government Buildings

\[ [edit] \]

- [**Fort St. George**] is the name of the first [British] fortress in India, founded in 1639[\[15\]] at the coastal city of Madras. The fort is a stronghold with 6-meter-high (20 ft) walls that withstood a number of assaults in the 18th century. It is a feasible contention to say that the city evolved around the fortress. The fort currently houses the [Tamil Nadu legislative assembly] and other official buildings. The Fort Museum contains many relics of the [Raj], including portraits of many of the Governors. Other monuments present inside the fort are [St. Mary's Church], the oldest [Anglican] church in India, and Wellesley House, which holds the paintings of the Governor of the Fort and other high officials of the Regime.
- **[Ripon Building] **, commissioned in 1913 and named after Lord Ripon, Governor General of India and father of local self-government. It is the headquarters of the city's municipal body [Greater Chennai Corporation], the world's 2nd oldest municipal corporation after the [City of London Corporation]. The building is a fine example of the [Neoclassical] style of architecture, a combination of, [Ionic] and [Corinthian]. The Ripon Building is an all-white structure and is located near the iconic [Chennai Central] railway station.
- **[Victoria Public Hall],** or the Town Hall, is a historical building located in between the [Ripon Building] and the [Chennai Central Railway Station] and is seen as one of the finest examples of [British architecture] in Chennai. Built in 1888 as a town hall for the city of Madras and named after Queen Victoria to commemorate the golden jubilee.

### Wildlife

\[ [edit] \]

Black Buck at Guindy National Park

- **[Arignar Anna Zoological Park] ** (better known as **[Vandalur Zoo] **) is located south-west of the city. Covering an area of 1490 acres, it is India's largest zoo. It has about 2200 animals belonging to 170 different species including of mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians and fish. The zoo also has a lion safari, an elephant safari, a nocturnal animal house, walk-through aviary, butterfly house, reptile house and a crocodile enclosure. The park has tree-lined paved paths for long treks inside the campus, enabling the visitors to walk 15 to 20 kilometres (9.3 to 12.4 mi) during a visit. [Battery-operated vehicles] with a range of up to 80 kilometres (50 miles) are available for rent.
- **[Madras Crocodile Bank Trust] **, located south of the city, along the [East Coast Road], is an important centre for [herpetological] research. It houses several fresh-water and salt-water [crocodiles], [alligators], [gharials], [turtles] and [snakes]. Founded by [Romulus Whitaker] in 1976, the CrocBank now has a total of 2,483 animals, including 14 species of crocodiles, 10 species of turtles, 3 species of snakes, and 1 species of lizard. The bank is home to 14 species of the 23 crocodilian species living across the world, two of which are listed by the IUCN as critically endangered and three more as threatened.
- **[Guindy National Park] **, carved as a garden space from the Guindy Forest in 1670 and later established as a park in 1976, it is one of the country's smallest [National Park] with an area of 2.76 km2 and is located completely inside the city. It hosts a variety of endangered deer, foxes, monkeys and snakes. The Guindy **Snake Park** situated in the National Park has a large collection of snakes and is an important source of [antivenom] serum. For ex-situ conservation, about 22 acres (8.9 hectares) of the Guindy National Park has been carved out into a park which is known as the **Children's Park** and play area at the northeast corner of the national park with a collection of animals and birds.
- **Pallikaranai Marshland Park**

### Nature

\[ [edit] \]

- [**Adyar Eco Park**], locally known as Tholkappia Poonga is an ecological park set up in the [Adyar] estuary area to restore the vegetation of the freshwater eco-systems of the [Coromandel Coast], especially the fragile ecosystem of the Adyar estuary and creek. A total of 143 species of fish, amphibians, birds and reptiles have been seen in the park and the number is expected to go up to 200. More than 85 different kinds of birds, including rare [black bittern], [cinnamon bittern], [black-winged kite], [white-bellied sea eagle], [pied kingfisher], [yellow wagtails] and [black-winged stilt] have been spotted by ornithologists in the green expanse.
- **[Chetpet Lake] **, also known as Chetpet Eco Park is a lake spread over 15 acres and is the only lake in the centre of the city. The lake became a tourist attraction from 2014 after it underwent a remarkable transformation from being a heavily encroached filthy lake to having clean water with facilities for boating, angling, water sports and beautifully surrounded by a park.
- **[The Huddleston Gardens of Theosophical Society] ** is the garden that lies on the south bank of the [Adyar River] where the river meets the [Bay of Bengal] and covers 260 acres. The garden is located inside the Adyar Theosophical society which is the headquarters of the [Theosophical Society] which was an organization formed in 1875 to advance [Theosophy]. The garden also has a 450-year-old [banyan tree], which was known locally as _Adyar aala maram,_ whose aerial roots covered some 60,000 sq m. and is situated in the middle of the Theosophical Society Campus. One of the largest banyan trees in the world, it continues to miraculously survive and even thrive despite severe storm damage to its main trunk in 1989.

### Art and Crafts

\[ [edit] \]

- **[DakshinaChitra] ** is a [living history museum] run by the Chennai Craft Foundation, is a depiction of the way of life prevalent in 5 south Indian states collectively called as South India with exhibitions and workshops of the arts and crafts and performing artists of South India. There are around 4,220 artefacts on display; 3,200 are art-related, and 70 pertain to contemporary subjects.
- **[Kalakshetra] **, a centre for the revival of Indian art and crafts — especially the dance form of [Bharatnatyam] is located in [Besant Nagar]. Founded in January 1936 by [Rukmini Devi Arundale], the institution achieved national and international recognition for its unique style and perfectionism.[\[16\]] 
- **[Cholamandalam Artists' Village] **, on the East Coast Road, offers a view of artists and sculptors at work in their own studios and permanent gallery.

### Places of Worship

\[ [edit] \]

Kapaleeshwarar temple in Mylapore

- **[Kapaleeshwarar temple] ** in [Mylapore] estimated to have been built in the 7th century by the [Pallavas] and has inscriptions dating back to 12th century is one of the most visited worship places by tourists visiting the city.
- [**Parthasarathy Temple**] in [Triplicane], an 8th-century [Hindu] [Vaishnavite] temple dedicated to the god [Krishna]. The temple is one of the oldest structures in [Chennai].
- **[St. Thomas Mount] **, the site where [St. Thomas], one of the disciples of Jesus Christ, was believed to have been martyred.[\[17\]] A shrine dedicated to "Our Lady of Expectation" ( [Mother Mary]) was built in 1523 on top of the mount.
- **[Santhome Basilica] ** is a [Roman Catholic] minor [basilica] in [Santhome]. It was built in the 16th century by [Portuguese] explorers, over the tomb of [Saint Thomas]. This church is one of the only three known churches in the world built over the tomb of an Apostle of Jesus, the other two are located in [Vatican City] and [Spain].
- [**Armenian Church of Virgin Mary**], is located on the Armenian Street in Parrys. Constructed in 1712, it is one of the oldest churches of the [Indian subcontinent] and is famous for its [belfry] of six.
- [**St. Mary's Church**] located at Fort St George, is the oldest Anglican church East of Suez and also the oldest British building in India. The church is popularly known as the 'Westminster Abbey of the East'.
- **[Thousand Lights Mosque],** a multi-domed mosque opened in 1810 and spread over an area of 3 acres, it is one of the largest mosques in India and is situated on Anna Salai Road. It is said that 1000 lights needed to be lit in order to illuminate the assembly hall and hence it was named Thousand Lights Mosque. It is also believed that the lights were lit by the Indian National Congress to mark their first visit to Madras.
- **[Triplicane Big Mosque],** constructed in the [Mughal architectural style], the mosque was built in 1795 by the family of Muhammad Ali Khan Wallajah, the [Nawab of Arcot]. It is considered as the largest mosque in the city of Chennai.

### Shopping

\[ [edit] \]

[Spencer Plaza] is one of the oldest and largest shopping malls in Chennai.

Chennai has some unique places to offer for shopping. Art and crafts, contemporary and traditional artwork, antiques, jewellery are available in the city. Traditional items like the leaf and palmyra-fiber handicrafts from [Tirunelveli], bronze and brass castings and traditional jewellery from [Kumbakonam], metal works from [Thanjavur], stone carvings from [Mahabalipuram], silks from [Kanchipuram] are for sale in shops and boutiques.

- **[T. Nagar] **, the neighbourhood is the shopping hub of the city. Two main areas are [Pondy Bazaar] and [Ranganathan Street] which are home to several multi-storey stores, unique to Chennai, which deals mainly in textiles and silks or gold, silver and diamond jewellery.
- [**George Town**] and **[Parrys Corner] ** are wholesale markets of the city where one can purchase almost anything.
- **Mint Street** plays host to communities from Rajasthan and Gujarat and is where north Indian snacks can be sampled along with textiles, kitchenware, and jewellery.
- **[Burma Bazaar] ** is famous for its counterfeit electronic goods and media.
- **[Moore Market] ** in Central is known for its large number of bookstores.
- The city also has a number of shopping malls spread across the landscape including the oldest [Spencer Plaza] and several other modern malls that include [Express Avenue], [Phoenix Market City], [Forum Vijaya Mall], [Ampa Skywalk], [Abirami Mega Mall], [Mayajaal], [Spectrum Mall].

### Entertainment

\[ [edit] \]

There are four large [amusement parks], [MGM Dizzee World], [VGP Universal Kingdom], [Queen's Land] near Poonnamalle and [Kishkinta] Located near [Mudichur] in Chennai. The city also houses a paintball centre and water sports club on the east coast road. There are also a large number of beach resorts all along the East Coast Road highway to Mahabalipuram. The city is home to the Tamil movie industry, has over 100+ large cinema theatres including a few multiplexes which screen [Tamil], English, [Hindi], [Telugu], [Kannada] and [Malayalam] films.[\[18\]] The city has a large number of restaurants offering a variety of Tamil, Indian and international cuisines.[\[19\]] The nightlife in Chennai is vibrant and growing ranging from bars to pool parlours to lounges and clubs.[\[20\]]

## Embracing tradition, embracing tomorrow

Chennai, formerly known as Madras, is a city of contrasts, where ancient temples stand tall amidst skyscrapers and traditional dance forms share the stage with contemporary art. A beautiful blend of South Indian traditions and modernity, Chennai, the capital of Tamil Nadu is one of the important metro cities of India. Be its cuisine, culture or development, Chennai is a cultural hot spot and a leader in modern India.

##### Fort St. George

#### Unveiling the city of temples

Visiting Chennai as a tourist is an exciting and enriching experience. The city has a unique charm with its blend of rich history, vibrant culture, and modern amenities. As a tourist, you'll find yourself captivated by a few hundred magnificent temples, each with its own architectural marvels and spiritual significance. No wonder it is known worldwide as the city of temples. The warmth and friendliness of the locals add to the welcoming atmosphere, making you feel right at home. Whether you're exploring historical sites, enjoying the lively cultural scene, or simply relaxing by the sea on some of the best beaches in the country, visiting Chennai as a tourist promises to be an unforgettable journey filled with delightful experiences.
History has it that the name Chennai was derived from the name of Chennappa Nayaka, a Nayak ruler who served as a general under Venkata Raya of the Vijayanagara Empire from whom the British East India Company acquired the town in 1639. Chennai was previously known as Madras, a name which probably was derived from Madrasapattinam, a fishing village north of Fort St. George. Today Chennai stands as a city that seamlessly blends tradition with contemporary elements, offering an enchanting experience to all its visitors. Nestled along the south-eastern coast of India, the city is celebrated for its deep-rooted cultural heritage, lively festivals, tantalising cuisine and picturesque shorelines.

#### Understanding the history

Historically, Chennai region was part of the Chola, Pandya, Pallava and Vijayanagara kingdoms during various eras. The coastal land which then contained the fishing village Madrasapattinam, was purchased by the British East India Company in the 17th century. British garrison established Madras city and port, and built Fort St. George, the first British fortress in India. The city was made the winter capital of the Madras Presidency, a colonial province of the British Raj in the Indian subcontinent. After India gained its independence in 1947, Madras continued as the capital city of the Madras State and present-day Tamil Nadu. The city was officially renamed as Chennai in 1996.

#### Prominent landmarks

Chennai, the vibrant capital of Tamil Nadu, boasts of several iconic landmarks that epitomize its rich heritage and cultural significance. Among these, Marina Beach stands out as one of the longest urban beaches globally, offering stunning views of the Bay of Bengal and serving as a favourite spot for locals and tourists alike. [Fort St. George], dating back to 1644, holds the distinction of being the first English fortress in India. Today, it houses the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and the [Fort Museum], showcasing artefacts from the colonial era, providing a glimpse into Chennai's historical past. The [Kapaleeshwarar Temple], dedicated to Lord Shiva, showcases magnificent Dravidian-style architecture, adorned with intricate sculptures and towering gopurams. Another religious gem, the [Parthasarathy Temple], is a 6th-century [Hindu] [Vaishnavite] temple dedicated to [Lord Vishnu]. These temples remain significant religious and cultural landmarks in the city, attracting devotees and visitors throughout the year. Meanwhile, the [Santhome Cathedral Basilica], built over the tomb of Saint Thomas the Apostle, is a revered pilgrimage site for Christians, known for its neo-Gothic architecture and serene ambience. Other notable landmarks include the [Government Museum], Vivekananda House (Ice House), [Valluvar Kottam], [Arignar Anna Zoological Park] and [MGR Memorial], each contributing to Chennai's rich tapestry of history, culture, and architectural splendour. Together, these landmarks showcase Chennai's diverse and vibrant character, inviting visitors to explore its captivating heritage and timeless charm.

#### Captivating beaches

The beaches of Chennai offer a unique and diverse experience that's worth exploring. Marina Beach, one of the longest urban beaches in the world, is a prominent attraction known for its vast expanse of golden sand and stunning views of the Bay of Bengal. Besant Nagar Beach, also known as Elliot's Beach, is a favourite hangout spot for locals and tourists alike. It's less crowded compared to Marina Beach and offers a more relaxed atmosphere. Covelong Beach, located a short drive from Chennai, is known for its picturesque setting and water sports activities like surfing and windsurfing. The serene surroundings and fishing village nearby add to its charm, making it an ideal spot for a day trip away from the city. For nature lovers, the hidden gem of Muttukadu Beach offers tranquil backwaters and scenic boat rides. It's a peaceful escape from the hustle and bustle of the city, perfect for those seeking a quieter beach experience. Overall, the beaches of Chennai cater to different preferences, whether you're looking for a lively ambience, water sports or a peaceful retreat amidst nature. Each beach has its own character and offers a glimpse into the coastal beauty of this vibrant city.

#### Cultural tapestry

Chennai emerges as a cultural hub where ancient traditions seamlessly intertwine with contemporary influences. The city comes alive during festivals such as Pongal, a fervently celebrated harvest festival, and the Margazhi Festival, a month-long extravaganza of classical music and dance. Visitors can immerse themselves in the captivating performances of Bharatanatyam, a classical dance form native to Tamil Nadu, or revel in the soul-stirring melodies of Carnatic music, which holds a special place in the hearts of Chennaites.

#### Culinary delights

A visit to Chennai would be incomplete without savouring its culinary offerings. The city presents a culinary paradise, offering a delectable array of experiences, ranging from street food delights to upscale dining options. Delight in the bold flavours of authentic Chettinad cuisine, characterized by its aromatic spices and flavourful curries or indulge in the iconic South Indian breakfast of crispy dosas and fluffy idlis paired with piping hot sambar and chutney. For seafood enthusiasts, a trip to the bustling fish markets of Kasimedu promises a feast for the senses, with freshly caught seafood transformed into mouth-watering dishes.

#### Conclusion

In conclusion, Chennai emerges as a city of contrasts, where the past seamlessly converges with the present, creating a tapestry of tradition and modernity. Whether exploring its historical landmarks, immersing oneself in its vibrant cultural scene or indulging in its diverse culinary offerings, Chennai promises an immersive experience that leaves an indelible mark on every visitor. With its rich heritage, lively festivals and culinary treasures, Chennai stands as a captivating destination that beckons travellers to unravel the secrets of India's cultural mosaic.
There are innumerable tourist attractions close to Chennai. The list includes Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram), Pondicherry (Puducherry), Kanchipuram, Vedanthangal Bird Sanctuary, Kovalam and Tirupati. These attractions offer a diverse range of experiences, from exploring ancient heritage sites to relaxing on pristine beaches and immersing yourself in spiritual retreats. They make excellent day trips or weekend getaways from the bustling city of Chennai.

#### Reaching there

Chennai is well connected by all means of transport. Chennai International Airport (Meenambakkam) is not very far from the city and is one of the busiest in the country. There are daily flights here from various cities in the country as well as international flights from major countries. If one intends to travel by train, Chennai Central (Puratchi Thalaivar Dr. M. G. Ramachandran Central Railway Station) is the busiest station providing access to other major cities and smaller towns across India. Further, there are various national highways connecting Chennai with Trichy, Bengaluru, Coimbatore and Tiruvallur.

Chennai offers a rich mix of history, culture, and modern attractions beyond its famous temples. Here are some must-visit places in Chennai:

- **Fort St. George**: The first English fortress in India, now housing a museum showcasing artifacts from the British colonial period, including old uniforms, coins, and letters.
- **Marina Beach**: One of the longest urban beaches in the world, perfect for early morning or evening strolls, with numerous food stalls, a lighthouse, and statues along the promenade.
- **Elliot’s Beach (Besant Nagar Beach)**: A quieter alternative to Marina Beach, known for its serene environment and popular eateries nearby.
- **Guindy National Park**: A small national park within the city, home to a variety of flora and fauna, including blackbucks, spotted deer, and numerous bird species.
- **Semmozhi Poonga**: A beautifully landscaped botanical garden featuring exotic plant species, fountains, and a serene ambiance perfect for relaxation.
- **Madras Crocodile Bank Trust**: Located just outside Chennai, this reptile zoo is home to various species of crocodiles, alligators, and other reptiles, and focuses on conservation and research.
- **Express Avenue Mall**: One of the largest shopping malls in Chennai, featuring international and local brands, a multiplex, and numerous dining options.
- **Phoenix Marketcity**: Another major shopping destination, offering a wide range of shops, restaurants, and entertainment options including a multiplex cinema.

These attractions provide a comprehensive experience of Chennai’s diverse offerings, from its historical significance and cultural heritage to modern-day entertainment and natural beauty.

Chennai, a city rich in culture and history, offers many attractions beyond its famous temples. Here are some of the best places to visit:

1. **Marina Beach**: One of the longest urban beaches in the world, Marina Beach is a must-visit for its stunning sunrise views and vibrant local atmosphere. You can enjoy a leisurely walk, sample local snacks, or explore nearby landmarks like the Lighthouse.
2. **Fort St. George**: Built in 1644, Fort St. George is a historic fort that now houses a museum showcasing artifacts from the British colonial period. It's a great spot to learn about Chennai's colonial past.
3. **Government Museum**: Located in Egmore, the Government Museum is one of the oldest museums in India. It has an impressive collection of artifacts, including ancient sculptures, coins, and rare paintings.
4. **Kapaleeshwarar Temple**: While primarily a temple, this site in Mylapore is worth visiting for its stunning Dravidian architecture and vibrant local markets.
5. **Valluvar Kottam**: A monument dedicated to the classical Tamil poet Thiruvalluvar, Valluvar Kottam is an architectural marvel with a giant stone chariot and a hall that can seat 4,000 people.
6. **DakshinaChitra**: This heritage museum is located a little outside Chennai and showcases the art, architecture, lifestyle, and culture of South Indian states. It's an immersive experience that offers insight into the region's traditions.

For a comfortable stay, you might consider planning a visit to [Daman hotels], a peaceful coastal town. Daman offers several excellent hotels where you can relax after your Chennai adventure. Staying in Daman allows you to enjoy a quieter atmosphere with access to beautiful beaches and rich Portuguese heritage. It’s a perfect getaway after exploring the bustling city of Chennai.

Chennai, the capital city of Tamil Nadu, is a vibrant metropolis known for its rich cultural heritage, bustling markets, and stunning coastline. Here's a quick guide to some of the best places to visit in Chennai.

#### Top Attractions​

01. **Marina Beach**
 - One of the longest urban beaches in the world
 - Perfect for morning walks and sunsets
02. **Fort St. George**
 - Historical fort built by the British in 1644
 - Houses a museum with colonial artifacts
03. **Kapaleeshwarar Temple**
 - A beautiful Dravidian-style temple dedicated to Lord Shiva
 - Known for its intricate architecture and vibrant festivals
04. **San Thome Basilica**
 - A majestic church built over the tomb of Apostle St. Thomas
 - Features neo-Gothic architecture
05. **Government Museum**
 - One of the oldest museums in India
 - Rich collection of archaeological and numismatic artifacts
06. **Elliot's Beach**
 - Serene beach, ideal for relaxing
 - Popular among locals for its calm ambiance
07. **Chennai Central Railway Station**
 - Iconic railway station with Gothic architecture
 - Major transportation hub in the city
08. **Valluvar Kottam**
 - Monument dedicated to the classical Tamil poet, Thiruvalluvar
 - Features a large auditorium and intricately carved temple chariot
09. **Guindy National Park**
 - Urban national park and sanctuary
 - Home to various species of flora and fauna
10. **Mahabalipuram**
 - Ancient port city with UNESCO World Heritage sites
 - Famous for rock-cut temples and sculptures

Chennai offers a mix of historical landmarks, cultural sites, and natural beauty, making it a must-visit destination in India. For those planning a trip from [**Orlando to Chennai,**] Indian Eagle offers convenient flight options to ensure a smooth and enjoyable journey.

Great article! You’ve done an amazing job capturing the essence of Chennai and highlighting some of its most popular spots. I thought I’d add a few more places that travelers might find interesting.
For anyone who loves history and culture, the [DakshinaChitra Museum] is a must-visit. It’s this beautiful heritage center that brings South Indian art and traditions to life. And speaking of culture, the Parthasarathy Temple in Triplicane is one of the oldest temples in Chennai and is absolutely stunning with its ancient architecture and serene vibe.
If you’re into arts and performance, the [Kalakshetra Foundation] is another gem. It’s famous for Bharatanatyam and Carnatic music, and they often have events or workshops worth checking out.
For some outdoor fun, Besant Nagar Beach (Elliot’s Beach) is more than just a relaxing spot. It’s surrounded by great cafes and street food that give it a lively, fun vibe. And if you’re traveling with kids (or just love wildlife), the Chennai Snake Park near Guindy is super fascinating with its variety of reptiles.
Shopping in Chennai wouldn’t be complete without a trip to T. Nagar! It’s a bustling area full of colorful markets where you can find everything from silk sarees to souvenirs.

It is easy to find a list of places of tourist attraction at Chennai by a simple search in google. Hence, without repeating those results, I am putting forward my selection of few places in Chennai that are not to be missed: San Thome Church The church is located at Mylapore area of Chennai. This is one of the four churches in the world thats build over the tomb of an apostle. (Other ones being, St. Peter's Basilica in Rome, Italy; Church of Saint James in Spain; and St. Thaddeus Monastery in Ghara-Kilise.) The church has excellent stained-glass arches that are not to be missed. And if you are lucky, you may even witness a Christian wedding that often takes place in the church. Dakshina Chitra This is an open air heritage village where the lifestyle of South Indians are displayed. The place has display of several articles (sort of museum) as well performing artisans, who show their creative skills to the visitors. The museum has 18 heritage houses representing the living styles of people from the states of South India such as Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, North Karnataka, Kerala and Telangana. You may also get to see artisans involved in creating artwork for display (and some for sale too). In some cases, you may even try your hand in creating and replicating their efforts. A must visit for people interested in heritage and culture. Madras War Cemetery Madras War Cemetery is located on Mount-Poonamallee Road at Nandambakkam. The memorial does not contain any buried dead-bodies, but has plaques with the names of many soldiers who died in both the World Wars. Broken Bridge &amp; Besant Nagar Beach Chennai would be incomplete without visiting a beach. Most people generally refer to Marina Beach. However, I find Marina Beach over crowded, with lots of shops and not that clean either. Instead, Besant Nagar beach can be a good option. A little walk (rather pleasant stroll) from the beach is the Broken Bridge. As the name suggests, this is a bridge to nowhere. In seventies, the bridge partly collapsed due to strong currents of the river, and has never been repaired. However, it has now become a popular spot to view the sunrise and sunsets. This has also been shooting spots for many movies. The is one of the few places from where you can enjoy a panoramic beauty of Chennai.
