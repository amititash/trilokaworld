## Pilgrimage for the palate, paradise for the past

Guntur, a city located in the fertile plains of Andhra Pradesh, offers visitors a unique blend of historical intrigue and natural splendour. This city, which threads through the fabric of India’s agrarian and cultural narratives, serves as a compelling destination for those eager to explore the less trodden paths of India’s vast and varied landscape. It's a city where the aroma of freshly ground spices permeates the air, while the echoes of history can be heard from its ancient monuments. It's a place where every corner holds a story and every street reverberates with the footsteps of both past and present.

##### Undavalli Caves, Guntur

#### Echoes of yore: A walk, through time

Ancient Sanskrit texts etch Guntur’s name as "Garthapuri," a place cradled by water ponds, hinting at its fertile beginnings. Delve deeper, and you'll find the city's soul intertwined with the rise and fall of empires – the powerful Satavahanas, the artistic Kakatiyas, the valiant Reddys. Each dynasty left its mark, enriching Guntur's cultural heritage with architectural marvels and enduring traditions.
Legend whispers that even Lord Buddha himself graced these lands, performing the first Kalachakra ceremony, a sacred Buddhist ritual. The Agastyeshwara temple, an ancient sentinel standing tall amidst the bustling city, stands as a testament to this rich spiritual heritage. Recent excavations unearthed inscriptions hinting at a forgotten Buddhist site, its secrets waiting to be unravelled by curious minds.

#### Helium: Elevating Guntur's spirit of innovation to the skies

Guntur is not just a storied city embedded in history; it is a vibrant, dynamic place that continues to echo with the beats of modernity and innovation. One of the most significant moments in its scientific history occurred in 1868, when French astronomer Pierre Janssen, observing a solar eclipse from this locale, first identified helium. This discovery was not just a pivotal moment for science but also a beacon highlighting Guntur's role on the global stage of astronomical research. It underscored the city's spirit of discovery and its capacity to contribute to momentous scientific advancements.

#### Unveiling timeless treasures of tranquillity

The Baudhasree Archaeological Museum in Guntur is a treasure trove of artifacts celebrating Buddhist heritage. Established in May 1956 by collector S. Bhagwan Das as Buddha Vihar, the museum originally served as a centre for Buddhist learning before transitioning into a museum in 1975. It hosts a diverse collection, including Buddhist sculptures, a limestone Buddha statue, and rock-cut sculptures from Sekuru, Divuladinne, and Amudalapalle. Also on display are bronze and copper plates, palm leaf manuscripts, ancient coins, paintings, and prehistoric artifacts.

#### Beyond the city walls: A journey into nature's embrace

Guntur isn't just about urban exploration. Step outside the city limits, and you'll find yourself embraced by the unspoiled beauty of nature. The Uppalapadu Bird Sanctuary is a haven for birdwatchers, with over 200 species of feathered friends calling this their home. Witness the vibrant flash of kingfishers, the graceful glide of egrets, and the melodious calls of migratory birds as you explore the lush greenery of the sanctuary.
For a taste of the sea, head towards the pristine shores of Surya Lanka beach, approximately 60 kilometres from Guntur. Here, the turquoise waters lap against the golden sands, creating a picture-perfect paradise. Bask in the warm sun, feel the soft sand between your toes, and let the rhythmic crash of waves lull you into a state of tranquillity.

#### Adventures for the thrill-seeker: Unveiling hidden gems

History buffs and adventure seekers can embark on a journey to the [Undavalli Caves]. Carved into the sandstone cliffs just 10 kilometres southwest of Guntur, these caves are a marvel of rock-cut architecture. Dating back to the 4th and 5th centuries CE, Undavalli Caves were dedicated to the Trimurti (Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva). Explore the intricate carvings that adorn the walls, depicting scenes from mythology and everyday life. Descend deeper and discover the magnificent reclining statue of Vishnu, a masterpiece of ancient Indian sculpture.
A short drive away lies the Amareswaraswamy temple, a revered site dedicated to Lord Shiva. This ancient temple complex boasts intricate carvings and towering gopurams (gateway towers), showcasing the Dravidian architectural style in all its glory. As you walk through the temple courtyards, feel the peace wash over you, a stark contrast to the bustling energy of Guntur city.

#### A fortress of the past: Kondaveedu Fort

Fortified atop a granite hill, approximately 30 kilometres from Guntur, lies the majestic Kondaveedu Fort. This strategic stronghold, dating back to the 14th century, served as the capital of the Reddy dynasty. Explore the imposing ramparts, remnants of palaces, and ancient granaries, each whispering tales of battles fought and empires won. As you stand atop the fort, take in the breathtaking panoramic view of the surrounding plains and admire the strategic brilliance of its builders.

#### Where every bite is a fiery delight

Foodies, rejoice! Guntur is a party for your taste buds. Dive into the fiery explosion of the Red Chili Biryani, a local legend known for its potent spice and melt-in-your-mouth rice. Don't miss the Guntur Sannam, a chili variety so unique it boasts its own Geographical Indication tag. This fiery red wonder adds a distinct kick to any dish, a witness to the city's love for all things spicy.
Wander through Guntur’s lively markets where the air is thick with the aroma of freshly ground spices, offering an invitation to a world of taste. Street vendors and restaurants alike serve up a feast of Andhra specialties—from the crispiness of dosas and vadas to the softness of idlis and puris. Not to be missed are local street snacks like the crispy murukku (deep-fried lentil snacks) to the tangy avakai (pickled mangoes) and the creamy bobbattu (stuffed flatbreads), each bite offers a unique explosion of taste and texture.

#### Festivals and celebrations: A window into local culture

Guntur comes alive during festivals, offering a window into local culture. Witness the fervour of Vinayaka Chavithi, where elaborately decorated idols of Lord Ganesha are worshipped. During Vijaya Dasami, the city reverberates with the sounds of drumbeats and chants as people celebrate the triumph of good over evil. Ugadi, the Telugu New Year, brings a festive spirit with families gathering for elaborate feasts and exchanging gifts.

#### Beyond the tourist trail: Experiencing the heart of Guntur

Guntur offers a unique opportunity to experience the heart of Andhra culture, not just curated tourist experiences. Take a stroll through the bustling bazaars, where vendors hawk their wares with infectious enthusiasm. Engage in friendly conversation with locals, learning about their traditions and way of life. In the evenings, head to one of the many parks, such as Gandhi Park or Nagaravanam, and soak in the laid-back atmosphere as families picnic and children play.

#### A city of contrasts: A journey for the soul

Guntur isn't just a destination; it's an experience. It's a city that challenges your preconceptions, that invites you to delve deeper, to explore beyond the surface. It's a place where history comes alive, where flavours ignite your taste buds, and where nature's beauty unfolds in a breathtaking symphony.
So, come, embark on a journey to Guntur. Let yourself be captivated by its history, spices, and untamed beauty. Discover a city that will make a place in your heart, a city you'll long to return to again and again.

### Kondaveedu

It is one of the most famous places to visit in Guntur, which is home to an ancient fort and a recently discovered Buddhist stupa. The fort dates back to the 14th century when the Reddy Kings built it. It consists of 21 grandiose structures.

### Uppaladau Conservatory

One of the top places to visit in Guntur city if you are into bird watching is Uppaladau Bird Conservatory. Thousands of native and migratory birds, including Spot-billed Pelicans and Painted Storks, call this place home, with beautiful lush greenery and sprawling lakes.

### Mangalagiri

This truly stands out among places to visit in Guntur Andhra Pradesh, owing to its rich mythological importance. It’s the home of Lord Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy; legends have it that this place has been worshipped since the first of four yugas, Satya Yug.

### Krishna Barrage

Also known as Prakasam Barrage, this place gives a breathtaking view of the Krishna River. Some 18 miles away from the city, it connects Andhra Pradesh’s Krishna district with the Guntur district. It is one of the top places to visit in Guntur.

### Amaravati

The place is a historically significant site for Buddhists and features a famous Buddhist holy shrine. [Amaravati] is named after Amerswara Swamy, a famous temple in the region where Swayambhu Linga is present. It is among the most tranquil places to visit in Guntur.

## Best Time to Visit Guntur

### Summer

Summer falls between April and June in Guntur. The weather stays scorching and humid during this time.

### Monsoon

From July to September, Guntur sees moderate amounts of rainfall. It’s ideal if you want to avoid crowds at popular Guntur tourist attractions.

### Winter

Guntur sees a pleasant climate, with temperatures between 16 and 25°C. It’s the best time for Guntur tourism.

## Places to Visit in Guntur with Family & Friends

### Kotappakonda Trikutaparvattam

The name literally translates to “three-peaked hill”, which lies nearby. Kotappakonda Trikutaparvattam offers a beautiful 360-degree-view of the hills nearby, making it one of the most scenic places to visit in Guntur.

### Suryalanka Beach

This beach is one of the best nearby places to visit in Guntur. Enjoy the azure waters lap up on your feet or take a dip in the sea with family or friends or simply enjoy the sight of the majestic Bay of Bengal.

### Bhattiprolu

It is a quaint village close to Guntur city. One of the offbeat places to visit in Guntur district, Bhattiprolu is known for its centrally protected Buddhist stupa, which is regarded as of national importance.

## Best Places in Guntur for Couples

### Downtown Guntur

Guntur is divided into three primary regions, residential area, midtown and downtown. Downtown Guntur is proximal to many places to see Guntur, such as the Center of Brodipet and Brindavan Gardens, besides eateries for couples to hang out.

### Brewbakes Guntur

Brewbakes Guntur is a great place for a date or just to relax after a day of sightseeing. They serve top-notch beverages and coffee and other snacks such as pizza. It is one of the top places to visit in Guntur if you would like to satiate your palate.

### Teas N Trees

This café features outdoor sitting in a garden area and is ideal for a romantic date and conversation. The place serves a range of beverages and other finger-food items and is known for its great service.

## About Guntur

**Guntur** is a city in the district of Guntur, Andhra Pradesh, India. Gunturis well known for export of chilies, tobacco and cotton, education, agriculture, business and even e-commerce industries. The main language spoken here is Telugu. Guntur has a tropical climate which is characterized by hot summers, monsoons and pleasant winters. The city is well connected by roadways.

The nearest domestic airport is at Vijayawada and Hyderabad has the nearest international airport. There are many tourist attractions which include forts, parks, nature conservation sites and museums. The main festivals celebrated here are Karthika Purnima, Diwali, Vinayaka – Chaviti, Krishna Ashtami, Ugadi, Ram Navami and Sakranti. The food here consists of typical South Indian food like idli and dosa. This place deserves a visit from all nature and history enthusiasts.
